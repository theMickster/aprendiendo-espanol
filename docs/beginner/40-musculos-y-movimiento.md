# Músculos y Movimiento

**Level:** Beginner (A2)
**Estimated Time:** 25 minutes
**Topics:** Runner's body vocabulary, sports injuries, condition adjectives, introduction to simple past tense for narrating injuries

## Overview

If you run, hike, swim, or play any sport, you need vocabulary for the parts that do the heavy lifting — and the words for when things go wrong. This lesson gives you the runner's body: calves, hamstrings, thighs, tendons. You will learn adjectives for how your muscles feel (tight, stiff, sore, swollen) and — for the first time in the beginner series — the **simple past tense** (pretérito) to tell someone what happened. "Me tiré el isquiotibial" is more useful than you think.

> **Prerequisites:** [Lesson 14 - El Cuerpo y la Salud](14-el-cuerpo-y-la-salud.md), [Lesson 38 - Más Partes del Cuerpo](38-mas-partes-del-cuerpo.md)

## Learning Objectives

- Name muscles, joints, and body parts used in running and sports
- Describe how your body feels using condition adjectives with **estar**
- Use basic simple past tense (pretérito) to narrate what happened with an injury
- Understand sports and exercise vocabulary (warm up, stretch, cramp, etc.)
- Have a basic conversation about a sports injury

## Lesson Content

### The Runner's Body

These are the body parts that runners, hikers, and athletes talk about most. Lesson 14 gave you piernas, rodillas, and pies. Here is the rest:

| Spanish                | English                    | Notes                                                |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **la pantorrilla**     | calf (muscle)              | las pantorrillas = calves                            |
| **el muslo**           | thigh                      | los muslos = thighs                                  |
| **los isquiotibiales** | hamstrings                 | the muscles on the back of the thigh                 |
| **el tendón**          | tendon                     | el tendón de Aquiles = Achilles tendon               |
| **el tobillo**         | ankle                      | review from [Lesson 38](38-mas-partes-del-cuerpo.md) |
| **la cadera**          | hip                        | las caderas = hips                                   |
| **la espinilla**       | shin                       | also called la tibia                                 |
| **la planta del pie**  | sole of the foot           |                                                      |
| **el talón**           | heel                       |                                                      |
| **el gemelo**          | calf muscle (anatomy term) | also means "twin" — context makes it clear           |

> **Pantorrilla vs. gemelo:** Both refer to the calf area. "Pantorrilla" is the general word for the calf (the back of the lower leg). "Gemelo" is the specific muscle (gastrocnemius). In everyday conversation, most people say "pantorrilla." In a doctor's office, you might hear "gemelo."

### How Does It Feel? (Condition Adjectives)

Use **estar** (not ser) for temporary physical conditions:

| Spanish          | English           | Example                                         |
| ---------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **tenso/a**      | tight, tense      | Las pantorrillas están tensas.                  |
| **rígido/a**     | stiff             | Tengo la espalda rígida.                        |
| **adolorido/a**  | sore              | Los muslos están adoloridos.                    |
| **hinchado/a**   | swollen           | El tobillo está hinchado.                       |
| **torcido/a**    | twisted, sprained | Tengo el tobillo torcido.                       |
| **cansado/a**    | tired             | Las piernas están cansadas.                     |
| **entumecido/a** | numb              | Tengo los dedos entumecidos.                    |
| **caliente**     | warm/hot          | Los músculos están calientes después de correr. |

**Pattern:** Estar + adjective (agrees in gender and number)

- El tobillo está **hinchado**. (masculine singular)
- La rodilla está **hinchada**. (feminine singular)
- Los músculos están **tensos**. (masculine plural)
- Las pantorrillas están **tensas**. (feminine plural)

### Your First Past Tense: What Happened?

Until now, the beginner lessons have used present tense. But when something goes wrong during a run, you need to tell someone **what happened** — and that requires past tense.

Here is a simple introduction to the **pretérito** (simple past) with injury verbs. We will keep it to the most essential forms.

#### Regular -ar verbs (like caminar, estirar)

|             | -ar ending  |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| yo          | -**é**      |
| tú          | -**aste**   |
| él/ella     | -**ó**      |
| nosotros    | -**amos**   |
| vosotros    | -**asteis** |
| ellos/ellas | -**aron**   |

**Examples:**

- Caminé hasta el final. (I walked to the end.)
- Estiré antes de correr. (I stretched before running.)
- Descansé dos días. (I rested for two days.)

#### Key Irregular Past Tenses (just the ones you need here)

| Verb                     | yo       | él/ella   | nosotros     |
| ------------------------ | -------- | --------- | ------------ |
| **caerse** (to fall)     | me caí   | se cayó   | nos caímos   |
| **torcerse** (to sprain) | me torcí | se torció | nos torcimos |
| **ser/ir** (was/went)    | fui      | fue       | fuimos       |
| **tener** (to have)      | tuve     | tuvo      | tuvimos      |
| **poder** (to be able)   | pude     | pudo      | pudimos      |

> **Don't panic:** You do not need to memorize every form right now. Focus on the "yo" and "él/ella" forms — those are the ones you will use most when telling someone what happened.

#### Injury Narration Patterns

| Spanish                   | English                  |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Me caí.                   | I fell.                  |
| Me torcí el tobillo.      | I sprained my ankle.     |
| Me tiré un músculo.       | I pulled a muscle.       |
| Me lesioné la rodilla.    | I injured my knee.       |
| Tuve un calambre.         | I had a cramp.           |
| No pude seguir corriendo. | I couldn't keep running. |
| Paré de correr.           | I stopped running.       |

### Scenario: Race Day in Valencia

Papá is running a 10K race along the Jardín del Turia. At kilometer 8, something goes wrong.

**Después de la carrera:**

**Mamá:** ¿Qué te pasó? ¿Estás bien?

**Papá:** Me tiré el isquiotibial en el kilómetro ocho. Me dolía mucho y no pude seguir corriendo.

> **Note:** "Me dolía" is a different past tense called the **imperfect** — it describes how things were feeling (ongoing), not what happened (completed). You will learn it formally later. For now, just know: "me dolía" = it was hurting (ongoing state), "me tiré" = I pulled (completed event).

**Mamá:** ¿Te caíste?

**Papá:** No, no me caí. Pero paré de correr y caminé hasta el final.

**Mamá:** ¿Cómo está la pierna ahora?

**Papá:** El muslo está muy tenso y la pantorrilla está un poco hinchada.

**Mamá:** Vamos a poner hielo. ¿Estiraste antes de la carrera?

**Papá:** Sí, estiré, pero no calenté lo suficiente. Fue un error.

**Mamá:** Necesitas descansar unos días.

**Key phrases from this scene:**

- ¿Qué te pasó? (What happened to you?)
- Me tiré el isquiotibial. (I pulled my hamstring.)
- No pude seguir corriendo. (I couldn't keep running.)
- Paré de correr. (I stopped running.)
- Caminé hasta el final. (I walked to the end.)

### Scenario: After a Long Run at Turia Park

**Papá:** ¡Uf! Diez kilómetros hoy. Tengo las pantorrillas muy adoloridas.

**Hijo:** ¿Por qué corres tanto, papá?

**Papá:** Porque me gusta. Pero hoy las piernas están muy cansadas. Y tengo un calambre en el gemelo derecho.

**Hijo:** ¿Qué es un calambre?

**Papá:** Es cuando el músculo se pone muy duro y duele mucho. Necesito estirar.

**Mamá:** Siéntate y estira las piernas. Yo te traigo agua.

**Papá:** También tengo la cadera rígida. Mañana voy a descansar.

### Quick Reference: Telling Someone What Hurts and What Happened

**Present (right now):**

- Me duele la pantorrilla. (My calf hurts.)
- Tengo el tobillo hinchado. (My ankle is swollen.)
- Las piernas están muy cansadas. (My legs are very tired.)

**Past (what happened):**

- Me tiré un músculo. (I pulled a muscle.)
- Me torcí el tobillo. (I sprained my ankle.)
- Tuve un calambre. (I had a cramp.)

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                          | Right                              | Why                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _Las pantorrillas son tensas._ | **Las pantorrillas están tensas.** | Temporary condition = estar, not ser                                                |
| _Tiré el isquiotibial ayer._   | **Me tiré el isquiotibial ayer.**  | Tirarse un músculo is reflexive — do not drop the "me"                              |
| _Tuve calambre._               | **Tuve un calambre.**              | Calambre needs the article: un calambre                                             |
| _Me cayé._                     | **Me caí.**                        | Caerse in the yo form is "me caí" (irregular — no "y")                              |
| _Caminé hasta la final._       | **Caminé hasta el final.**         | "El final" (the end) is masculine. "La final" means "the finals" (of a tournament). |

## Cultural Notes

### Running Culture in Valencia

Valencia has become one of Spain's top running cities. The **Jardín del Turia** — a 9-kilometer park built in the old riverbed — is a runner's paradise with smooth paths, shade trees, and no cars. The **Maratón Valencia Trinidad Alfonso** (held every December) regularly produces world-record times thanks to the flat course and mild weather. If you run in Valencia, you will share the Turia with joggers, cyclists, families, and the occasional busker.

Useful phrases if you run into a fellow runner (literally or figuratively):

| Spanish              | English                     |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- |
| ¿Cuántos kilómetros? | How many kilometers?        |
| Hoy hice diez.       | I did ten today.            |
| Buen ritmo.          | Good pace.                  |
| ¡Ánimo!              | Keep going! / You got this! |

### Sports Injuries and the Spanish Approach

In Spain, the first stop for a minor sports injury is often the **farmacia** (pharmacy) rather than the doctor. For more serious injuries, the public healthcare system (Seguridad Social) covers treatment at the **centro de salud** (health center) or hospital. Private clinics specializing in sports medicine (**clínica deportiva**) are also popular among runners.
