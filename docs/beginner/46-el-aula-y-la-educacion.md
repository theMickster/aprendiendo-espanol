# El Aula y la Educación

**Level:** Beginner (A2)
**Estimated Time:** 25 minutes
**Topics:** Classroom vocabulary, school subjects, education verbs, the Spanish school system

## Overview

If you have kids in Spain — or if you are a student yourself — school vocabulary is essential. You need to know what is in the classroom, what the subjects are called, how to talk about homework and grades, and how the Spanish school system works (spoiler: it is quite different from the US or UK). This lesson covers it all, from "el pupitre" to "el recreo," with real scenarios from the Shawsky kids starting school in Valencia.

> **Related:** [Lesson 44 - Profesiones de Servicio](44-profesiones-de-servicio.md) covers teacher vocabulary (enseñar, evaluar, calificar). This lesson focuses on the student side.

## Learning Objectives

- Name classroom objects and school supplies in Spanish
- Identify common school subjects
- Use education verbs (estudiar, aprender, repasar, aprobar, suspender)
- Talk about homework, exams, grades, and school schedules
- Understand how the Spanish school system is structured
- Express opinions about school subjects using "se me da bien/mal"

## Lesson Content

### Classroom Objects (En el Aula)

> **Grammar note:** "El aula" follows the same rule as "el asma" from [Lesson 41](41-respirar-y-los-pulmones.md) — it is a feminine noun that takes "el" in the singular because it starts with a stressed "a." Plural: **las** aulas. Adjectives stay feminine: el aula **pequeña**.

| Spanish                    | English               | Notes                                                                    |
| -------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **el pupitre**             | student desk          | not "escritorio" (that is a work desk)                                   |
| **la pizarra**             | whiteboard/chalkboard | la pizarra digital = smartboard                                          |
| **la tiza**                | chalk                 | less common now with whiteboards                                         |
| **el rotulador**           | marker                | for whiteboards                                                          |
| **el bolígrafo / el boli** | pen                   | "boli" is the everyday short form                                        |
| **el lápiz**               | pencil                | plural: los lápices                                                      |
| **el cuaderno**            | notebook              |                                                                          |
| **la libreta**             | small notebook        | common in Spain                                                          |
| **la mochila**             | backpack              |                                                                          |
| **la goma**                | eraser                | also means "rubber band"                                                 |
| **las tijeras**            | scissors              | always plural                                                            |
| **la regla**               | ruler                 | also means "rule"                                                        |
| **el sacapuntas**          | pencil sharpener      | literally "point-puller"                                                 |
| **la carpeta**             | folder                | NOT "carpet" — that is "alfombra" (see [Lesson 48](48-falsos-amigos.md)) |
| **el libro de texto**      | textbook              |                                                                          |
| **la agenda**              | planner/diary         | for writing homework assignments                                         |

### Expressing Opinions About Subjects

#### "Se me da bien / mal" (I'm good / bad at)

This is a key construction for talking about school subjects:

| Spanish                             | English                   |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Se me dan bien** las matemáticas. | I'm good at math.         |
| **Se me da mal** la historia.       | I'm bad at history.       |
| **Se me dan bien** las ciencias.    | I'm good at science.      |
| **¿Se te da bien** el inglés?       | Are you good at English?  |
| **Se le da muy bien** la música.    | She's very good at music. |

> **Agreement:** The verb "dar" agrees with the subject (the thing). Singular subject = "da." Plural subject = "dan." "Se me **da** bien la historia." vs. "Se me **dan** bien las mates."

#### Other Opinion Phrases

| Spanish                      | English                   |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Mi asignatura favorita es... | My favorite subject is... |
| Me encanta la clase de...    | I love the ... class.     |
| No me gusta nada...          | I don't like ... at all.  |
| ... es muy fácil / difícil.  | ... is very easy / hard.  |
| ... es aburrido/a.           | ... is boring.            |
| ... es interesante.          | ... is interesting.       |
| Me aburro en clase de...     | I get bored in ... class. |

### Scenario: First Day at School in Valencia

The Shawsky kids are starting at a new colegio in Valencia.

**Tutora:** ¡Buenos días! Bienvenidos al colegio. Yo soy Carmen, vuestra tutora. ¿Cómo os llamáis?

**Hija:** Me llamo Emily. Y este es mi hermano, Jake.

**Tutora:** Encantada. Vuestro pupitre está aquí, al lado de la ventana. ¿Tenéis todo? ¿Cuadernos, bolígrafos?

**Hija:** Sí, tenemos todo en la mochila.

**Tutora:** Perfecto. Hoy es un día especial — el horario es diferente. Primero tenemos lengua, después matemáticas, y antes del recreo, ciencias.

**Jake:** ¿A qué hora es el recreo?

**Tutora:** A las once y media. Tenéis treinta minutos. Después del recreo, inglés y educación física.

**Jake:** ¡Genial! La EF es mi asignatura favorita.

**Tutora:** _(sonríe)_ Eso dicen todos.

### Scenario: Talking About Subjects with Classmates

**Compañera:** ¿Qué tal las clases? ¿Os gustan?

**Hija:** Sí. Me encanta la clase de ciencias. La profesora explica muy bien.

**Compañera:** A mí se me dan fatal las ciencias. Prefiero la historia.

**Hija:** ¿Y las mates? A mí se me dan bastante bien.

**Compañera:** ¡Uf! Las mates son lo peor. Siempre suspendo los exámenes.

**Hija:** ¿En serio? Si quieres, puedo ayudarte a repasar.

**Compañera:** ¡Sí! Muchas gracias. ¿Después de clase?

**Hija:** Vale. En la biblioteca.

### Scenario: Parents Asking About Homework

**Mamá:** ¿Tienes deberes hoy?

**Hija:** Sí, tengo que hacer los ejercicios de matemáticas y estudiar para el examen de historia.

**Mamá:** ¿Cuándo es el examen?

**Hija:** El jueves. Tengo que repasar los temas tres y cuatro.

**Papá:** ¿Y tú, Jake? ¿Tienes deberes?

**Jake:** Solo una cosa de lengua. Tenemos que escribir una redacción sobre Valencia.

**Papá:** ¡Eso debería ser fácil! Lleváis viviendo aquí seis meses.

**Jake:** Sí, pero en español... Es más difícil.

**Mamá:** Venga, a trabajar. Primero deberes, después podéis jugar.

### The Spanish Grading System

In Spain, grades are on a scale of 0-10:

| Grade | Spanish Term            | Meaning              |
| ----- | ----------------------- | -------------------- |
| 10    | Matrícula de honor      | Outstanding / honors |
| 9-10  | Sobresaliente           | Excellent            |
| 7-8   | Notable                 | Very good            |
| 6     | Bien                    | Good                 |
| 5     | Suficiente / Aprobado   | Pass                 |
| 0-4   | Insuficiente / Suspenso | Fail                 |

- A **5** is the minimum to pass. Anything below 5 is a "suspenso" (fail).
- Getting a **10** is called "sacar un diez" and is a big deal.
- "Matrícula de honor" is the highest distinction and is rare.

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                                           | Right                         | Why                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _La aula es grande._                            | **El aula es grande.**        | "Aula" is feminine but takes "el" in singular (stressed-a rule). Adjective stays feminine: el aula **pequeña**. |
| _Suspendieron a mi hijo._ (meaning "he failed") | **Mi hijo suspendió.**        | "Suspender" means to fail (a test/subject), not to suspend (discipline). To suspend a student = "expulsar."     |
| _Pasé el examen._                               | **Aprobé el examen.**         | "Pasar" a test is a direct English calque. In Spanish, use "aprobar."                                           |
| _Estudio por el examen._                        | **Estudio para el examen.**   | Use "para" (purpose/goal), not "por" (reason), when studying for a test                                         |
| _Se me da bien las mates._                      | **Se me dan bien las mates.** | "Las mates" is plural, so the verb must be "dan," not "da"                                                      |

## Cultural Notes

### The Spanish School System

The Spanish education system is structured differently from the US or UK:

| Stage               | Age   | Spanish Name                           | Equivalent                  |
| ------------------- | ----- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Preschool           | 0-3   | Guardería / Escuela infantil           | Daycare / Preschool         |
| Kindergarten        | 3-6   | Educación Infantil                     | Pre-K through Kindergarten  |
| Primary             | 6-12  | Educación Primaria (1°-6°)             | Elementary school           |
| Secondary           | 12-16 | ESO (Educación Secundaria Obligatoria) | Middle + high school        |
| Pre-university      | 16-18 | Bachillerato                           | Last 2 years of high school |
| University entrance | 18    | Selectividad / EBAU                    | SAT equivalent              |

**Key differences from the US:**

- School is **mandatory** until age 16 (end of ESO). Bachillerato is optional.
- Many primary schools run 9:00-14:00 (no afternoon classes). Others do 9:00-17:00 with a 2-hour lunch.
- **Repetir curso** (repeating a year) is common and not stigmatized the way it can be in other countries.
- Most public schools do NOT require uniforms. Many private/concertado schools do.
- **Colegios concertados** are semi-private schools — privately run but publicly funded. They are very common in Spain.

### "El Recreo" — Sacred Time

In Spain, the recreo (recess) is sacred. Primary school kids get 30 minutes, typically at 11:00 or 11:30. Secondary students (ESO) also get a break, called "el patio" informally. During recreo, kids play, eat their "almuerzo" (mid-morning snack — usually a sandwich or fruit), and socialize. Teachers also take their coffee break.

### School Supplies Shopping: "La Vuelta al Cole"

Every September, Spain goes through "la vuelta al cole" (back to school). Supermarkets and stores fill with school supplies. Each school provides a list of supplies needed. In Valencia, you will find these at Carrefour, El Corte Inglés, or small "papelerías" (stationery shops).
