# Respirar y los Pulmones

**Level:** Beginner (A2)
**Estimated Time:** 25-30 minutes
**Topics:** Respiratory system vocabulary, breathing verbs, asthma, hospital vocabulary, past tense narration of health events

## Overview

This is the most personal lesson in the series. The Shawsky family knows hospitals well — their daughter has been hospitalized seven times in Denver due to asthma attacks and lung infections. This lesson teaches you the vocabulary for breathing, lungs, and asthma, plus the hospital words you hope you will never need but absolutely should know. You will also practice narrating health events in the past tense, building on the pretérito introduced in [Lesson 40](40-musculos-y-movimiento.md). By the end, you can describe what happened during a health scare and understand a doctor's basic instructions.

> **Prerequisites:** [Lesson 14 - El Cuerpo y la Salud](14-el-cuerpo-y-la-salud.md), [Lesson 38 - Más Partes del Cuerpo](38-mas-partes-del-cuerpo.md), [Lesson 40 - Músculos y Movimiento](40-musculos-y-movimiento.md) (for past tense)

## Learning Objectives

- Name respiratory system parts and breathing-related vocabulary
- Use breathing verbs: respirar, inhalar, exhalar, toser, estornudar
- Understand and use asthma-related vocabulary
- Know essential hospital and emergency vocabulary
- Narrate a past health event using the simple past tense
- Understand the special grammar of "el asma" (feminine noun with masculine article)

### Asthma (El Asma)

#### The Grammar of "El Asma"

This is a special Spanish grammar point. **Asma** is a **feminine** noun, but it uses the **masculine article** "el" because it starts with a stressed "a" sound. This is the same rule as "el agua" (water) and "el alma" (soul).

- **El** asma es una enfermedad crónica. (Asthma is a chronic illness.)
- El asma **severa** (not severo — the adjective is still feminine!)
- **Las** asmas alérgicas (plural goes back to feminine article)
- Tiene **mucha** asma (adjectives and quantifiers stay feminine)

> **The rule:** Feminine nouns starting with a stressed "a" or "ha" take "el" in the singular to avoid the awkward "la a..." sound. But the noun is still feminine for everything else — adjectives, plurals, and other modifiers.

### Past Tense Narration: Telling the Story

Building on the pretérito from [Lesson 40](40-musculos-y-movimiento.md), here are the key irregular past tenses you need for hospital narration:

| Verb      | Meaning                | yo     | él/ella | ellos/ellas |
| --------- | ---------------------- | ------ | ------- | ----------- |
| **ir**    | to go                  | fui    | fue     | fueron      |
| **ser**   | to be                  | fui    | fue     | fueron      |
| **estar** | to be (location/state) | estuve | estuvo  | estuvieron  |
| **tener** | to have                | tuve   | tuvo    | tuvieron    |
| **dar**   | to give                | di     | dio     | dieron      |
| **poner** | to put                 | puse   | puso    | pusieron    |
| **poder** | to be able             | pude   | pudo    | pudieron    |
| **hacer** | to do/make             | hice   | hizo    | hicieron    |

> **Ir and ser share the same past tense forms.** "Fue al hospital" = She went to the hospital. "Fue difícil" = It was difficult. Context tells you which verb it is.

**Time expressions for past narration:**

| Spanish                 | English                     |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------- |
| ayer                    | yesterday                   |
| anoche                  | last night                  |
| la semana pasada        | last week                   |
| el mes pasado           | last month                  |
| el año pasado           | last year                   |
| hace dos días           | two days ago                |
| hace una semana         | a week ago                  |
| de repente              | suddenly                    |
| por la noche            | at night / during the night |
| a las tres de la mañana | at three in the morning     |

### Scenario: A Night in the Hospital in Denver

This scenario is based on the Shawsky family's real experience — their daughter has been hospitalized seven times in Denver for asthma attacks and lung infections.

**Mamá cuenta la historia (Mamá tells the story):**

El martes por la noche, nuestra hija empezó a toser mucho. Tenía sibilancias y le dolía el pecho. Le dimos el inhalador, pero no mejoró.

A las dos de la mañana, no podía respirar bien. Le costaba mucho inhalar. Llamamos al 911 y fuimos al hospital.

En la sala de urgencias, los médicos le pusieron una mascarilla de oxígeno. Le hicieron una radiografía de los pulmones. Los doctores dijeron que tenía una infección pulmonar.

La ingresaron en el hospital. Estuvo tres días. Le dieron nebulizaciones cada cuatro horas y antibióticos por vía intravenosa. Poco a poco, mejoró.

El viernes le dieron de alta. Volvimos a casa con una receta para más medicinas. Fue muy difícil, pero nuestra hija es fuerte.

**Key past-tense verbs from this story:**

- empezó (she started) — empezar
- dimos (we gave) — dar
- mejoró (she improved) — mejorar
- podía (she could — ongoing state) — poder
- llamamos (we called) — llamar
- fuimos (we went) — ir
- pusieron (they put) — poner
- hicieron (they did/made) — hacer
- dijeron (they said) — decir
- ingresaron (they admitted) — ingresar
- estuvo (she was/stayed) — estar
- dieron (they gave) — dar
- dieron de alta (they discharged) — dar de alta
- volvimos (we returned) — volver
- fue (it was) — ser

### Scenario: Daily Asthma Management

Not every day is an emergency. Here is what daily management sounds like:

**Papá:** ¿Tomaste tu inhalador esta mañana?

**Hija:** Sí, lo usé antes del desayuno.

**Papá:** Bien. ¿Cómo te sientes hoy?

**Hija:** Bien. Respiro bien hoy. No tengo silbidos.

**Papá:** Perfecto. Pero lleva el inhalador en la mochila, por si acaso.

**Hija:** Siempre lo llevo, papá.

**Papá:** Y si sientes que te falta el aire durante la clase de gimnasia, para y dile a la profesora.

**Hija:** Lo sé, papá. No te preocupes.

**Key daily management phrases:**

| Spanish                     | English                          |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| ¿Tomaste tu inhalador?      | Did you take your inhaler?       |
| Lleva el inhalador contigo. | Carry your inhaler with you.     |
| Por si acaso.               | Just in case.                    |
| Si te falta el aire, para.  | If you're short of breath, stop. |
| Dile al profesor/a.         | Tell the teacher.                |
| Respiro bien hoy.           | I'm breathing well today.        |

### Scenario: Running with Asthma

**Papá:** ¿Quieres correr un poco en el Turia?

**Hija:** Sí, pero despacio. El médico dijo que puedo hacer ejercicio si caliento bien.

**Papá:** Perfecto. Primero calentamos cinco minutos. Y llevas el inhalador, ¿verdad?

**Hija:** Sí, está en mi bolsillo.

**Hija:** Papá, necesito parar. Me cuesta un poco respirar.

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                                                                  | Right                   | Why                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _La asma es difícil._                                                  | **El asma es difícil.** | Feminine nouns starting with stressed "a" take "el" in singular                                                                       |
| _El asma es severo._                                                   | **El asma es severa.**  | The noun is still feminine — adjectives agree with the feminine gender                                                                |
| _Fuimos a el hospital._                                                | **Fuimos al hospital.** | a + el always contracts to "al"                                                                                                       |
| _Le dieron alta._                                                      | **Le dieron de alta.**  | The phrase is "dar **de** alta" — do not drop the "de"                                                                                |
| _Ella no podía respirar. Fue al hospital._ (mixing tenses incorrectly) | Both are fine here.     | Podía (imperfect: ongoing state) + fue (preterite: completed action) is correct. Mixing these two past tenses is normal in narration. |

## Cultural Notes

### Healthcare in Spain vs. the United States

The Shawsky family's experience with hospitals in Denver is different from what they would encounter in Spain. Here are key differences:

|                      | United States               | Spain                                          |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Emergency number** | 911                         | 112                                            |
| **System**           | Primarily private insurance | Universal public healthcare (Seguridad Social) |
| **ER visits**        | Often very expensive        | Free with tarjeta sanitaria (health card)      |
| **Prescriptions**    | Often costly                | Heavily subsidized (often just a few euros)    |
| **Wait times**       | Varies by facility          | Can be long for non-emergencies                |

**If your child has asthma in Spain:**

- Register with the local **centro de salud** (public health center) to get a **tarjeta sanitaria** (health card)
- Inhalers and asthma medication are very affordable through the public system
- For emergencies, go directly to **urgencias** at any hospital — no appointment needed
- **Hospital La Fe** in Valencia is one of Spain's largest and best-equipped hospitals

### The Importance of "Historial Médico"

When you arrive at a new doctor or hospital, they will ask about your **historial médico** (medical history). For a child with asthma, be ready to explain:

- Tiene asma desde los \_\_ años. (She has had asthma since age \_\_.)
- Ha estado en el hospital \_\_ veces. (She has been in the hospital \_\_ times.)
- Usa un inhalador todos los días. (She uses an inhaler every day.)
- Es alérgica a... (She is allergic to...)
