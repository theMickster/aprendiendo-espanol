# Question Words

**Level:** Beginner (A1)
**Estimated Time:** 20-25 minutes
**Topics:** Question words (interrogatives), forming questions, inverted question marks, asking about things, people, places, time, manner, quantity, reasons, and choices

## Overview

Asking questions is how you navigate a new city, order food, make friends, and survive daily life. Spanish has eight core question words that unlock all of this. Once you learn them and pair them with verbs you already know, you can ask about almost anything.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Use all eight Spanish question words correctly
- Form questions using the question word + verb + rest of sentence pattern
- Distinguish between Cuál and Qué (a common source of confusion)
- Ask practical questions for daily life in Valencia

## Lesson Content

### The Basics: Inverted Question Marks

Every Spanish question is wrapped in question marks -- one at the end (?) and an inverted one at the start (¿). This tells the reader a question is coming before they even finish the sentence.

- Statement: Tienes hambre. (You are hungry.)
- Question: **¿Tienes hambre?** (Are you hungry?)

This lesson will use the inverted marks throughout -- just like real written Spanish.

---

### The 8 Question Words at a Glance

| Spanish             | English             | What It Asks About                |
| ------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Qué**             | What                | Things, definitions, explanations |
| **Quién / Quiénes** | Who                 | People                            |
| **Dónde**           | Where               | Places, locations                 |
| **Cuándo**          | When                | Time                              |
| **Cómo**            | How                 | Manner, condition                 |
| **Cuánto/a/os/as**  | How much / How many | Quantity                          |
| **Por qué**         | Why                 | Reasons                           |
| **Cuál / Cuáles**   | Which / What        | Choices, selection                |

All question words carry an accent mark when used in questions. This is how you tell them apart from their non-question cousins (e.g., **por qué** = why, **porque** = because).

---

### Qué - What

Use **qué** to ask about things, definitions, and explanations.

- **¿Qué es esto?** - What is this?
- **¿Qué haces?** - What are you doing? (HACER from lesson 03)
- **¿Qué quieres?** - What do you want?
- **¿Qué hora es?** - What time is it?

**At the Mercado Central:**

You point at an unfamiliar fruit at a stall: **¿Qué es esto?** The vendor replies: "Es una chirimoya -- una fruta tropical."

**Your Turn:** You walk into a restaurant near the Turia park and want to know what the daily special is. How would you ask? **¿Qué es \_\_\_?**

---

### Quién / Quiénes - Who

Use **quién** for one person, **quiénes** for multiple people.

- **¿Quién es ella?** - Who is she?
- **¿Quién tiene mi libro?** - Who has my book?
- **¿Quiénes son tus amigos?** - Who are your friends?
- **¿Con quién vas?** - Who are you going with?

Note how **quién** works with prepositions: **con quién** (with whom), **para quién** (for whom), **de quién** (whose).

**At school pickup:**

You see an unfamiliar parent at the school gates. Your daughter says they know her classmate. You ask: **¿Quién es? ¿Es la madre de tu amiga?**

---

### Dónde - Where

Use **dónde** to ask about places and locations. Pairs naturally with ESTAR (location) and IR (going).

- **¿Dónde está el baño?** - Where is the bathroom?
- **¿Dónde vives?** - Where do you live?
- **¿Dónde vas?** - Where are you going?
- **¿De dónde eres?** - Where are you from?

**Getting around Valencia:**

You are near Ciudad de las Artes y las Ciencias and need to find food. You stop a passerby: **Perdona, ¿dónde hay un restaurante cerca de aquí?**

**Your Turn:** You are lost near the beach. How do you ask where the bus stop is? **¿Dónde está \_\_\_?**

---

### Cuándo - When

Use **cuándo** to ask about time.

- **¿Cuándo es la fiesta?** - When is the party?
- **¿Cuándo vamos?** - When are we going?
- **¿Cuándo tienes clase?** - When do you have class?
- **¿Cuándo llega el autobús?** - When does the bus arrive?

**Planning a family outing:**

You want to visit the Turia park this weekend. You ask your family: **¿Cuándo vamos al Turia? ¿El sábado o el domingo?**

---

### Cómo - How

Use **cómo** to ask about manner, method, or condition.

- **¿Cómo estás?** - How are you? (You already know this one!)
- **¿Cómo se dice "thank you" en español?** - How do you say "thank you" in Spanish?
- **¿Cómo vas a la escuela?** - How do you get to school?
- **¿Cómo se llama?** - What is his/her name? (literally: How does he/she call himself/herself?)

**At a neighbor's house:**

You are invited for dinner by new neighbors. You taste the food and want the recipe: **Está delicioso. ¿Cómo se hace esta paella?**

---

### Cuánto/a/os/as - How Much / How Many

This is the question word that changes form. It agrees in gender and number with the noun it asks about.

| Form        | Used With                        | Example                                                   |
| ----------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cuánto**  | masculine singular (uncountable) | ¿Cuánto cuesta? (How much does it cost?)                  |
| **Cuánta**  | feminine singular (uncountable)  | ¿Cuánta agua quieres? (How much water do you want?)       |
| **Cuántos** | masculine plural (countable)     | ¿Cuántos años tienes? (How old are you?)                  |
| **Cuántas** | feminine plural (countable)      | ¿Cuántas hermanas tienes? (How many sisters do you have?) |

**Shopping at the Mercado Central:**

- **¿Cuánto cuesta el queso?** - How much does the cheese cost?
- **¿Cuántos tomates necesitas?** - How many tomatoes do you need?
- **¿Cuántas naranjas quieres?** - How many oranges do you want?

**Your Turn:** You are buying fruit for the family. Ask how much the strawberries cost: **¿Cuánto \_\_\_ las fresas?**

---

### Por qué - Why

Use **por qué** (two words) to ask for reasons. The answer uses **porque** (one word) meaning "because."

- **¿Por qué estudias español?** - Why do you study Spanish?
  - **Porque vivo en Valencia.** - Because I live in Valencia.
- **¿Por qué estás cansado?** - Why are you tired?
  - **Porque tengo mucho trabajo.** - Because I have a lot of work.

**Important:** Do not confuse these:

| Spanish       | Meaning | Usage             |
| ------------- | ------- | ----------------- |
| **¿Por qué?** | Why?    | Asking a question |
| **Porque**    | Because | Giving a reason   |

**At school:**

Your daughter does not want to do her homework. You ask: **¿Por qué no haces los deberes?** She replies: **Porque son difíciles.**

---

### Cuál / Cuáles - Which / What

Use **cuál** for singular and **cuáles** for plural. This one is tricky because it sometimes translates as "what" in English.

- **¿Cuál es tu nombre?** - What is your name?
- **¿Cuál prefieres?** - Which do you prefer?
- **¿Cuáles son tus libros?** - Which are your books?
- **¿Cuál es tu color favorito?** - What is your favorite color?

### Cuál vs Qué -- The Key Distinction

This is one of the trickiest points for English speakers. Here is the rule:

| Use Qué...                              | Use Cuál...                                                   |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Before a noun: **¿Qué libro quieres?**  | Before ES/SON (choosing from options): **¿Cuál es tu libro?** |
| For definitions: **¿Qué es la paella?** | For selection: **¿Cuál prefieres, la paella o la fideuà?**    |

Think of it this way: **Qué** asks "what kind of thing is this?" while **Cuál** asks "which one of these things?"

**At a restaurant near the beach:**

The waiter asks: **¿Qué quieren?** (What do you want?) You look at two desserts and ask: **¿Cuál es más dulce?** (Which one is sweeter?)

**Your Turn:** You want to know which bus goes to Ciudad de las Artes. How do you ask? **¿Cuál es el autobús que va a \_\_\_?**

---

### Forming Questions: The Pattern

The basic structure for Spanish questions is:

**Question word + conjugated verb + rest of sentence**

| Question                 | Structure                  |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| ¿Dónde vives?            | Dónde + vives              |
| ¿Qué haces hoy?          | Qué + haces + hoy          |
| ¿Cuándo vamos al parque? | Cuándo + vamos + al parque |
| ¿Cuántos años tienes?    | Cuántos + años + tienes    |

Notice the subject often disappears because the verb form already tells you who is doing the action. You can include it for emphasis or clarity: **¿Dónde vive usted?** (formal).

### The Shawsky Family Explores Valencia

The family is at Ciudad de las Artes y las Ciencias for the first time. Here are the questions flying around:

- The daughters: **¿Qué es ese edificio tan grande?** (What is that huge building?)
- Asking at the ticket booth: **¿Cuánto cuestan las entradas?** (How much do the tickets cost?)
- **¿Cuántas entradas necesitan?** - Cuatro, por favor. Dos adultos y dos niñas.
- **¿Dónde está el acuario?** (Where is the aquarium?)
- **¿Cuándo empieza la película en el planetario?** (When does the movie start at the planetarium?)
- **¿Cómo llegamos al Museo de las Ciencias?** (How do we get to the Science Museum?)
- **¿Por qué se llama "Ciudad de las Artes"?** (Why is it called "City of the Arts"?)
- **¿Cuál es mejor, el acuario o el museo?** (Which is better, the aquarium or the museum?)

All eight question words, one family outing.

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                       | Right                          | Why                                                                           |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _¿Qué es tu nombre?_        | **¿Cuál es tu nombre?**        | You are selecting your name from all possible names -- use Cuál before es/son |
| _¿Cuál es una paella?_      | **¿Qué es una paella?**        | You are asking for a definition -- use Qué                                    |
| _¿Porque estudias español?_ | **¿Por qué estudias español?** | Por qué (two words) is the question; porque (one word) is the answer          |
| _¿Cuántos agua?_            | **¿Cuánta agua?**              | Agua is feminine singular -- Cuánto must agree in gender and number           |
| _¿Dónde es el baño?_        | **¿Dónde está el baño?**       | Use ESTAR for location, not SER                                               |
| _¿Quién son tus amigos?_    | **¿Quiénes son tus amigos?**   | Multiple people require the plural Quiénes                                    |

## Practice Exercises

### Exercise 1: Match the Question Word

Choose the correct question word for each blank.

1. ¿**\_\_\_** es tu profesor favorito? (Who)
2. ¿**\_\_\_** está la biblioteca? (Where)
3. ¿**\_\_\_** cuesta un café? (How much)
4. ¿**\_\_\_** estudias español? (Why)
5. ¿**\_\_\_** te llamas? (How / What is your name)
6. ¿**\_\_\_** es tu clase? (When)
7. ¿**\_\_\_** es la paella? (What -- asking for a definition)
8. ¿**\_\_\_** prefieres, Valencia o Barcelona? (Which)

### Exercise 2: Complete the Conversations

Fill in the blanks with the correct question word.

1. A: ¿**\_\_\_** vives?
   B: Vivo en Valencia, cerca de la playa.

2. A: ¿**\_\_\_** hermanas tienes?
   B: Tengo una hermana.

3. A: ¿**\_\_\_** vas al mercado?
   B: Voy los sábados.

4. A: ¿**\_\_\_** no comes la sopa?
   B: Porque no tengo hambre.

5. A: ¿**\_\_\_** es la capital de España?
   B: Es Madrid.

### Exercise 3: Write the Question

You know the answer. Write the question that would produce it.

1. Answer: Me llamo Sofía. Question: ¿**\_\_\_**?
2. Answer: Estoy bien, gracias. Question: ¿**\_\_\_**?
3. Answer: Tengo doce años. Question: ¿**\_\_\_**?
4. Answer: Voy a la escuela. Question: ¿**\_\_\_**?
5. Answer: Porque me gusta mucho. Question: ¿**\_\_\_**?
