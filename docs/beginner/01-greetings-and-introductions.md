# Greetings and Introductions

**Level:** Beginner (A1)
**Estimated Time:** 20 minutes
**Topics:** Basic greetings, introductions, farewells

## Overview

Learn essential Spanish greetings and how to introduce yourself. These phrases are the foundation of every Spanish conversation and will help you make a great first impression!

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Greet people appropriately in different contexts (formal and informal)
- Introduce yourself with your name and basic information
- Ask someone's name and how they are
- Say goodbye in various situations

## Lesson Content

### Basic Greetings

**Informal (friends, family, peers):**

- **Hola** - Hi/Hello
- **¿Qué tal?** - How's it going?
- **¿Cómo estás?** - How are you?

**Formal (strangers, elders, professional settings):**

- **Buenos días** - Good morning (until ~2pm)
- **Buenas tardes** - Good afternoon/evening (2pm-8pm)
- **Buenas noches** - Good night/evening (after 8pm)
- **¿Cómo está usted?** - How are you? (formal)

**When to use:**
Use formal greetings with people you don't know well, anyone older, or in professional situations. Use informal with friends, family, and people your age.

### Introducing Yourself

**Basic introduction:**

- **Me llamo [name]** - My name is [name] (literally: "I call myself")
- **Soy [name]** - I am [name]
- **Mucho gusto** - Nice to meet you
- **Encantado/a** - Delighted (to meet you)
  - Use _encantado_ if you're male, _encantada_ if you're female

**Example:**

- **Spanish:** Hola, me llamo María. Mucho gusto.
- **English:** Hello, my name is María. Nice to meet you.

### Asking Someone's Name

- **¿Cómo te llamas?** - What's your name? (informal)
- **¿Cómo se llama usted?** - What's your name? (formal)
- **¿Cuál es tu nombre?** - What is your name? (informal, less common)

### Responding to "How are you?"

**Common responses:**

- **Bien, gracias** - Good/Well, thank you
- **Muy bien** - Very well
- **Regular** - So-so/Okay
- **Mal** - Bad/Not well
- **¿Y tú?** - And you? (informal)
- **¿Y usted?** - And you? (formal)

### Farewells

**Informal:**

- **Adiós** - Goodbye
- **Chao** - Bye (very casual, common in Spain and Latin America)
- **Hasta luego** - See you later
- **Hasta mañana** - See you tomorrow
- **Nos vemos** - See you

**Formal:**

- **Hasta luego** - See you later
- **Que tenga un buen día** - Have a good day

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ **Wrong:** "Buenos días" at night
  - ✅ **Right:** "Buenas noches" after 8pm
  - 💡 **Why:** Time-specific greetings matter in Spanish culture

- ❌ **Wrong:** "¿Cómo estás usted?" (mixing informal with formal)
  - ✅ **Right:** "¿Cómo está usted?" (fully formal)
  - 💡 **Why:** Don't mix "tú" forms with "usted"

- ❌ **Wrong:** Saying "Mucho gusto" every time you see someone
  - ✅ **Right:** Only say it when meeting someone for the first time
  - 💡 **Why:** It specifically means "nice to meet you" (first time)

## Cultural Notes

**Greetings in Spain:**

- In Spain, it's common to greet with **two kisses on the cheeks** (air kisses, starting with the right cheek) even among people who just met, especially among women or between women and men
- Men typically shake hands with other men, unless they're close friends or family
- Always greet shopkeepers when entering stores: "Buenos días" or "Buenas tardes"

**The "tú" vs "usted" question:**

- Valencia and most of Spain tend to be fairly informal
- When in doubt, start with "usted" and let the other person suggest using "tú"
- Young people almost always use "tú" with each other
- In business settings, wait to be invited to use "tú"

## Speaking Practice

Try these in front of a mirror:

1. Introduce yourself to an imaginary new friend (informal)
2. Introduce yourself to an imaginary business colleague (formal)
3. Have a complete conversation: greet, introduce, ask how they are, say goodbye
4. Practice all the greetings at different times of day
5. Record yourself and listen back—how's your pronunciation?
