# De Compras — Shopping

**Level:** Beginner (A1-A2)
**Estimated Time:** 15-20 minutes
**Topics:** Shopping vocabulary, prices, asking for things, Mercado Central scenarios

## Overview

Shopping is one of the first real-world situations where you need to speak Spanish with strangers. Whether you are buying tomatoes at Mercado Central or trying on a jacket on Calle Colón, you need specific vocabulary and phrases to get what you want. This lesson covers stores, shopping verbs, how to talk about prices, and gives you complete dialogues for the market and clothing stores so you can practise before you go.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Name common types of stores in Spanish
- Use key shopping verbs: comprar, vender, pagar, costar, buscar, probarse
- Ask for prices and understand the answer
- Navigate a market stall conversation from greeting to payment
- Ask for sizes and try on clothes at a clothing store
- Understand basic Spanish market culture and etiquette

## Lesson Content

### Types of Stores

Spanish stores often have names that tell you exactly what they sell. Notice the pattern: many end in **-ería**.

| Spanish         | English     | What They Sell     |
| --------------- | ----------- | ------------------ |
| la tienda       | store/shop  | general            |
| el supermercado | supermarket | everything         |
| el mercado      | market      | fresh food, stalls |
| la panadería    | bakery      | pan (bread)        |
| la carnicería   | butcher     | carne (meat)       |
| la frutería     | fruit shop  | fruta (fruit)      |
| la pescadería   | fish shop   | pescado (fish)     |
| la farmacia     | pharmacy    | medicine           |

> **Word pattern:** See how the product name becomes the store? carne → carnicería, fruta → frutería, pescado → pescadería. Once you know the food, you know the store.

### Shopping Verbs

| Infinitive | Meaning     | Example                        |
| ---------- | ----------- | ------------------------------ |
| comprar    | to buy      | Quiero comprar naranjas.       |
| vender     | to sell     | Aquí venden pescado fresco.    |
| pagar      | to pay      | ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?      |
| costar     | to cost     | ¿Cuánto cuesta este queso?     |
| buscar     | to look for | Busco un kilo de tomates.      |
| probarse   | to try on   | ¿Puedo probarme esta chaqueta? |

**Costar** is a stem-changing verb (o → ue). It is almost always used in third person:

| Subject       | Conjugation |
| ------------- | ----------- |
| Singular (it) | cuesta      |
| Plural (they) | cuestan     |

```
¿Cuánto cuesta la camiseta?  → How much does the t-shirt cost?
¿Cuánto cuestan los zapatos? → How much do the shoes cost?
```

### Essential Shopping Phrases

Memorize these — you will use them every time you shop.

**Asking:**

| Spanish         | English                |
| --------------- | ---------------------- |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta? | How much does it cost? |
| ¿Cuánto vale?   | How much is it worth?  |
| ¿Tiene...?      | Do you have...?        |
| Quisiera...     | I would like...        |
| Busco...        | I'm looking for...     |
| ¿Algo más?      | Anything else?         |

**Responding:**

| Spanish                   | English                        |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Me llevo esto.            | I'll take this.                |
| No, gracias. Nada más.    | No, thanks. Nothing else.      |
| Es muy caro/a.            | It's very expensive.           |
| ¿Tiene algo más barato/a? | Do you have something cheaper? |

> **Cuesta vs. Vale:** Both mean "how much?" and are interchangeable in everyday shopping. "¿Cuánto es?" is another common alternative.

### Talking About Prices

#### Numbers Review

Connecting back to Lesson 02, here are the numbers you will need most when shopping:

| Number | Spanish | Number | Spanish     |
| ------ | ------- | ------ | ----------- |
| 1      | uno     | 10     | diez        |
| 2      | dos     | 15     | quince      |
| 3      | tres    | 20     | veinte      |
| 4      | cuatro  | 25     | veinticinco |
| 5      | cinco   | 30     | treinta     |
| 6      | seis    | 40     | cuarenta    |
| 7      | siete   | 50     | cincuenta   |
| 8      | ocho    | 100    | cien        |
| 9      | nueve   | 200    | doscientos  |

#### Currency

In Spain, the currency is the **euro** (el euro). Cents are **céntimos**.

```
€3.50 → tres euros con cincuenta céntimos
€1.20 → un euro con veinte céntimos
€10   → diez euros
€0.75 → setenta y cinco céntimos
```

In casual speech, people often drop "céntimos": "tres con cincuenta" (three fifty).

#### Singular vs. Plural Subjects

The verb must agree with what is being priced:

```
La camiseta cuesta cinco euros.     → The t-shirt costs five euros. (singular)
Los tomates cuestan dos euros.      → The tomatoes cost two euros. (plural)
```

### Mercado Central Scenario

_Valencia's famous Mercado Central — buying fresh food at the market_

#### At the Fruit Stall (La Frutería)

|     | Español                                                   | English                                       |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 👋  | **Vendedor:** ¡Buenos días! ¿Qué le pongo?                | Good morning! What can I get you?             |
| 🍊  | **Mamá:** Buenos días. Quisiera un kilo de naranjas.      | Good morning. I would like a kilo of oranges. |
| 🍅  | **Mamá:** Y medio kilo de tomates, por favor.             | And half a kilo of tomatoes, please.          |
| 💬  | **Vendedor:** ¿Algo más?                                  | Anything else?                                |
| 🍋  | **Mamá:** Sí, una docena de limones. ¿Cuánto cuesta todo? | Yes, a dozen lemons. How much is everything?  |
| 💶  | **Vendedor:** Son seis euros con cincuenta.               | That's six euros fifty.                       |
| 💳  | **Mamá:** Aquí tiene. Gracias.                            | Here you go. Thank you.                       |
| 👋  | **Vendedor:** ¡Gracias a usted! ¡Hasta luego!             | Thank you! See you later!                     |

**Key quantity phrases:**

| Spanish           | English           |
| ----------------- | ----------------- |
| un kilo de...     | a kilo of...      |
| medio kilo de...  | half a kilo of... |
| una docena de...  | a dozen of...     |
| un cuarto de...   | a quarter of...   |
| cien gramos de... | 100 grams of...   |

#### At the Fish Stall (La Pescadería)

|     | Español                                                                      | English                                                    |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🐟  | **Papá:** Hola, ¿tiene gambas frescas?                                       | Hi, do you have fresh prawns?                              |
| 🦐  | **Vendedor:** Sí, están muy buenas hoy. ¿Cuánto quiere?                      | Yes, they're very good today. How much do you want?        |
| ⚖️  | **Papá:** Medio kilo, por favor. ¿Cuánto cuestan?                            | Half a kilo, please. How much do they cost?                |
| 💶  | **Vendedor:** Las gambas cuestan doce euros el kilo. Medio kilo, seis euros. | Prawns cost twelve euros per kilo. Half a kilo, six euros. |
| ✅  | **Papá:** Perfecto. Me llevo medio kilo.                                     | Perfect. I'll take half a kilo.                            |

> **Your Turn:** Practise ordering at the market. Say: "Quisiera un kilo de \_\_\_ y medio kilo de \_\_\_. ¿Cuánto cuesta todo?"

### Clothing Store Scenario

_En la tienda de ropa — connecting to Lesson 16 vocabulary_

|     | Español                                                    | English                                       |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 👋  | **Dependienta:** ¡Hola! ¿En qué puedo ayudarle?            | Hi! How can I help you?                       |
| 🔍  | **Hija 1:** Busco una camiseta azul.                       | I'm looking for a blue t-shirt.               |
| 👕  | **Dependienta:** ¿Qué talla?                               | What size?                                    |
| 📏  | **Hija 1:** La pequeña, por favor.                         | Small, please.                                |
| 🤔  | **Hija 1:** ¿Tiene una talla más grande?                   | Do you have a bigger size?                    |
| ✅  | **Dependienta:** Sí, aquí tiene la mediana.                | Yes, here's the medium.                       |
| 🪞  | **Hija 1:** ¿Puedo probarme esto? ¿Dónde está el probador? | Can I try this on? Where is the fitting room? |
| 👉  | **Dependienta:** El probador está al fondo a la derecha.   | The fitting room is at the back on the right. |
| 💶  | **Hija 1:** Me queda bien. ¿Cuánto cuesta?                 | It fits well. How much is it?                 |
| 🏷️  | **Dependienta:** Cuesta quince euros.                      | It costs fifteen euros.                       |
| 🛍️  | **Hija 1:** Me la llevo.                                   | I'll take it.                                 |

**Size vocabulary:**

| Spanish                       | English                     |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| ¿Qué talla?                   | What size?                  |
| la talla pequeña / la S       | small                       |
| la talla mediana / la M       | medium                      |
| la talla grande / la L        | large                       |
| ¿Tiene una talla más grande?  | Do you have a bigger size?  |
| ¿Tiene una talla más pequeña? | Do you have a smaller size? |

**Fitting room phrases:**

| Spanish                    | English                    |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| ¿Puedo probarme esto?      | Can I try this on?         |
| ¿Dónde está el probador?   | Where is the fitting room? |
| Me queda bien.             | It fits me well.           |
| Me queda grande/pequeño/a. | It's too big/small on me.  |
| Me lo/la llevo.            | I'll take it.              |

### Cultural Note: Shopping in Spain

- **Market culture:** Markets like Mercado Central are social places. Vendors greet you, ask how you are, and recommend their best products. It is normal to chat briefly. Don't rush.
- **Tipping:** Tipping is NOT expected in shops or markets. In restaurants, leaving small change (rounding up) is appreciated but not obligatory.
- **Cash vs. card:** Most shops accept card payments, but small market stalls may prefer cash, especially for purchases under five euros. It is a good idea to carry some coins.
- **Market hours:** Mercado Central is open Monday to Saturday, roughly 7:30 to 15:00. It is closed on Sundays. Go early for the best selection.
- **"¿Qué le pongo?"** This is the most common way a vendor will greet you. It literally means "What shall I put for you?" and is the equivalent of "What can I get you?"

## Common Mistakes

| #   | Wrong                           | Right                              | Why                                                          |
| --- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | ¿Cuánto es cuestan los tomates? | ¿Cuánto **cuestan** los tomates?   | Use cuestan alone — don't mix "es" and "cuestan."            |
| 2   | La camiseta cuestan diez euros. | La camiseta **cuesta** diez euros. | Singular subject (la camiseta) needs singular verb (cuesta). |
| 3   | Quiero probar esta chaqueta.    | Quiero **probarme** esta chaqueta. | Trying on clothes is reflexive — the action is on yourself.  |
| 4   | ¿Tiene un talla más grande?     | ¿Tiene **una** talla más grande?   | Talla is feminine — use "una," not "un."                     |
| 5   | Quisiera un kilo naranjas.      | Quisiera un kilo **de** naranjas.  | Quantities always use "de" before the noun.                  |
