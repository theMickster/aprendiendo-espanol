# Possessives & Demonstratives

**Level:** Beginner (A1)
**Estimated Time:** 15-20 minutes
**Topics:** Possessive adjectives (mi/tu/su/nuestro/vuestro), possessive pronouns (mío/tuyo/suyo), demonstratives (este/ese/aquel)

## Overview

You already know family vocabulary from Lesson 07 -- now you can talk about whose family members they are! This lesson brings together possessive adjectives (the short forms you have already seen), possessive pronouns (the long forms used after nouns and with SER), and demonstrative adjectives and pronouns for pointing things out. These are essential tools for everyday life in Valencia -- from shopping at the Mercado Central to sorting out whose things are whose at home.

## Learning Objectives

- Use short form possessive adjectives correctly (mi, tu, su, nuestro, vuestro)
- Understand why possessives agree with the thing possessed, not the possessor
- Use long form possessive pronouns (mío, tuyo, suyo, nuestro, vuestro) after SER and with articles
- Distinguish between este (near me), ese (near you), and aquel (far from both)
- Apply demonstratives in real Valencia shopping and daily life situations

## Lesson Content

### Short Form Possessive Adjectives (Review)

These go **before** the noun. You met them in Lesson 07 with family vocabulary. Here is the full table:

| English                       | Singular Noun         | Plural Noun             |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------- |
| my                            | **mi**                | **mis**                 |
| your (informal)               | **tu**                | **tus**                 |
| his / her / your (formal)     | **su**                | **sus**                 |
| our                           | **nuestro / nuestra** | **nuestros / nuestras** |
| your (plural, Spain)          | **vuestro / vuestra** | **vuestros / vuestras** |
| their / your (plural, formal) | **su**                | **sus**                 |

#### The Key Rule

Possessives agree with the **thing possessed**, NOT with the possessor. This is the number one mistake English speakers make.

- A father talking about his daughter: **mi hija** (mi is singular because there is one daughter)
- A mother talking about her daughters: **mis hijas** (mis is plural because there are two daughters)
- Both parents talking about their house: **nuestra casa** (nuestra is feminine because casa is feminine)
- Both parents talking about their car: **nuestro coche** (nuestro is masculine because coche is masculine)

Most possessives (mi, tu, su) only change for singular vs plural. Only **nuestro** and **vuestro** change for both gender AND number:

| Form         | Example                                              |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **nuestro**  | nuestro piso (our flat -- masculine singular)        |
| **nuestra**  | nuestra calle (our street -- feminine singular)      |
| **nuestros** | nuestros vecinos (our neighbors -- masculine plural) |
| **nuestras** | nuestras hijas (our daughters -- feminine plural)    |

#### The "Su" Ambiguity

**Su/sus** can mean his, her, its, your (formal), or their. Context usually makes it clear, but when you need to be precise, use **de + person** after the noun:

- **su libro** = his/her/your/their book (ambiguous)
- **el libro de María** = Maria's book (clear)
- **el libro de ella** = her book (clear)
- **el libro de ellos** = their book (clear)

### Long Form Possessive Pronouns

The long forms go **after** the noun or stand alone (usually with SER). They agree in both gender and number with the thing possessed.

| Person              | Masculine Sg | Feminine Sg | Masculine Pl | Feminine Pl  |
| ------------------- | ------------ | ----------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| yo                  | **mío**      | **mía**     | **míos**     | **mías**     |
| tú                  | **tuyo**     | **tuya**    | **tuyos**    | **tuyas**    |
| él/ella/usted       | **suyo**     | **suya**    | **suyos**    | **suyas**    |
| nosotros            | **nuestro**  | **nuestra** | **nuestros** | **nuestras** |
| vosotros            | **vuestro**  | **vuestra** | **vuestros** | **vuestras** |
| ellos/ellas/ustedes | **suyo**     | **suya**    | **suyos**    | **suyas**    |

#### When to Use Long Forms

**1. After the verb SER (to say who owns something):**

- ¿De quién es este libro? -- Es **mío**. (Whose book is this? -- It's mine.)
- Estas llaves son **tuyas**. (These keys are yours.)
- La decisión es **nuestra**. (The decision is ours.)

**2. After a noun, with an article (a friend of mine, an idea of yours):**

- Un amigo **mío** vive en Valencia. (A friend of mine lives in Valencia.)
- Una idea **tuya** funcionó bien. (An idea of yours worked well.)
- Unos vecinos **nuestros** son de Argentina. (Some neighbors of ours are from Argentina.)

**3. Standing alone with an article (replacing the noun):**

- Mi coche es rojo. El **tuyo** es azul. (My car is red. Yours is blue.)
- Mis hijas van al colegio. Las **suyas** van a otro. (My daughters go to the school. His/hers go to another.)

### Demonstrative Adjectives and Pronouns

Demonstratives point things out. Spanish has three levels of distance, not just two like English (this/that).

| Distance      | Masculine Sg | Feminine Sg | Masculine Pl | Feminine Pl  | Neutral     |
| ------------- | ------------ | ----------- | ------------ | ------------ | ----------- |
| Near me       | **este**     | **esta**    | **estos**    | **estas**    | **esto**    |
| Near you      | **ese**      | **esa**     | **esos**     | **esas**     | **eso**     |
| Far from both | **aquel**    | **aquella** | **aquellos** | **aquellas** | **aquello** |

**Think of it this way:**

- **Este/esta** = this (I can touch it, it is right here with me)
- **Ese/esa** = that (it is near you, or not far away)
- **Aquel/aquella** = that over there (it is far from both of us)

**As adjectives (before a noun):**

- **Este** libro es interesante. (This book is interesting.)
- **Esa** tienda es cara. (That shop is expensive.)
- **Aquella** iglesia es muy antigua. (That church over there is very old.)

**As pronouns (replacing a noun):**

- ¿Cuál quieres? -- **Este.** (Which one do you want? -- This one.)
- No me gusta ese. Prefiero **aquel.** (I don't like that one. I prefer that one over there.)

#### The Neutral Forms: esto, eso, aquello

The neutral forms refer to abstract ideas, situations, or unidentified things -- never to a specific noun with a known gender.

- ¿Qué es **esto**? (What is this?) -- pointing at something unknown
- **Eso** es interesante. (That is interesting.) -- referring to an idea
- No quiero hablar de **aquello**. (I don't want to talk about that.) -- a past situation

### Valencia Scenario: Shopping at the Mercado Central

The Shawsky family is at the Mercado Central buying fruit and vegetables. Demonstratives are essential when pointing at items.

**At the fruit stall:**

**Vendedor:** ¡Buenos días! ¿Qué desean?

**Mamá:** ¿Cuánto cuestan **estas** naranjas?

**Vendedor:** **Estas** de aquí son a dos euros el kilo. **Esas** de ahí son más dulces, a tres euros.

**Papá:** ¿Y **aquellas** del fondo?

**Vendedor:** **Aquellas** son naranjas de Valencia, las mejores. Cuatro euros el kilo.

**Mamá:** Queremos un kilo de **estas** y medio kilo de **aquellas**.

**At the cheese counter:**

**Hija:** Mamá, ¿**este** queso es **nuestro**?

**Mamá:** No, el **nuestro** es **ese** de ahí. **Este** es de la señora.

**Hija:** ¿Y **aquel** grande?

**Mamá:** **Ese** no es **nuestro**. Es **suyo** -- de la señora.

---

### Valencia Scenario: Whose Things Are Whose?

After school, the Shawsky house is chaos. Everyone's things are everywhere.

**Mamá:** ¿De quién es **esta** mochila?

**Hija 1:** Es **mía**.

**Mamá:** ¿Y **esos** zapatos? ¿Son **tuyos**?

**Hija 2:** No, no son **míos**. Son de mi hermana.

**Papá:** Las llaves del coche... ¿son **estas**?

**Mamá:** No, las **tuyas** están en la cocina. **Estas** son **mías**.

**Papá:** ¿Y **aquel** paraguas en la entrada?

**Mamá:** **Ese** es de los vecinos. El **nuestro** es el azul.

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                 | Right                       | Why                                                               |
| --------------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _Este libro es mía._  | **Este libro es mío.**      | Libro is masculine, so use mío, not mía                           |
| _Nuestro hijas_       | **Nuestras hijas**          | Hijas is feminine plural, so use nuestras                         |
| _Un amigo de mi_      | **Un amigo mío**            | Use the long form after the noun                                  |
| _Esto libro_          | **Este libro**              | Esto is neutral (no specific noun). Use este with masculine nouns |
| _Esa casa allí lejos_ | **Aquella casa allí lejos** | If it is far from both speakers, use aquel/aquella                |

## Practice Exercises

### Exercise 1: Short Form or Long Form?

Fill in the blank with the correct possessive (short form before the noun, long form after SER or after the noun).

1. \_\_\_ casa está en el centro de Valencia. (our)
2. ¿De quién es este bolígrafo? -- Es \_\_\_. (mine)
3. Un primo \_\_\_ viene a visitarnos. (of ours)
4. \_\_\_ padres viven en Inglaterra. (my)
5. Estas maletas son \_\_\_. (yours, informal singular)
6. \_\_\_ colegio está cerca del Turia. (their)
7. La idea es \_\_\_. (yours, informal singular)
8. \_\_\_ vecinos son muy simpáticos. (our)

### Exercise 2: Este, Ese, or Aquel?

Choose the correct demonstrative for each situation.

1. You are holding a book: \_\_\_ libro es muy bueno.
2. Your friend is wearing a hat: \_\_\_ sombrero es bonito.
3. You both see a mountain in the distance: \_\_\_ montaña es muy alta.
4. You point at oranges right in front of you: Quiero \_\_\_ naranjas.
5. Something abstract your friend just said: \_\_\_ es interesante.
6. A church at the far end of the plaza: \_\_\_ iglesia es del siglo XV.

### Exercise 3: Valencia Shopping Challenge

Complete the conversation at the Mercado Central with the correct possessives and demonstratives.

**Vendedor:** ¡Hola! ¿Qué necesitan hoy?

**Tú:** Queremos tomates. ¿Cuánto cuestan (1) \_\_\_ tomates? _(these, near me)_

**Vendedor:** Dos euros el kilo. ¿Y algo más?

**Tú:** Sí, (2) \_\_\_ pimientos de ahí también. _(those, near you)_

**Vendedor:** ¿Un kilo?

**Tú:** Sí, y también (3) \_\_\_ melón de allí al fondo. _(that one over there)_

**Vendedor:** Aquí tienen. ¿La bolsa es (4) \_\_\_? _(yours, plural informal)_

**Tú:** No, la (5) \_\_\_ es la azul. (6) \_\_\_ es de otra señora. _(ours / that one)_
