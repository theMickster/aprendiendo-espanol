# Articles & Noun Gender

**Level:** Beginner (A1)
**Estimated Time:** 15-20 minutes
**Topics:** el/la/un/una, masculine/feminine rules, plurals (los/las/unos/unas), contractions al/del

## Overview

Every noun in Spanish has a gender -- masculine or feminine -- and the article in front of it must match. This is one of the biggest shifts for English speakers because English just has "the" and "a." Once you learn the patterns, though, you will get the gender right most of the time. This lesson is also essential groundwork for adjective agreement, which you will learn in Lesson 10.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Choose the correct definite article (el, la, los, las) for common nouns
- Choose the correct indefinite article (un, una, unos, unas) for common nouns
- Predict noun gender using word endings
- Form plurals of Spanish nouns
- Use the contractions al and del correctly

## Lesson Content

### Definite Articles: "The"

In English, "the" works for everything. In Spanish, there are four versions:

| Article | Gender + Number    | Example    | English    |
| ------- | ------------------ | ---------- | ---------- |
| **el**  | masculine singular | el libro   | the book   |
| **la**  | feminine singular  | la mesa    | the table  |
| **los** | masculine plural   | los libros | the books  |
| **las** | feminine plural    | las mesas  | the tables |

**When to use definite articles:** Spanish uses "the" more than English does. You need it for general statements, days of the week, and talking about things in general:

- El chocolate es delicioso (Chocolate is delicious -- general statement)
- Los lunes voy a clase (On Mondays I go to class)
- Me gusta la música (I like music)

---

### Indefinite Articles: "A / An / Some"

| Article  | Gender + Number    | Example     | English     |
| -------- | ------------------ | ----------- | ----------- |
| **un**   | masculine singular | un libro    | a book      |
| **una**  | feminine singular  | una mesa    | a table     |
| **unos** | masculine plural   | unos libros | some books  |
| **unas** | feminine plural    | unas mesas  | some tables |

**When to use indefinite articles:** Use these when introducing something for the first time or talking about an unspecified item:

- Tengo un perro (I have a dog)
- Hay una farmacia en esa calle (There's a pharmacy on that street)
- Necesito unos huevos (I need some eggs)

### How to Know the Gender: Word Ending Rules

Most of the time, the ending of a noun tells you its gender.

**Masculine endings (use el/un):**

| Ending                 | Examples                                |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **-o**                 | el libro, el queso, el mercado, el vaso |
| **-or**                | el color, el calor, el profesor         |
| **-aje**               | el viaje, el paisaje, el garaje         |
| **-ma** (Greek origin) | el problema, el programa, el sistema    |

**Feminine endings (use la/una):**

| Ending    | Examples                                  |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **-a**    | la casa, la mesa, la naranja, la paella   |
| **-ción** | la estación, la información, la educación |
| **-sión** | la decisión, la televisión                |
| **-dad**  | la ciudad, la universidad, la verdad      |
| **-tad**  | la libertad, la dificultad                |

**The shortcut:** If it ends in -o, think masculine. If it ends in -a, -ción, -sión, -dad, or -tad, think feminine. This covers the vast majority of nouns you will encounter.

### Common Exceptions

Spanish would not be Spanish without some exceptions. Memorize these -- they come up constantly:

**Masculine nouns that end in -a:**

| Noun        | English     | Why?                          |
| ----------- | ----------- | ----------------------------- |
| el día      | the day     | Just irregular -- memorize it |
| el mapa     | the map     | Greek origin                  |
| el problema | the problem | Greek origin (-ma ending)     |
| el programa | the program | Greek origin (-ma ending)     |
| el sistema  | the system  | Greek origin (-ma ending)     |

**Feminine nouns that end in -o:**

| Noun     | English        | Why?                          |
| -------- | -------------- | ----------------------------- |
| la mano  | the hand       | Just irregular -- memorize it |
| la foto  | the photo      | Short for la fotografía       |
| la moto  | the motorcycle | Short for la motocicleta      |
| la radio | the radio      | Short for la radiodifusión    |

**Tip:** When a shortened word ends in -o but comes from a feminine word, it stays feminine. And words of Greek origin ending in -ma are masculine.

### Forming Plurals

The rules are straightforward:

| Noun ends in... | Rule               | Singular  | Plural       |
| --------------- | ------------------ | --------- | ------------ |
| a vowel         | add **-s**         | el libro  | los libros   |
| a consonant     | add **-es**        | la ciudad | las ciudades |
| **-z**          | change to **-ces** | el lápiz  | los lápices  |

**Examples:**

- la naranja --> las naranjas
- el tomate --> los tomates
- el profesor --> los profesores
- la luz --> las luces

**Note:** The article changes to plural too. El becomes los, la becomes las, un becomes unos, una becomes unas.

---

### Contractions: AL and DEL

Spanish has only two contractions, and both involve **el**:

| Full Form | Contraction | Example          | English              |
| --------- | ----------- | ---------------- | -------------------- |
| a + el    | **al**      | Vamos al mercado | We go to the market  |
| de + el   | **del**     | Vengo del parque | I come from the park |

**Important:** These ONLY happen with **el** (masculine singular). They never happen with la, los, or las:

- Vamos a la playa (NOT "vamos ala playa")
- Vengo de la escuela (NOT "vengo dela escuela")
- Vamos a los jardines (no contraction with los)

**Your Turn:** Combine these: Voy a + el cine = Voy **\_\_\_** cine. Es la puerta de + el colegio = Es la puerta **\_\_\_** colegio.

### Valencia Scenario: Shopping at Mercado Central

The family is at Mercado Central buying ingredients for a big Sunday paella. Notice the articles on every noun:

- Necesitamos **el** arroz (We need the rice)
- Quiero **un** kilo de **los** tomates (I want a kilo of the tomatoes)
- **Las** naranjas de Valencia son **las** mejores (Valencia's oranges are the best)
- Dame **una** barra de pan (Give me a loaf of bread)
- **El** queso cuesta cinco euros (The cheese costs five euros)
- **La** fruta está muy fresca hoy (The fruit is very fresh today)
- Compramos **unos** limones y **unas** cebollas (We buy some lemons and some onions)

The girls spot something: "Mamá, quiero **un** helado." (Mom, I want an ice cream.) "Y yo quiero **una** empanada." (And I want an empanada.)

### Valencia Scenario: Around the City

The family is walking through Valencia and describing what they see:

- **El** Jardín del Turia es enorme (The Turia Garden is enormous)
- Vamos **al** parque después de **la** escuela (We go to the park after school)
- **La** Ciudad de las Artes y las Ciencias es impresionante (The City of Arts and Sciences is impressive)
- **Las** playas de Valencia son bonitas (Valencia's beaches are pretty)
- Hay **un** museo cerca de **la** estación (There's a museum near the station)
- **Los** autobuses son baratos (The buses are cheap)

Notice the contractions: Jardín **del** Turia (de + el), vamos **al** parque (a + el).

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                     | Right                        | Why                                                                            |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| _la problema_             | **el problema**              | Greek-origin -ma words are masculine                                           |
| _el mano_                 | **la mano**                  | Irregular -- always feminine                                                   |
| _las ciudads_             | **las ciudades**             | Consonant endings add -es, not just -s                                         |
| _Voy a el mercado_        | **Voy al mercado**           | a + el always contracts to al                                                  |
| _el agua fría / las agua_ | **el agua fría / las aguas** | Feminine nouns starting with stressed "a" use el in singular but las in plural |
| _Tengo el hambre_         | **Tengo hambre**             | Some expressions drop the article entirely                                     |
