# Más Partes del Cuerpo

**Level:** Beginner (A1-A2)
**Estimated Time:** 20 minutes
**Topics:** Expanded body part vocabulary, body action verbs, describing physical features

## Overview

In [Lesson 14](14-el-cuerpo-y-la-salud.md) you learned the basics — head, arms, legs, eyes, and how to say "it hurts." But the body has a lot more going on. This lesson fills in the gaps: face details, joints, muscles, bones, and skin. More importantly, you will learn verbs for what you **do** with those body parts — raise your hand, turn your head, bend your knees. By the end, you will be able to describe people and give simple physical instructions.

## Learning Objectives

- Name additional body parts not covered in Lesson 14 (face, joints, muscles, bones, skin, internal organs)
- Use action verbs paired with body parts in present tense
- Give and follow simple physical commands ("Simón Dice" style)
- Describe people's physical features using body vocabulary + adjectives

## Lesson Content

### Expanding the Face (La Cara)

Lesson 14 gave you ojos, nariz, boca, orejas, dientes, lengua, and frente. Here are the parts it skipped:

| Spanish                         | English   | Notes                                    |
| ------------------------------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **el labio**                    | lip       | los labios = lips                        |
| **la mejilla**                  | cheek     |                                          |
| **el mentón** / **la barbilla** | chin      | both are common; barbilla is more casual |
| **las cejas**                   | eyebrows  | singular: la ceja                        |
| **las pestañas**                | eyelashes | singular: la pestaña                     |
| **la mandíbula**                | jaw       |                                          |

### Joints and Connectors (Las Articulaciones)

These are the parts that bend, twist, and connect everything together:

| Spanish        | English  | Notes                                      |
| -------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **el codo**    | elbow    |                                            |
| **la muñeca**  | wrist    | also means "doll" — context makes it clear |
| **el tobillo** | ankle    |                                            |
| **la cadera**  | hip      |                                            |
| **la cintura** | waist    |                                            |
| **el hombro**  | shoulder | los hombros = shoulders (review from L14)  |
| **la rodilla** | knee     | las rodillas = knees (review from L14)     |

**Watch out:** "La muñeca" means both "wrist" and "doll." If someone says "Me duele la muñeca," they mean their wrist. If a child says "¿Dónde está mi muñeca?" they probably mean their doll.

### Verbs You Do with Body Parts

This is where it gets fun. Instead of just labeling parts, now you can say what you **do** with them.

#### Movement Commands

| Spanish                 | English              | Body Part    |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- | ------------ |
| **levantar** la mano    | raise your hand      | la mano      |
| **girar** la cabeza     | turn your head       | la cabeza    |
| **doblar** las rodillas | bend your knees      | las rodillas |
| **estirar** los brazos  | stretch your arms    | los brazos   |
| **abrir** los ojos      | open your eyes       | los ojos     |
| **cerrar** la boca      | close your mouth     | la boca      |
| **encoger** los hombros | shrug your shoulders | los hombros  |
| **mover** los dedos     | wiggle your fingers  | los dedos    |
| **cruzar** las piernas  | cross your legs      | las piernas  |
| **tocar** la nariz      | touch your nose      | la nariz     |

> **Remember from Lesson 14:** Spanish uses the definite article (el/la/los/las) with body parts, not possessives. "Levanta **la** mano" — not "Levanta **tu** mano."

#### Using "Poder" (Can/To Be Able To)

Combine **poder** with these verbs to say what you can or cannot do:

- **No puedo mover** el brazo. (I cannot move my arm.)
- **¿Puedes doblar** la rodilla? (Can you bend your knee?)
- **No puede girar** la cabeza. (He/she cannot turn their head.)
- **Podemos estirar** las piernas aquí. (We can stretch our legs here.)

### Scenario: Family Stretching in the Turia Park

The Shawsky family is stretching before a walk through the Jardín del Turia.

**Papá:** ¡Vamos a estirar antes de caminar! Primero, levantad los brazos.

**Mamá:** Ahora girad la cabeza a la derecha... y a la izquierda.

**Hija:** ¡Me duele el cuello!

**Mamá:** Es normal. Ahora doblad las rodillas un poco.

**Papá:** Moved los tobillos en círculos. Así... perfecto.

**Hijo:** ¿Puedo tocar los dedos de los pies? ¡Mira!

**Papá:** ¡Muy bien! Yo no puedo. Tengo las piernas muy rígidas hoy.

**Key verbs from this scene:**

- levantad (raise — vosotros command)
- girad (turn — vosotros command)
- doblad (bend — vosotros command)
- moved (move — vosotros command)
- tocar (to touch)

### Describing People (Describir a las Personas)

Now that you know face parts and body vocabulary, you can describe what people look like. Use **tener** for features:

| Spanish                   | English                 |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Tiene los ojos grandes.   | She has big eyes.       |
| Tiene la nariz pequeña.   | He has a small nose.    |
| Tiene los labios finos.   | She has thin lips.      |
| Tiene los hombros anchos. | He has broad shoulders. |
| Tiene las piernas largas. | She has long legs.      |
| Tiene el pelo rizado.     | He has curly hair.      |
| Tiene las cejas gruesas.  | She has thick eyebrows. |

**Useful description adjectives:**

| Spanish  | English    | Opposite                 |
| -------- | ---------- | ------------------------ |
| grande   | big        | pequeño/a (small)        |
| largo/a  | long       | corto/a (short)          |
| ancho/a  | wide/broad | estrecho/a (narrow)      |
| grueso/a | thick      | fino/a (thin)            |
| fuerte   | strong     | débil (weak)             |
| rizado/a | curly      | liso/a (straight — hair) |

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                       | Right                            | Why                                                                      |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| _Levanta tu mano._          | **Levanta la mano.**             | Use the definite article, not possessive, with body parts                |
| _Tengo dolor en mi muñeca._ | **Me duele la muñeca.**          | The indirect object pronoun (me) already indicates whose body part it is |
| _No puedo mover mi brazo._  | **No puedo mover el brazo.**     | Same rule — el/la, not mi/tu/su                                          |
| _Ella tiene ojos grandes._  | **Ella tiene los ojos grandes.** | Descriptions of body features use the article                            |
| _El muñeca_                 | **La muñeca**                    | Muñeca is feminine despite sounding like it could be masculine           |

## Cultural Notes

### Describing People in Spanish Culture

In Spain and Latin America, it is quite common and culturally acceptable to describe people by their physical features in casual conversation — much more so than in English-speaking countries. You might hear nicknames like "el gordo" (the chubby one) or "la flaca" (the skinny one) used affectionately among friends and family. This is not considered rude in context — it is simply descriptive. However, as a learner, it is best to stick to neutral descriptions until you understand the social context well.

### "Vosotros" Commands in Spain

In the stretching scenario, you saw commands like "levantad" and "girad." In Spain, when speaking to a group informally (like a family), you use the **vosotros** command form. In Latin America, you would hear "levanten" and "giren" (ustedes form) instead. Since the Shawsky family lives in Valencia, you will see vosotros forms in family scenarios throughout these lessons.

- Point to five different body parts and say their names in Spanish
- Give yourself three "Simón Dice" commands and act them out
- Describe a family member using at least three body-part sentences with **tener**
- Practice saying: "No puedo mover **_" and "¿Puedes doblar _**?" with different body parts
