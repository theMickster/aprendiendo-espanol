# Regular Verb Patterns (-AR, -ER, -IR)

**Level:** Beginner (A1)
**Estimated Time:** 20-25 minutes
**Topics:** hablar/comer/vivir conjugation, regular verb endings, building sentences

## Overview

In Lesson 03, you learned six irregular verbs that form the backbone of Spanish. Now it is time to learn the predictable patterns. Spanish verbs fall into three families based on their infinitive ending: -AR, -ER, and -IR. Once you learn the endings for each family, you can conjugate hundreds of verbs correctly without memorizing each one individually.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Identify which family a verb belongs to by its infinitive ending
- Conjugate regular -AR, -ER, and -IR verbs in the present tense
- Recognize the stem + ending pattern that makes conjugation predictable
- Use common regular verbs to describe daily life and routines
- Build simple sentences with Subject + Verb + Complement

## Lesson Content

### The Big Idea: Stems and Endings

Every Spanish infinitive has two parts:

| Part       | What it is                     | Example: hablar |
| ---------- | ------------------------------ | --------------- |
| **Stem**   | The root meaning of the verb   | habl-           |
| **Ending** | Changes based on who is acting | -ar             |

To conjugate, you drop the infinitive ending (-ar, -er, or -ir) and add the correct ending for the person. That is the entire system. If a verb is regular, this pattern never changes.

---

### -AR Verbs (The Largest Family)

About 70% of Spanish verbs are -AR verbs. Learn this pattern well -- you will use it constantly.

**Model verb: HABLAR (to speak)**

| Person              | Ending    | Conjugation | English                  |
| ------------------- | --------- | ----------- | ------------------------ |
| Yo                  | **-o**    | hablo       | I speak                  |
| Tú                  | **-as**   | hablas      | you speak                |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | **-a**    | habla       | he/she speaks, you speak |
| Nosotros/as         | **-amos** | hablamos    | we speak                 |
| Vosotros/as         | **-áis**  | habláis     | you all speak            |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | **-an**   | hablan      | they/you all speak       |

**Common regular -AR verbs:**

| Verb      | English    | Example                    |
| --------- | ---------- | -------------------------- |
| caminar   | to walk    | Camino por el parque       |
| estudiar  | to study   | Estudio español cada día   |
| trabajar  | to work    | Trabajo en una oficina     |
| comprar   | to buy     | Compro fruta en el mercado |
| cocinar   | to cook    | Cocino paella los domingos |
| llegar    | to arrive  | Llego a las ocho           |
| necesitar | to need    | Necesito un bolígrafo      |
| preparar  | to prepare | Preparo la cena            |

**Your Turn:** Conjugate "estudiar" for nosotros: Nosotros **\_\_\_** español. Now try "comprar" for ellos: Ellos **\_\_\_** naranjas.

### -ER Verbs

The second family. Fewer verbs than -AR, but many are everyday words.

**Model verb: COMER (to eat)**

| Person              | Ending    | Conjugation | English              |
| ------------------- | --------- | ----------- | -------------------- |
| Yo                  | **-o**    | como        | I eat                |
| Tú                  | **-es**   | comes       | you eat              |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | **-e**    | come        | he/she eats, you eat |
| Nosotros/as         | **-emos** | comemos     | we eat               |
| Vosotros/as         | **-éis**  | coméis      | you all eat          |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | **-en**   | comen       | they/you all eat     |

**Common regular -ER verbs:**

| Verb       | English       | Example                  |
| ---------- | ------------- | ------------------------ |
| beber      | to drink      | Bebo agua con la comida  |
| leer       | to read       | Leo un libro cada semana |
| correr     | to run        | Corro por las mañanas    |
| aprender   | to learn      | Aprendo español rápido   |
| comprender | to understand | Comprendo la lección     |
| vender     | to sell       | Venden fruta fresca      |

**Your Turn:** Conjugate "aprender" for yo: Yo **\_\_\_** español. Now try "beber" for tú: Tú **\_\_\_** mucha agua.

---

### -IR Verbs

The smallest family. The endings are almost identical to -ER verbs, with differences only in the nosotros and vosotros forms.

**Model verb: VIVIR (to live)**

| Person              | Ending    | Conjugation | English                |
| ------------------- | --------- | ----------- | ---------------------- |
| Yo                  | **-o**    | vivo        | I live                 |
| Tú                  | **-es**   | vives       | you live               |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | **-e**    | vive        | he/she lives, you live |
| Nosotros/as         | **-imos** | vivimos     | we live                |
| Vosotros/as         | **-ís**   | vivís       | you all live           |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | **-en**   | viven       | they/you all live      |

**Common regular -IR verbs:**

| Verb      | English        | Example                |
| --------- | -------------- | ---------------------- |
| escribir  | to write       | Escribo en mi cuaderno |
| abrir     | to open        | Abro la ventana        |
| recibir   | to receive     | Recibo un mensaje      |
| subir     | to go up/climb | Subo las escaleras     |
| compartir | to share       | Compartimos la comida  |

**Your Turn:** Conjugate "vivir" for nosotros: Nosotros **\_\_\_** en Valencia. Now try "escribir" for ella: Ella **\_\_\_** una carta.

### Spot the Pattern: -ER vs -IR Side by Side

Notice how similar these two families are:

| Person              | -ER (comer) | -IR (vivir) | Different? |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ----------- | ---------- |
| Yo                  | com**o**    | viv**o**    | Same       |
| Tú                  | com**es**   | viv**es**   | Same       |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | com**e**    | viv**e**    | Same       |
| Nosotros/as         | com**emos** | viv**imos** | Different  |
| Vosotros/as         | com**éis**  | viv**ís**   | Different  |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | com**en**   | viv**en**   | Same       |

The only differences are in the nosotros (-emos vs -imos) and vosotros (-éis vs -ís) forms. Every other ending is identical.

### Building Simple Sentences

Now that you can conjugate, put it together with this formula:

**Subject + Conjugated Verb + Complement**

You can often drop the subject pronoun in Spanish because the verb ending already tells you who is acting:

| Full sentence                | Shortened (natural) | English               |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------- |
| Yo estudio español           | Estudio español     | I study Spanish       |
| Nosotros caminamos al parque | Caminamos al parque | We walk to the park   |
| Ella escribe una carta       | Escribe una carta   | She writes a letter   |
| Ellos viven en Valencia      | Viven en Valencia   | They live in Valencia |

**When to keep the subject pronoun:** Use it for emphasis, contrast, or clarity:

- **Yo** cocino y **tú** limpias (I cook and you clean -- contrast)
- **Ella** vive aquí (She lives here -- clarifying who)

### Valencia Scenario: A Morning in the Shawsky House

Here is a typical school morning, using regular verbs from all three families:

The alarm sounds at seven. The parents **preparan** (prepare) breakfast in the kitchen. The older daughter **abre** (opens) her backpack and **busca** (looks for) her homework. The younger daughter **bebe** (drinks) her orange juice slowly.

- Mamá **cocina** el desayuno (Mom cooks breakfast)
- Papá **lee** las noticias en el teléfono (Dad reads the news on his phone)
- Las niñas **comen** tostadas con mermelada (The girls eat toast with jam)
- Todos **caminamos** a la escuela juntos (We all walk to school together)
- Las niñas **suben** al autobús escolar (The girls get on the school bus)

After school, the family walks to the Jardín del Turia:

- Las niñas **corren** por el parque (The girls run through the park)
- Papá **camina** y **escucha** música (Dad walks and listens to music)
- Mamá **lee** un libro en un banco (Mom reads a book on a bench)
- Todos **compartimos** un helado (We all share an ice cream)

**Your Turn:** Describe what your family does in the morning. Use at least one -AR, one -ER, and one -IR verb.

### Valencia Scenario: Saturday at the Beach

The family heads to la Malvarrosa beach on Saturday:

- **Caminamos** hasta la playa (We walk to the beach)
- Las niñas **nadan** en el mar (The girls swim in the sea)
- Papá **lee** un libro bajo la sombrilla (Dad reads a book under the umbrella)
- Mamá **escribe** postales a la familia (Mom writes postcards to family)
- Todos **comemos** en un restaurante cerca de la playa (We all eat at a restaurant near the beach)
- **Bebemos** horchata -- la bebida típica de Valencia (We drink horchata -- Valencia's typical drink)

### A Note on Irregular Verbs

You already know six irregular verbs from Lesson 03 (SER, ESTAR, IR, TENER, HACER, HABER). Those verbs are irregular because their stems change or their conjugations do not follow the patterns above.

The good news: the majority of Spanish verbs ARE regular. When you encounter a new verb, assume it follows these patterns unless told otherwise. Your vocabulary will grow fast because you only need to learn the infinitive and apply the endings.

---

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                                      | Right                   | Why                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _Yo hablo, tú hablo_                       | **Yo hablo, tú hablas** | Each person gets a unique ending -- do not reuse the yo form   |
| _Nosotros vivemos_                         | **Nosotros vivimos**    | -IR verbs use -imos for nosotros, not -emos                    |
| _Ella come (for "she runs")_               | **Ella corre**          | Come means "eats" -- watch which verb you are conjugating      |
| _Yo estudio español_ (always including yo) | **Estudio español**     | Dropping the pronoun is more natural; use yo only for emphasis |
| _Ellos comes_                              | **Ellos comen**         | Ellos/ellas/ustedes always use the -n ending                   |
