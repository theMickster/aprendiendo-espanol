# Social Spanish — Beyond the Textbook

**Level:** Beginner (A1-A2)
**Estimated Time:** 15-20 minutes
**Topics:** Casual greetings, informal goodbyes, slang, texting shortcuts, informal register

## Overview

Textbook Spanish gets you started, but real conversations in Valencia sound very different. People drop formalities fast, use slang constantly, and text in abbreviations that look like a secret code. This lesson covers the casual greetings, informal goodbyes, Spain-specific slang, and texting shortcuts you will actually hear and see every day. Think of it as the gap between what your textbook teaches and what your neighbors actually say.

## Learning Objectives

- Use casual greetings that go beyond "Hola, ¿cómo estás?"
- Say goodbye informally like a local in Valencia
- Recognize and use common Spain-specific slang words
- Read and write basic Spanish text messages and WhatsApp abbreviations
- Know when to use informal register vs formal register in daily life

## Lesson Content

### Casual Greetings

Lesson 01 covered formal greetings like "Buenos días" and "¿Cómo está usted?" Those are real and useful, but here is what you will actually hear most of the time in Valencia:

| Greeting         | Meaning                      | When to use                                                 |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **¿Qué tal?**    | What's up? / How's it going? | The most common casual greeting in Spain                    |
| **¿Qué hay?**    | What's up?                   | Very informal, among friends                                |
| **¿Cómo vas?**   | How are you doing?           | Casual, implies "how's life going?"                         |
| **¿Cómo andas?** | How are you doing?           | Same as above, slightly more colloquial                     |
| **¡Buenas!**     | Hey! / Hi!                   | Short for buenos días/buenas tardes — works any time of day |
| **¿Qué pasa?**   | What's happening?            | Casual, among friends and acquaintances                     |

> **Valencia tip:** "¡Buenas!" is incredibly common. You will hear it at the bakery, at school pickup, at the gym. It replaces the full "Buenos días / Buenas tardes" and nobody thinks twice about it.

**Common responses:**

| Response              | Meaning                     | Tone                                                |
| --------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bien**              | Good                        | Neutral, works everywhere                           |
| **Todo bien**         | All good                    | Relaxed, positive                                   |
| **Aquí andamos**      | Here we are / Getting along | Casual, common in Spain                             |
| **Tirando**           | Getting by                  | Honest, slightly humorous — like "hanging in there" |
| **Pues nada, biennn** | Well, nothing, goood        | Very colloquial, drawn-out "bien" is common         |

### Informal Goodbyes

Forget "Adiós" for a moment. Here is how people actually say goodbye in everyday situations:

| Goodbye                | Meaning              | Notes                                                |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **¡Nos vemos!**        | See you!             | Very common, casual                                  |
| **¡Hasta luego!**      | See you later!       | Works in both casual and semi-formal contexts        |
| **¡Venga!**            | Alright then! / Bye! | Spain-specific — used constantly as a casual goodbye |
| **¡Cuídate!**          | Take care!           | Warm, slightly affectionate                          |
| **¡Que te vaya bien!** | Hope it goes well!   | Used when someone is leaving for something specific  |
| **¡Venga, nos vemos!** | Alright, see you!    | Very natural combination                             |

> **About "¡Venga!":** This word is everywhere in Spain. It can mean "come on," "let's go," "OK," or "bye" depending on context. At the end of a conversation, it signals "alright, we're wrapping up." You will hear it multiple times per day.

### Common Spanish Slang (Spain-Specific)

These are words and expressions you will hear constantly in Valencia and across Spain. They are not rude, but they are informal — use them with friends, neighbors, and other parents, not with your bank manager.

| Slang                 | Meaning                         | Example                                                     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **tío / tía**         | dude / girl (used like "mate")  | ¡Tío, qué fuerte! (Dude, that's crazy!)                     |
| **mola**              | it's cool                       | Esa camiseta mola. (That shirt is cool.)                    |
| **mola mucho**        | it's awesome                    | Valencia mola mucho. (Valencia is awesome.)                 |
| **¡Qué guay!**        | How cool!                       | ¡Qué guay tu casa nueva! (How cool, your new house!)        |
| **flipar**            | to be amazed / shocked          | ¡Estoy flipando! (I'm amazed/shocked!)                      |
| **quedar / quedarse** | to meet up / to arrange to meet | ¿Quedamos mañana? (Shall we meet up tomorrow?)              |
| **currar**            | to work (informal)              | Mañana curro hasta las cinco. (Tomorrow I work until five.) |
| **¡Vale!**            | OK! / Alright!                  | ¿Quedamos a las tres? ¡Vale! (Meet at three? OK!)           |

> **About "¡Vale!":** If you learn one slang word, make it this one. Spaniards say "vale" the way Americans say "OK" — dozens of times a day, in every conversation. It confirms, agrees, acknowledges, and wraps up. "Vale, vale" (double vale) means "OK, OK, I got it."

**A few more useful expressions:**

- **Mola mogollón** — It's super cool (mogollón = a ton / a lot, very colloquial)
- \*\*Ir de

cañas\*\* — To go out for beers (a caña is a small draft beer, very Spanish tradition)

- \*\*Hacer

botellón\*\* — To drink outdoors in a group (common among young people, you will see it in parks)

- \*\*Pasarlo

bien / genial\*\* — To have a good / great time

### Texting and Messaging

Spanish texting shortcuts look wild at first, but once you learn the patterns, they make sense. Here are the most common abbreviations you will see on WhatsApp:

| Abbreviation | Full form       | Meaning          |
| ------------ | --------------- | ---------------- |
| **q**        | que             | that / what      |
| **xq**       | porque          | because          |
| **tb**       | también         | also             |
| **x**        | por             | for / by         |
| **tkm**      | te quiero mucho | I love you a lot |
| **bss**      | besos           | kisses           |
| **bn**       | bien            | good / well      |
| **d**        | de              | of / from        |
| **xa**       | para            | for              |
| **kdd**      | quedada         | meetup           |
| **finde**    | fin de semana   | weekend          |

**Example WhatsApp conversation:**

```
Ana: ¿Qdamos sta tarde? (¿Quedamos esta tarde?)
Mick: Vale, xa q? (Vale, ¿para qué?)
Ana: Ir d cañas x el centro (Ir de cañas por el centro)
Mick: Mola! A q hora? (¡Mola! ¿A qué hora?)
Ana: A las 6 tb viene Laura (A las 6, también viene Laura)
Mick: Perfecto, bss! (Perfecto, ¡besos!)
```

**Other texting notes:**

- "Jajaja" is the Spanish "hahaha" — the J is pronounced like an H, so it sounds the same as laughing
- "Jeje" is a softer giggle, "jijiji" is a mischievous laugh
- Spaniards love voice messages on WhatsApp — sometimes more than typing
- Emojis are used heavily, especially the laughing-crying face and the heart

### Formal vs Informal Register

In Valencia, daily life runs on the informal register. Here is a quick guide:

**Almost always use tú (informal) with:**

- Parents at your kids' school
- Neighbors
- Shopkeepers at your regular stores
- Anyone roughly your age or younger
- Waiters and bar staff
- Other parents at the park

**Use usted (formal) with:**

- Elderly people you do not know well
- Official settings (government offices, police)
- Doctors (though many switch to tú quickly)
- Job interviews

**How generations differ:**

| Situation           | Younger people (under 40) | Older people (over 60)        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Meeting someone new | "¡Buenas! ¿Qué tal?"      | "Buenos días, ¿cómo está?"    |
| Saying goodbye      | "¡Venga, nos vemos!"      | "Hasta luego, que vaya bien." |
| Agreeing            | "Vale, vale"              | "Muy bien, de acuerdo"        |
| Expressing surprise | "¡Qué fuerte, tío!"       | "¡Madre mía!"                 |

> **Family tip:** When in doubt, start with tú in Valencia. If someone is clearly much older, use usted. If they want you to switch, they will say "Trátame de tú" (Use tú with me). This happens constantly — Spaniards prefer informality.

### Valencia Scenarios

**Scenario 1: School pickup**

You arrive at school to pick up the kids and see another parent you have met a few times.

| Speaker      | Spanish                                    | English                                                   |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| You          | ¡Buenas! ¿Qué tal?                         | Hey! How's it going?                                      |
| Other parent | Bien, bien. ¿Y tú? ¿Cómo vas?              | Good, good. And you? How are you doing?                   |
| You          | Tirando, que es viernes.                   | Getting by — it's Friday.                                 |
| Other parent | Jaja, ¡ya! ¿Hacéis algo el finde?          | Haha, right! Are you doing anything this weekend?         |
| You          | Pues no sé, a ver. ¿Quedamos en el parque? | Well, I don't know, we'll see. Shall we meet at the park? |
| Other parent | ¡Mola! ¡Venga, nos vemos!                  | Cool! Alright, see you!                                   |
| You          | ¡Vale, hasta luego! ¡Cuídate!              | OK, see you later! Take care!                             |

**Scenario 2: Chatting with your neighbor**

Your neighbor catches you in the hallway of your apartment building.

| Speaker  | Spanish                              | English                                         |
| -------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Neighbor | ¡Buenas! ¿Qué pasa, tío?             | Hey! What's up, dude?                           |
| You      | Aquí andamos, ¿y tú?                 | Getting along, and you?                         |
| Neighbor | Bien, acabo de currar. Estoy muerto. | Good, just finished work. I'm dead (exhausted). |
| You      | Ya, qué fuerte. ¿Mucho curro?        | Yeah, that's rough. A lot of work?              |
| Neighbor | Mogollón. Bueno, que te vaya bien.   | A ton. Well, hope it goes well.                 |
| You      | ¡Igualmente! ¡Venga, hasta luego!    | Likewise! Alright, see you later!               |

**Scenario 3: WhatsApp group with Spanish friends**

```
Laura: Ey qdamos sta noche? 🍺
Pablo: Mola, yo me apunto
You: Vale! Xa donde?
Laura: X el Carmen, hay bares nuevos q molan mucho
Pablo: A q hora?
Laura: A las 21:30 os va bn?
You: Perfecto 👍
Pablo: Venga bss a todos!
```

## Common Mistakes

| #   | Mistake                                    | Better                             | Why                                                                     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Using "tío" with someone much older        | Use their name or skip the address | "Tío/tía" is casual — fine with peers, awkward with elders              |
| 2   | Saying "adiós" to close friends every time | Use "¡Venga!" or "¡Nos vemos!"     | "Adiós" can sound overly final in casual settings                       |
| 3   | Texting "por qué" in full on WhatsApp      | Use "xq" or at least "porq"        | Not wrong, but your Spanish friends will notice you're being formal     |
| 4   | Overusing "¡Qué guay!" with older locals   | "¡Qué bien!" works for all ages    | "Guay" is younger slang — older people understand it but may not use it |
| 5   | Forgetting "vale" exists                   | Sprinkle "vale" everywhere         | It is the oil that keeps Spanish conversation running smoothly          |

**Greetings:**
