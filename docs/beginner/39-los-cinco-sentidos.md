# Los Cinco Sentidos

**Level:** Beginner (A1-A2)
**Estimated Time:** 20-25 minutes
**Topics:** The five senses, sense verbs, ver vs. mirar, oír vs. escuchar, irregular verb oler

## Overview

Your body does not just sit there — it takes in the world. You see, hear, smell, taste, and touch everything around you. In this lesson, you will connect the sense organs you already know (ojos, nariz, orejas, boca, manos) with the **verbs** that describe what they do. You will learn the important difference between "seeing" and "looking" (ver vs. mirar), "hearing" and "listening" (oír vs. escuchar), and you will tackle the irregular verb **oler** (to smell). By the end, you can describe a walk through the Mercado Central using all five senses.

> **Prerequisite:** [Lesson 14 - El Cuerpo y la Salud](14-el-cuerpo-y-la-salud.md) and [Lesson 38 - Más Partes del Cuerpo](38-mas-partes-del-cuerpo.md)

## Learning Objectives

- Name the five senses and their associated body parts in Spanish
- Use the correct sense verb for each sense (ver, oír, oler, tocar, probar)
- Distinguish between ver/mirar and oír/escuchar (passive vs. active perception)
- Conjugate the irregular verb **oler** in the present tense
- Describe sensory experiences in everyday situations

## Lesson Content

### The Five Senses at a Glance

| Sentido (Sense)       | Órgano (Organ)         | Verbo (Verb)                                        | Example           |
| --------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| **la vista** (sight)  | los ojos               | **ver** (to see) / **mirar** (to look at)           | Veo el mar.       |
| **el oído** (hearing) | las orejas / los oídos | **oír** (to hear) / **escuchar** (to listen)        | Oigo música.      |
| **el olfato** (smell) | la nariz               | **oler** (to smell)                                 | Huele a flores.   |
| **el gusto** (taste)  | la lengua / la boca    | **probar** (to taste/try) / **saborear** (to savor) | Prueba la paella. |
| **el tacto** (touch)  | las manos / la piel    | **tocar** (to touch)                                | No toques eso.    |

> **Ojo vs. oído:** "El ojo" is the eye (what you see with). "El oído" is the inner ear / the sense of hearing. "La oreja" is the outer ear (the part you can see and touch). When talking about hearing ability, use "oído": "Tiene buen oído" (She has good hearing).

---

### Ver vs. Mirar (See vs. Look At)

This is one of the most important distinctions in Spanish sensory verbs.

**Ver** = to see (passive — it happens to you)
**Mirar** = to look at (active — you choose to do it)

| Ver (to see)                            | Mirar (to look at)                         |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Veo un pájaro. (I see a bird.)          | Miro el pájaro. (I look at the bird.)      |
| ¿Ves esa señal? (Do you see that sign?) | Mira esa señal. (Look at that sign.)       |
| No veo nada. (I cannot see anything.)   | No mires el sol. (Do not look at the sun.) |

**Think of it this way:**

- **Ver** = your eyes receive the image (like English "see")
- **Mirar** = your eyes focus on purpose (like English "look at" or "watch")

You **ves** the TV in the room. You **miras** the TV when you sit down to watch.

**Present tense conjugation:**

|             | ver   | mirar   |
| ----------- | ----- | ------- |
| yo          | veo   | miro    |
| tú          | ves   | miras   |
| él/ella     | ve    | mira    |
| nosotros    | vemos | miramos |
| vosotros    | veis  | miráis  |
| ellos/ellas | ven   | miran   |

---

### Oír vs. Escuchar (Hear vs. Listen)

Same idea — passive vs. active.

**Oír** = to hear (passive — sound reaches your ears)
**Escuchar** = to listen (active — you pay attention on purpose)

| Oír (to hear)                           | Escuchar (to listen)                             |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Oigo un ruido. (I hear a noise.)        | Escucho música. (I listen to music.)             |
| ¿Oyes eso? (Do you hear that?)          | Escucha al profesor. (Listen to the teacher.)    |
| No oigo nada. (I cannot hear anything.) | Escuchamos un pódcast. (We listen to a podcast.) |

**Oír is irregular** — note the "y" that appears in the tú, él/ella, and ellos/ellas forms:

|             | oír   |
| ----------- | ----- |
| yo          | oigo  |
| tú          | oyes  |
| él/ella     | oye   |
| nosotros    | oímos |
| vosotros    | oís   |
| ellos/ellas | oyen  |

**Escuchar** is regular (-ar pattern): escucho, escuchas, escucha, escuchamos, escucháis, escuchan.

### Oler (To Smell) — The Tricky One

**Oler** has an unusual stem change: **o → hue** (not just o → ue). The "h" appears because Spanish does not allow words to start with "ue."

|             | oler       |
| ----------- | ---------- |
| yo          | **huelo**  |
| tú          | **hueles** |
| él/ella     | **huele**  |
| nosotros    | olemos     |
| vosotros    | oléis      |
| ellos/ellas | **huelen** |

> **Why the H?** In Spanish, you cannot start a word with the "ue" sound — it must have an "h" in front. So "uelo" becomes "**h**uelo." The nosotros/vosotros forms keep the "o" because the stress is on a different syllable and the stem does not change.

**Two uses of oler:**

1. **Oler** = to smell something (active): Huelo las flores. (I smell the flowers.)
2. **Oler a** = to smell like something: Huele a café. (It smells like coffee.)

| Spanish             | English                     |
| ------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Huele bien.         | It smells good.             |
| Huele mal.          | It smells bad.              |
| Huele a pan fresco. | It smells like fresh bread. |
| ¿A qué huele?       | What does it smell like?    |
| Huelo algo raro.    | I smell something strange.  |

---

### Probar y Saborear (To Taste/Try and To Savor)

**Probar** (o → ue stem change) = to taste, to try
**Saborear** (regular -ar) = to savor, to taste slowly

|             | probar   |
| ----------- | -------- |
| yo          | pruebo   |
| tú          | pruebas  |
| él/ella     | prueba   |
| nosotros    | probamos |
| vosotros    | probáis  |
| ellos/ellas | prueban  |

| Spanish              | English                         |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Prueba la sopa.      | Taste the soup. / Try the soup. |
| ¿Quieres probar?     | Do you want to try (it)?        |
| Saboreo cada bocado. | I savor every bite.             |

> **Note:** "Probar" also means "to try" in general (probar un restaurante nuevo = try a new restaurant). Context tells you whether it means "taste" or "try."

---

### Tocar (To Touch)

**Tocar** is regular (-ar) but has a second meaning: "to play" (an instrument).

| Spanish                          | English                     |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Toca la pared.                   | Touch the wall.             |
| No toques eso.                   | Don't touch that.           |
| La arena está caliente — tócala. | The sand is hot — touch it. |
| Toca la guitarra.                | She plays the guitar.       |

> **Context is key:** "Toca la guitarra" = She plays the guitar. "Toca la guitarra" (pointing at the instrument) = Touch the guitar. Tone, gesture, and context make it clear.

### Scenario: A Walk Through the Mercado Central

The Shawsky family visits Valencia's Mercado Central — one of the largest and most beautiful fresh food markets in Europe.

**Mamá:** ¡Mirad todo esto! ¿Veis cuántos colores? Las frutas, las verduras...

**Hija:** ¡Huele increíble aquí! Huele a jamón y a pan fresco.

**Papá:** Escuchad — oigo al vendedor de pescado. Está gritando los precios.

**Hijo:** ¿Puedo probar esa naranja?

**Vendedor:** ¡Claro! Prueba, prueba. Las naranjas valencianas son las mejores.

**Hijo:** _(prueba la naranja)_ ¡Está dulcísima!

**Mamá:** Tocad esta piel de melocotón — es suave como terciopelo.

**Hija:** Mira, papá — ¿ves esos mariscos? Son enormes.

**Papá:** Sí, los veo. Y huelen muy frescos. Vamos a comprar unos para la cena.

**Senses used in this scene:**

- 👁️ **Vista:** "¿Veis cuántos colores?" / "Mira... ¿ves esos mariscos?"
- 👂 **Oído:** "Escuchad" / "Oigo al vendedor"
- 👃 **Olfato:** "Huele a jamón y a pan fresco" / "Huelen muy frescos"
- 👅 **Gusto:** "¿Puedo probar?" / "Prueba, prueba"
- ✋ **Tacto:** "Tocad esta piel de melocotón"

### Scenario: Cooking Paella at Home

**Mamá:** ¿Hueles el azafrán? Es el ingrediente secreto de la paella.

**Hija:** Sí, huelo algo muy rico. ¿Puedo probar?

**Mamá:** Todavía no. Mira el color del arroz — ¿ves cómo cambia de blanco a amarillo?

**Hija:** ¡Sí! Es precioso. Y escucho cómo hierve el caldo.

**Mamá:** Exacto. Ahora toca el mango de la sartén — ¿está caliente?

**Hija:** ¡Sí, mucho! No toco más.

**Mamá:** Bien. En diez minutos puedes probar la primera cucharada.

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                         | Right                          | Why                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _Miro un pájaro en el cielo._ | **Veo un pájaro en el cielo.** | You spotted it passively — use ver. Use mirar when you actively focus on it.          |
| _Escucho un ruido extraño._   | **Oigo un ruido extraño.**     | A sudden noise you did not choose to hear — use oír. Escuchar = deliberate listening. |
| _Uelo las flores._            | **Huelo las flores.**          | The stem change adds an "h" — ue cannot start a word in Spanish.                      |
| _¿Quieres tastar la sopa?_    | **¿Quieres probar la sopa?**   | "Tastar" is not standard Spanish. The verb is probar.                                 |
| _Huele como café._            | **Huele a café.**              | "To smell like" uses oler **a**, not oler como.                                       |

## Cultural Notes

### The Mercado Central de Valencia

The Mercado Central is one of Europe's largest fresh food markets, housed in a stunning modernist building from 1928. With over 1,000 stalls, it is a feast for **all five senses**:

- **Vista:** The stained glass dome, colorful tile work, and mountains of fresh produce
- **Oído:** Vendors calling out prices, the buzz of hundreds of shoppers, the clink of coins
- **Olfato:** Fresh bread from nearby bakeries, cured ham, spices, fresh seafood
- **Gusto:** Free samples of olives, cheese, dried fruits, and of course, fresh Valencia oranges
- **Tacto:** Squeezing avocados for ripeness, feeling the smooth skin of tomatoes

It is open Monday through Saturday, and locals do their daily shopping here. Unlike supermarkets, you are expected to **ask the vendor** for what you want rather than grabbing items yourself.

### "¡No toques!" — The Universal Parent Phrase

If you visit any market, museum, or shop with children in Spain, you will hear parents say "¡No toques!" (Don't touch!) approximately five hundred times. It is the same in every language and every country.

Try these out loud:

- Walk around your home and describe what you see, hear, and smell in Spanish
- Practice the oler conjugation aloud: huelo, hueles, huele, olemos, oléis, huelen
- Describe your favorite meal using all five senses: "Veo... Huele a... Pruebo... Escucho... Toco..."
- Practice the ver/mirar distinction: point to things you "see" (ver) then actively "look at" (mirar)
