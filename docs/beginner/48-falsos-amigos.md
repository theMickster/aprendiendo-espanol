# Falsos Amigos

**Level:** Beginner (A2)
**Estimated Time:** 25 minutes
**Topics:** False cognates, English-Spanish word traps, common mistranslation patterns for English speakers

## Overview

Spanish and English share thousands of words that look and sound similar — "hospital," "animal," "hotel," "radio." These are true cognates, and they make learning Spanish easier. But lurking among them are the **falsos amigos** (false friends): words that look like English words but mean something completely different. Saying "estoy embarazada" when you mean "I'm embarrassed" will get you some very surprised looks. This lesson gives you the most important false friends to watch out for, organized by how badly things can go wrong if you mix them up.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify the most common English-Spanish false friends
- Avoid embarrassing or confusing mistranslations
- Know the correct Spanish word for each false friend trap
- Understand why false friends exist (shared Latin roots that diverged)
- Recognize false friends in context and translate correctly

## Lesson Content

### The Danger Scale

We have organized false friends into three categories based on how much trouble they can cause.

---

### Category 1: Dangerous False Friends

These will cause embarrassment, offense, or serious misunderstanding. Memorize these first.

| Spanish          | Looks Like   | Actually Means        | The Word You Want            |
| ---------------- | ------------ | --------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **embarazada**   | embarrassed  | **pregnant**          | avergonzado/a                |
| **molestar**     | to molest    | **to bother / annoy** | acosar, abusar               |
| **excitado/a**   | excited      | **sexually aroused**  | emocionado/a, entusiasmado/a |
| **violador**     | violator     | **rapist**            | infractor                    |
| **preservativo** | preservative | **condom**            | conservante                  |
| **constipado**   | constipated  | **having a cold**     | estreñido/a                  |

**Example sentences to burn into your memory:**

| Wrong (what you said)          | What they heard           | What you meant to say                                                                  |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ¡Estoy muy embarazada!         | I'm very pregnant!        | **Estoy muy avergonzada.** (I'm very embarrassed.)                                     |
| Mi hijo me molesta.            | My son bothers me.        | This one is actually correct — but be careful with "molestar" around English speakers! |
| ¡Estoy excitado por la fiesta! | I'm aroused by the party! | **¡Estoy emocionado por la fiesta!** (I'm excited about the party!)                    |

### Category 2: Confusing False Friends

These will not offend anyone, but they will cause misunderstanding. People will look at you funny.

| Spanish         | Looks Like              | Actually Means               | The Word You Want        |
| --------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **actual**      | actual                  | **current, present**         | real, verdadero/a        |
| **actualmente** | actually                | **currently**                | en realidad, de hecho    |
| **sensible**    | sensible                | **sensitive**                | sensato/a                |
| **éxito**       | exit                    | **success**                  | salida                   |
| **realizar**    | to realize              | **to carry out, accomplish** | darse cuenta de          |
| **asistir**     | to assist               | **to attend**                | ayudar                   |
| **carpeta**     | carpet                  | **folder**                   | alfombra, moqueta        |
| **librería**    | library                 | **bookstore**                | biblioteca               |
| **largo**       | large                   | **long**                     | grande                   |
| **recordar**    | to record               | **to remember**              | grabar                   |
| **fábrica**     | fabric                  | **factory**                  | tela                     |
| **introducir**  | to introduce (a person) | **to insert, enter**         | presentar                |
| **conductor**   | conductor (music)       | **driver**                   | director/a (de orquesta) |
| **contestar**   | to contest              | **to answer**                | competir, disputar       |

**Example sentences:**

| Wrong                                                     | Right                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| El presidente actual es... (meaning "the real president") | **El presidente actual** actually means "the current president." For "real/actual," say: **El verdadero presidente.** |
| Necesito ir a la librería. (meaning "library")            | You just said you need to go to the **bookstore**. For library: **Necesito ir a la biblioteca.**                      |
| No realicé el problema. (meaning "I didn't realize")      | You just said "I didn't carry out the problem." For "realize": **No me di cuenta del problema.**                      |
| Asistí a mi amigo. (meaning "I helped my friend")         | You just said "I attended/accompanied my friend." For "assist/help": **Ayudé a mi amigo.**                            |

These will not cause a scene, but they will make you sound slightly off. Native speakers will understand you but think "that is not quite right."

| -------------- | ----------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **pretender**  | to pretend  | **to try, intend, aim to**  | fingir                       |
| **soportar**   | to support  | **to tolerate, endure**     | apoyar                       |
| **campo**      | camp        | **countryside, field**      | campamento                   |
| **suceso**     | success     | **event, incident**         | éxito                        |
| **sano**       | sane        | **healthy**                 | cuerdo/a                     |
| **simpático**  | sympathetic | **nice, friendly, likable** | compasivo/a                  |
| **disgusto**   | disgust     | **upset, annoyance**        | asco, repugnancia            |
| **compromiso** | compromise  | **commitment, obligation**  | acuerdo                      |
| **lectura**    | lecture     | **reading (the act of)**    | conferencia, clase magistral |
| **pariente**   | parent      | **relative**                | padre/madre                  |
| **desgracia**  | disgrace    | **misfortune**              | deshonra, vergüenza          |
| **quitar**     | to quit     | **to remove, take away**    | dejar, renunciar             |

**Example sentences:**

| Wrong                                                 | Right                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pretendo ser feliz. (meaning "I pretend to be happy") | This means "I aim/try to be happy." For "pretend": **Finjo ser feliz.**                                                |

| Mis parientes viven en Denver. (meaning "my parents") | This means "my relatives." For "parents": **Mis padres viven en Denver.**                                              |

### Why Do False Friends Exist?

Spanish and English both borrowed heavily from Latin (and French, which also comes from Latin). Many words share the same root but evolved differently over centuries:

- **Latin "fabricam"** → English "fabric" (the material) / Spanish "fábrica" (the place where things are made)
- **Latin "libraria"** → English "library" (a place to read) / Spanish "librería" (a place to buy books)
- **Latin "actualis"** → English "actual" (real) / Spanish "actual" (current)
- **Latin "exitus"** → English "exit" (a way out) / Spanish "éxito" (a successful outcome)

The words started in the same place but took different paths. Neither language is "wrong" — they just diverged.

### Scenario: Mi Primer Mes en Valencia — A Story of Mix-Ups

**Papá cuenta sus errores favoritos:**

Cuando llegamos a Valencia, mi español era terrible. La primera semana, le dije a nuestra vecina: "¡Estoy muy excitado de estar aquí!" Quería decir "excited" pero ella puso una cara muy rara. Mi mujer me corrigió después: "Se dice **emocionado**, cariño."

Después, fui a la librería a buscar un libro para los niños. Pero no encontré lo que buscaba. Le pregunté a la dependienta: "¿Dónde están los libros para niños?" Me miró confundida y dijo: "Pues... aquí. Esto es una librería." Yo buscaba la **biblioteca**, no la librería.

Lo mejor fue en el centro de salud. La doctora me preguntó: "¿Tiene alguna alergia?" Y yo respondí: "No, pero estoy un poco constipado." La doctora asintió y me recetó algo para el resfriado. ¡Al menos esa vez acerté sin querer! En español, "constipado" sí significa que tienes un resfriado. Si hubiera querido decir lo que "constipated" significa en inglés, tendría que haber dicho "**estreñido**."

Y un día en el colegio de los niños, le dije a la tutora: "Mi hija es muy sensible." Quería decir "sensible" (prudente, razonable). Pero la tutora pensó que mi hija es muy **sensible** — es decir, que llora fácilmente y se emociona con todo. Para decir "sensible" en inglés, la palabra es **sensata**.

Ahora me río de esos errores. Pero los primeros meses, ¡qué vergüenza!

**False friends used in this story:**

- excitado ≠ excited → emocionado
- librería ≠ library → biblioteca
- constipado ≠ constipated → resfriado (which is what it means!)
- sensible ≠ sensible → sensata

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                                                          | Right                                      | Why                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _Estoy excitado por mi cumpleaños._                            | **Estoy emocionado por mi cumpleaños.**    | "Excitado" has a sexual connotation in Spanish. Use "emocionado" for excited.                                                                         |
| _¿Dónde está la librería más cercana?_ (wanting a library)     | **¿Dónde está la biblioteca más cercana?** | Librería = bookstore. Biblioteca = library.                                                                                                           |
| _La situación actual es buena._ (meaning "the real situation") | **La situación real/verdadera es buena.**  | "Actual" = current, not "real." But note: "La situación actual" (the current situation) is also a perfectly valid sentence — just not what you meant! |
| _No realicé mi error._                                         | **No me di cuenta de mi error.**           | "Realizar" = to carry out. "Darse cuenta de" = to realize.                                                                                            |
| _Necesito asistir a mi hermano._ (meaning "help")              | **Necesito ayudar a mi hermano.**          | "Asistir" = to attend. "Ayudar" = to help/assist.                                                                                                     |

## Cultural Notes

### "No Me Molesta" — The Verb That Confuses Everyone

"Molestar" (to bother/annoy) is perhaps the most anxiety-inducing false friend for English speakers, because the English word it resembles is much more serious. In Spanish, "molestar" is completely innocent:

- "¿Te molesta el ruido?" = Does the noise bother you?
- "No me molestes, estoy trabajando." = Don't bother me, I'm working.
- "¿Le molesta si abro la ventana?" = Do you mind if I open the window?

You will hear "molestar" constantly in Spain. It is a normal, everyday word with no negative connotation. Just remember: it means "to bother," nothing more.

### True Cognates: Your Best Friends

Not all cognates are false! Spanish and English share hundreds of true cognates that mean exactly what you think:

| Spanish      | English              |
| ------------ | -------------------- |
| el hospital  | hospital             |
| el hotel     | hotel                |
| el animal    | animal               |
| la idea      | idea                 |
| el problema  | problem (masculine!) |
| el chocolate | chocolate            |
| la música    | music                |
| el grupo     | group                |
| natural      | natural              |
| importante   | important            |

> **Watch out:** Some true cognates have gender surprises. "El problema" is **masculine** even though it ends in -a. Same with "el sistema," "el tema," "el programa." These come from Greek, not Latin, and follow different rules.

### The "Spanglish" Trap

Living in a bilingual environment, it is easy to start creating hybrid words that are neither English nor Spanish. This is called "Spanglish" and while it is common and understood in many communities, it is worth knowing when you are using a real Spanish word versus a Spanglish invention. False friends are the gateway to Spanglish — once you start inserting English meanings into Spanish words, it becomes a habit. The cure: learn the correct Spanish alternative for each false friend.
