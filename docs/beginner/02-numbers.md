# Spanish Numbers: 0-100

**Level:** Beginner (A1)
**Estimated Time:** 10-15 minutes
**Topics:** Numbers 0-100, combining patterns, gender agreement, real-world usage

## Overview

Numbers are everywhere in daily life -- prices, ages, times, addresses. This lesson teaches you to count from 0 to 100 in Spanish and use numbers in real conversations. You will learn the key patterns that make Spanish numbers logical and predictable once you know the building blocks.

## Learning Objectives

- Count from 0 to 100 in Spanish with correct pronunciation
- Combine tens and ones using the "y" rule (and the special 21-29 pattern)
- Use gender agreement with uno/una in context
- Apply numbers in real-world scenarios: shopping, ages, and telling time

## Lesson Content

### The Foundation: 0-10

These ten numbers are the building blocks for everything else. Memorize them first.

| Number | Spanish | Pronunciation | Memory Trick             |
| ------ | ------- | ------------- | ------------------------ |
| 0      | cero    | SEH-roh       | Like "zero" with accent  |
| 1      | uno     | OO-noh        | Say "uno!" when you win! |
| 2      | dos     | dohs          | Like "dose" of medicine  |
| 3      | tres    | trehs         | Three = tres             |
| 4      | cuatro  | KWAH-troh     | Sounds like "Quattro"    |
| 5      | cinco   | SEEN-koh      | Cinco de Mayo!           |
| 6      | seis    | sayss         | Sounds like "says"       |
| 7      | siete   | see-EH-teh    | Seven = siete (same S!)  |
| 8      | ocho    | OH-choh       | "Oh! CHO-colate!"        |
| 9      | nueve   | NWEH-veh      | "New" + "veh"            |
| 10     | diez    | dee-EHS       | "Diez" days = 10 days    |

**Practice Out Loud:** Count on your fingers forward (uno, dos, tres...) and backward (diez, nueve, ocho...).

### The Tricky Teens: 11-15

These five numbers are unique -- no pattern, just memorize them.

| Number | Spanish | Pronunciation |
| ------ | ------- | ------------- |
| 11     | once    | OHN-seh       |
| 12     | doce    | DOH-seh       |
| 13     | trece   | TREH-seh      |
| 14     | catorce | kah-TOR-seh   |

**Tip:** Notice they all end in "-ce" or "-ce" sounds. That rhythm helps them stick.

### The Pattern Unlocked: 16-19

Now the pattern kicks in: **dieci-** (ten-and) + the ones digit, written as one word.

| Number | Spanish    | Literally     | Pronunciation         |
| ------ | ---------- | ------------- | --------------------- |
| 16     | dieciséis  | ten-and-six   | dee-eh-see-SAYSS      |
| 17     | diecisiete | ten-and-seven | dee-eh-see-see-EH-teh |
| 18     | dieciocho  | ten-and-eight | dee-eh-see-OH-choh    |
| 19     | diecinueve | ten-and-nine  | dee-eh-see-NWEH-veh   |

### The Tens: 20-90

| Number | Spanish   | Pronunciation |
| ------ | --------- | ------------- |
| 20     | veinte    | BAYN-teh      |
| 30     | treinta   | TRAYN-tah     |
| 40     | cuarenta  | kwah-REN-tah  |
| 50     | cincuenta | seen-KWEN-tah |
| 60     | sesenta   | seh-SEN-tah   |
| 70     | setenta   | seh-TEN-tah   |
| 80     | ochenta   | oh-CHEN-tah   |
| 90     | noventa   | noh-BEN-tah   |

**Pattern:** 30-90 sound like their root number + "-enta":
TREinta (TREs), CUARenta (CUATro), CINCUenta (CINCo), SESenta (SEis), SETenta (SIete), OCHenta (OCHo), NOVenta (NUEve).

### The "Y" Rule: Combining Tens and Ones (21-99)

This is the key rule for building any number from 21 to 99.

**21-29 are special** -- they are written as one word using "veinti-":

| Number | Spanish      |
| ------ | ------------ |
| 21     | veintiuno    |
| 22     | veintidós    |
| 23     | veintitrés   |
| 24     | veinticuatro |
| 25     | veinticinco  |
| 26     | veintiséis   |
| 27     | veintisiete  |
| 28     | veintiocho   |
| 29     | veintinueve  |

**31-99 use "y"** (and) between the tens and ones -- three separate words:

- 31 = treinta **y** uno
- 45 = cuarenta **y** cinco
- 55 = cincuenta **y** cinco
- 68 = sesenta **y** ocho
- 78 = setenta **y** ocho
- 97 = noventa **y** siete

**Your Turn:** How would you say 34? 56? 83? (Answers: treinta y cuatro, cincuenta y seis, ochenta y tres.)

### Gender Agreement with UNO

When "one" comes before a noun, it changes to match gender:

- **un** + masculine noun: _un libro_ (one book), _veintiún años_ (21 years)
- **una** + feminine noun: _una casa_ (one house), _veintiuna personas_ (21 people)
- _Hay treinta y un días en enero_ = There are 31 days in January
- _Hay treinta y una personas_ = There are 31 people

This applies whenever "uno" appears in a number: 1, 21, 31, 41, etc.

---

### A Note on 100: Cien vs. Ciento

You will encounter 100 in prices and quantities, so know this rule:

- **100 exactly** = **cien** (_cien euros_ = 100 euros)
- **101-199** = **ciento** + the rest (_ciento cinco_ = 105, _ciento veintitrés_ = 123)

That is all you need for now -- hundreds in depth come in a later lesson.

### Valencia Scenario: Shopping at Mercado Central

Imagine you are at the market buying groceries. Here are phrases you will use:

- _¿Cuánto cuesta?_ = How much does it cost?
- _Quiero cinco naranjas_ = I want five oranges
- _Necesito ocho tomates_ = I need eight tomatoes
- _Compro dos barras de pan_ = I am buying two loaves of bread
- _Cuesta quince euros_ = It costs fifteen euros
- _El queso cuesta doce euros_ = The cheese costs twelve euros
- _Las manzanas cuestan tres euros el kilo_ = Apples cost three euros per kilo
- _Tengo veinte euros en mi cartera_ = I have twenty euros in my wallet

**Your Turn -- Complete These:**

- Quiero **\_\_\_** plátanos (7 bananas)
- Necesito **\_\_\_** huevos (12 eggs)
- Cuesta **\_\_\_** euros (25 euros)
- Hay **\_\_\_** personas en la cola (15 people in line)

### Valencia Scenario: Talking About Ages

In Spanish, you "have" years rather than "are" years old: _Tengo \_\_ años._

- _¿Cuántos años tienes?_ = How old are you?
- _Tengo cuarenta y dos años_ = I am 42 years old
- _Mi hija tiene siete años_ = My daughter is 7 years old
- _Somos cuatro personas en mi familia_ = We are 4 people in my family

**Your Turn -- Complete These:**

- Tengo **\_\_\_** años (your age)
- Mi hija mayor tiene **\_\_\_** años
- Mi hija menor tiene **\_\_\_** años
- Somos **\_\_\_** personas en mi familia

### Valencia Scenario: Telling Time

Time uses numbers constantly. Here are the basics:

- _¿Qué hora es?_ = What time is it?
- _Son las ocho_ = It is 8 o'clock (use _son las_ for 2-12)
- _Es la una_ = It is 1 o'clock (use _es la_ for 1 -- singular!)
- _Son las tres y media_ = It is 3:30 (_y media_ = and a half)
- _Son las nueve y cuarto_ = It is 9:15 (_y cuarto_ = and a quarter)
- _Es la una menos cuarto_ = It is 12:45 (_menos cuarto_ = minus a quarter)
- _Son las siete y diez_ = It is 7:10

**Your Turn -- What time do you:**

- Wake up? _Me despierto a las_ **\_\_\_**
- Eat lunch? _Como a las_ **\_\_\_**
- Go to bed? _Me acuesto a las_ **\_\_\_**

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                      | Right            | Why                                       |
| -------------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| _veinte y tres_            | **veintitrés**   | 21-29 are always one word (veinti-)       |
| _diez y seis_              | **dieciséis**    | 16-19 are always one word (dieci-)        |
| _ciento_ (for exactly 100) | **cien**         | Use "cien" when the number is exactly 100 |
| _cien cinco_               | **ciento cinco** | Use "ciento" for 101-199                  |
| _un mil_                   | **mil**          | Never put "uno" or "un" before mil        |
