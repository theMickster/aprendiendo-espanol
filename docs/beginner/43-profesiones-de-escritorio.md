# Profesiones de Escritorio

**Level:** Beginner (A2)
**Estimated Time:** 25 minutes
**Topics:** Office and knowledge worker professions, profession-specific verbs, describing daily work routines, obligations with "tener que"

## Overview

Not everyone works with their hands or on their feet. A huge number of people sit at a desk — writing, coding, calculating, planning, and arguing (professionally, of course). This lesson covers the professions of the office: programmers, writers, lawyers, accountants, project managers, and human resources professionals. For each one, you will learn not just the title but the **verbs they use every day** — what they actually do. By the end, you can describe someone's desk job or ask them about their typical workday.

> **Prerequisite:** [Lesson 42 - ¿A Qué Te Dedicas?](42-a-que-te-dedicas.md)

## Learning Objectives

- Name six common office/knowledge worker professions and their key verbs
- Describe what someone does at their job using present tense
- Use "tener que + infinitive" and "se encarga de + infinitive" for responsibilities
- Have a basic conversation with someone about their desk job
- Understand Spain-specific terms (contable, bufete, RRHH)

## Lesson Content

### The Professions and What They Do

Each profession below comes with the verbs that define it. These are the words you need to actually talk about the job — not just label it.

#### El/La Programador/a (Programmer)

| Spanish                    | English           |
| -------------------------- | ----------------- |
| **programar**              | to program        |
| **escribir código**        | to write code     |
| **depurar**                | to debug          |
| **diseñar**                | to design         |
| **desarrollar**            | to develop        |
| **probar**                 | to test           |
| **el software**            | software          |
| **la aplicación / la app** | application / app |
| **la base de datos**       | database          |
| **el error / el bug**      | error / bug       |

**A day in the life:**
Un programador escribe código todos los días. Tiene que depurar errores y probar el software. Diseña aplicaciones y desarrolla nuevas funciones. A veces trabaja solo, a veces trabaja en equipo.

| ----------------- | ----------------------- |

| **investigar**    | to investigate/research |

Un abogado investiga casos y asesora a sus clientes. Tiene que argumentar en el tribunal y negociar acuerdos. Representa a personas y empresas. Trabaja en un bufete y tiene muchas reuniones.

> **Spain vs. Latin America:** In Spain, a law firm is called "un bufete" or "un despacho de abogados." In Latin America, you might hear "una firma de abogados."

| Spanish                | English         |
| ---------------------- | --------------- |
| **calcular**           | to calculate    |
| **revisar**            | to review/check |
| **auditar**            | to audit        |
| **declarar impuestos** | to file taxes   |
| **analizar**           | to analyze      |
| **organizar**          | to organize     |
| **las cuentas**        | accounts        |

| **la factura**         | invoice         |
| **el balance**         | balance sheet   |
| **los gastos**         | expenses        |

**A day in the life:**
Un contable revisa las cuentas de una empresa. Calcula los impuestos y analiza los gastos. Tiene que organizar las facturas y preparar el balance. En España, muchos autónomos necesitan un contable para declarar impuestos cada trimestre.

> **Spain vs. Latin America:** In Spain, "accountant" is **el/la contable**. In Latin America, it is **el/la contador/a**. Both are understood everywhere.

| ----------------------- | --------------------- |

| **capacitar**           | to train (employees)  |
| **gestionar**           | to manage/handle      |
| **resolver conflictos** | to resolve conflicts  |

**A day in the life:**

> **RRHH:** The abbreviation doubles the letters because it stands for "Recursos Humanos" — both words are plural. You will see this written as RRHH, RR.HH., or just "Recursos Humanos."

### Grammar Spotlight: Talking About Responsibilities

#### "Tener que + infinitive" (To have to...)

Use this to describe obligations:

| Spanish                                            | English                              |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Un abogado **tiene que** investigar mucho.         | A lawyer has to research a lot.      |
| Los programadores **tienen que** probar su código. | Programmers have to test their code. |
| **Tengo que** terminar este informe.               | I have to finish this report.        |

#### "Se encarga de + infinitive" (Is in charge of...)

Use this to describe specific responsibilities:

| Spanish                                      | English                                       |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Se encarga de** coordinar el equipo.       | He/she is in charge of coordinating the team. |
| **Me encargo de** las entrevistas.           | I am in charge of interviews.                 |
| El contable **se encarga de** los impuestos. | The accountant is in charge of taxes.         |

#### "Su trabajo consiste en + infinitive" (Their job consists of...)

A more formal way to describe what someone does:

| Spanish                                             | English                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Su trabajo consiste en** escribir código.         | Their job consists of writing code.      |
| **Mi trabajo consiste en** defender a mis clientes. | My job consists of defending my clients. |

### Scenario: Chatting at a Coworking Space in Ruzafa

Ruzafa is a trendy neighborhood in Valencia full of cafés, coworking spaces, and freelancers.

**Papá:** Hola, ¿está ocupado este sitio?

**Carlos:** No, no. Siéntate. Soy Carlos.

**Papá:** Mick. Encantado. ¿Vienes mucho por aquí?

**Carlos:** Sí, trabajo aquí casi todos los días. Soy programador freelance.

**Papá:** ¡Yo también soy programador! ¿En qué lenguajes programas?

**Carlos:** Sobre todo Python y JavaScript. ¿Y tú?

**Papá:** Yo más en el lado de gestión ahora — soy gerente de proyecto. Pero antes programaba mucho.

**Carlos:** Ah, entonces te encargas de organizar al equipo.

**Papá:** Exacto. Planificar, coordinar, supervisar... y muchas reuniones. Demasiadas reuniones.

**Carlos:** _(ríe)_ Esa es la parte que no echo de menos. Prefiero escribir código tranquilamente.

### Scenario: Asking a Lawyer Friend About Her Day

**Mamá:** Oye, Ana, ¿cómo es un día típico para ti en el bufete?

**Ana:** Pues mira, llego a las nueve y reviso los correos. Normalmente tengo una o dos reuniones por la mañana con clientes.

**Mamá:** ¿Y después?

**Ana:** Por la tarde me dedico a investigar casos y preparar argumentos. A veces tengo que ir al tribunal.

**Mamá:** ¿Vas al tribunal muchas veces?

**Ana:** Depende. Algunas semanas, tres o cuatro veces. Otras, ninguna. Lo más difícil es negociar con la otra parte.

**Mamá:** Suena intenso.

**Ana:** Sí, pero me encanta. Cada día es diferente.

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong                                     | Right                                             | Why                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| _Él es un contable._                      | **Él es contable.**                               | No article with "ser + profession" (review from L42)                           |
| _Se encarga de coordina el equipo._       | **Se encarga de coordinar el equipo.**            | After "de," use the infinitive, not a conjugated verb                          |
| _Tiene que investiga._                    | **Tiene que investigar.**                         | After "tener que," always use the infinitive                                   |
| _Ella es un abogado._                     | **Ella es abogada.**                              | Profession must agree with the person's gender (and no article)                |
| _Trabajo en RRHH. Hago recursos humanas._ | **Trabajo en RRHH. Trabajo en recursos humanos.** | RRHH stands for Recursos Humanos (masculine plural) — do not change the gender |

## Cultural Notes

### The Rise of Remote Work and Coworking in Valencia

Valencia has become one of Europe's top destinations for remote workers and digital nomads. The Ruzafa neighborhood alone has over a dozen coworking spaces. If you work in tech, design, or writing, you will find a thriving community of "nómadas digitales" (digital nomads) and "autónomos" (freelancers) who work from cafés and shared offices.

Useful vocabulary for this world:

| Spanish                          | English                              |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| el espacio de coworking          | coworking space                      |
| trabajar en remoto / a distancia | to work remotely                     |
| el nómada digital                | digital nomad                        |
| el/la autónomo/a                 | freelancer/self-employed             |
| la tarifa plana                  | flat rate (for coworking membership) |

### "Funcionario" — The Coveted Government Job

In Spain, being a "funcionario/a" (government employee) is a highly desirable career path. Funcionarios enjoy job security, good benefits, and a stable schedule. To become one, you must pass "oposiciones" — extremely competitive public exams. Many Spaniards spend years studying for oposiciones in fields like teaching, law, medicine, and administration. If someone tells you "Estoy opositando," they are dedicating their life to this goal.
