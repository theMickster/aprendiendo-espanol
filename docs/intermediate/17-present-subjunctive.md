# 🌀 The Present Subjunctive (El Presente de Subjuntivo)

## Welcome to the Subjunctive -- Where Spanish Gets Really Interesting!

**⏱️ Study Time: 15-20 minutes**
**🎯 Goal: Understand when and how to use the subjunctive mood for wishes, doubts, emotions, and recommendations!**

---

## Overview

The subjunctive is not a tense -- it's a **mood**. While the indicative mood states facts and certainties ("I eat," "She goes"), the subjunctive expresses the world of wishes, doubts, emotions, and unreality ("I want you **to eat**," "I doubt she **goes**").

This is often called the hardest part of Spanish for English speakers, but don't be intimidated. You already understand the concept -- you just need to learn when Spanish requires it.

| Indicative (facts)          | Subjunctive (non-facts)           |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Sé que** hablas español   | **Quiero que** habl**es** español |
| I know you speak Spanish    | I want you to speak Spanish       |
| **Es verdad que** llueve    | **Ojalá que** lluev**a**          |
| It's true that it's raining | I hope it rains                   |

---

## 1️⃣ Formation: The "Opposite Vowel" Rule

Start with the **yo** form of the present indicative. Drop the -o. Then add the **opposite vowel** endings.

### -ar verbs → use -e endings

**hablar:** yo habl**o** → habl- + subjunctive endings

| Person              | Subjunctive | English              |
| ------------------- | ----------- | -------------------- |
| Yo                  | hable       | (that) I speak       |
| Tú                  | hables      | (that) you speak     |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | hable       | (that) he/she speaks |
| Nosotros/as         | hablemos    | (that) we speak      |
| Vosotros/as         | habléis     | (that) you all speak |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | hablen      | (that) they speak    |

### -er/-ir verbs → use -a endings

**comer:** yo com**o** → com- + subjunctive endings

| Person              | Subjunctive | English            |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ------------------ |
| Yo                  | coma        | (that) I eat       |
| Tú                  | comas       | (that) you eat     |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | coma        | (that) he/she eats |
| Nosotros/as         | comamos     | (that) we eat      |
| Vosotros/as         | comáis      | (that) you all eat |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | coman       | (that) they eat    |

**vivir:** yo viv**o** → viv- + subjunctive endings

| Person              | Subjunctive | English             |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ------------------- |
| Yo                  | viva        | (that) I live       |
| Tú                  | vivas       | (that) you live     |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | viva        | (that) he/she lives |
| Nosotros/as         | vivamos     | (that) we live      |
| Vosotros/as         | viváis      | (that) you all live |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | vivan       | (that) they live    |

**The pattern:** -ar verbs get -e endings, -er/-ir verbs get -a endings. Vowels swap!

## 2️⃣ Irregular Subjunctives

These common verbs have irregular subjunctive forms that must be memorized:

| Infinitive | Subjunctive (yo, tú, él, nosotros, vosotros, ellos) |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| ser        | sea, seas, sea, seamos, seáis, sean                 |
| estar      | esté, estés, esté, estemos, estéis, estén           |
| haber      | haya, hayas, haya, hayamos, hayáis, hayan           |
| ir         | vaya, vayas, vaya, vayamos, vayáis, vayan           |
| saber      | sepa, sepas, sepa, sepamos, sepáis, sepan           |
| dar        | dé, des, dé, demos, deis, den                       |
| ver        | vea, veas, vea, veamos, veáis, vean                 |

### Stem-Changing Subjunctives

These follow the "opposite vowel" rule but keep their stem changes:

| Infinitive | Subjunctive (yo) | Pattern |
| ---------- | ---------------- | ------- |
| querer     | quiera           | e → ie  |
| poder      | pueda            | o → ue  |
| sentir     | sienta           | e → ie  |
| dormir     | duerma           | o → ue  |
| pedir      | pida             | e → i   |
| pensar     | piense           | e → ie  |

## 3️⃣ When to Use: The WEIRDO Triggers

The subjunctive appears after certain triggers. A helpful mnemonic is **WEIRDO**:

### **W** -- Wishes and Desires

1. **Quiero que vengas a cenar**

   - (I want you to come to dinner)

2. **Espero que aprendas mucho**

   - (I hope you learn a lot)

3. **Deseo que seáis felices**
   - (I wish you all to be happy)

### **E** -- Emotions

4. **Me alegra que estés aquí**

   - (I'm glad you're here)

5. **Siento que no puedas venir**

   - (I'm sorry you can't come)

6. **Me sorprende que hable tan bien**
   - (It surprises me that he/she speaks so well)

### **I** -- Impersonal Expressions

7. **Es importante que estudies cada día**

   - (It's important that you study every day)

8. **Es necesario que hagamos la compra**

   - (It's necessary that we do the shopping)

9. **Es mejor que vayas al médico**
   - (It's better that you go to the doctor)

### **R** -- Recommendations and Requests

10. **Te recomiendo que pruebes la paella valenciana**

    - (I recommend you try Valencian paella)

11. **Te pido que me ayudes**

    - (I ask you to help me)

12. **Sugiero que salgamos temprano**
    - (I suggest we leave early)

### **D** -- Doubt and Denial

13. **Dudo que llegue a tiempo**

    - (I doubt he'll arrive on time)

14. **No creo que sea verdad**

    - (I don't think it's true)

15. **Es posible que llueva mañana**
    - (It's possible it will rain tomorrow)

### **O** -- Ojalá

16. **Ojalá que haga buen tiempo para las Fallas**

    - (I hope the weather is good for Las Fallas)

17. **Ojalá que aprobemos el examen**

    - (I hope we pass the exam)

18. **¡Ojalá que podamos ir a la playa!**
    - (I hope we can go to the beach!)

**Cultural note:** "Ojalá" comes from the Arabic "inshallah" (God willing), a reminder of Spain's Moorish history. It's one of the strongest subjunctive triggers in Spanish -- it ALWAYS takes the subjunctive.

---

## 4️⃣ The Key Rule: Two Subjects + QUE

The subjunctive typically requires:

1. **Two different subjects** (I want... YOU to go)
2. Connected by **que**

| Same subject (no subjunctive)        | Different subjects (subjunctive!)          |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Quiero ir** (I want to go)         | **Quiero que vayas** (I want you to go)    |
| **Espero aprobar** (I hope to pass)  | **Espero que apruebes** (I hope you pass)  |
| **Prefiero comer** (I prefer to eat) | **Prefiero que comamos** (I prefer we eat) |

When the subject is the same, just use the infinitive. When it changes, you need que + subjunctive.

---

## 5️⃣ When NOT to Use the Subjunctive

Expressions of **certainty** use the indicative (NOT subjunctive):

| Certainty (indicative)        | Doubt/Negation (subjunctive)     |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| **Creo que es** verdad        | **No creo que sea** verdad       |
| **Es verdad que tiene** razón | **No es verdad que tenga** razón |
| **Es obvio que sabe** español | **No es obvio que sepa** español |
| **Estoy seguro de que viene** | **No estoy seguro de que venga** |

19. **Creo que habla bien** (I think he speaks well -- certainty, indicative)
20. **No creo que hable bien** (I don't think he speaks well -- doubt, subjunctive!)

## 🏖️ Valencia Life Examples

21. **Espero que os guste vivir en Valencia**

    - (I hope you like living in Valencia)

22. **Es importante que los niños aprendan español en el colegio**

    - (It's important that the kids learn Spanish at school)

23. **Quiero que probemos todos los restaurantes del Carmen**

    - (I want us to try all the restaurants in El Carmen)

24. **Ojalá que no llueva durante las Fallas**

    - (I hope it doesn't rain during Las Fallas)

25. **Dudo que encontremos aparcamiento en el centro**
    - (I doubt we'll find parking in the center)

## 💡 Don't Panic!

The subjunctive takes time to internalize. Here's a practical approach:

1. **Start by recognizing it** -- listen for it in conversations in Valencia
2. **Memorize the WEIRDO triggers** -- they cover 90% of subjunctive uses
3. **Practice the most common phrases** -- "Quiero que...", "Espero que...", "Es importante que..."
4. **Don't overthink it** -- even if you use the indicative by mistake, people will understand you
