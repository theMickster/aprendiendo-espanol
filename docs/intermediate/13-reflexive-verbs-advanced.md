# 🪞 Advanced Reflexive Verbs - Beyond Daily Routines

## Emotions, Reciprocals, and Meaning-Changing Reflexives

**⏱️ Study Time: 10-15 minutes**
**🎯 Goal: Master reflexive verbs for emotions, reciprocal actions, and verbs that change meaning with SE!**

---

## 📖 Overview

In beginner lesson 15 you learned reflexive verbs for daily routines — despertarse, ducharse, vestirse. Now it's time to go deeper. Spanish reflexive verbs do much more than describe your morning routine. They express emotions, describe how people interact with each other, and even completely change the meaning of common verbs. This lesson covers three big categories:

1. **Emotional and psychological reflexives** — how you feel
2. **Reciprocal verbs** — actions people do to each other
3. **Meaning-changing verbs** — verbs that transform when you add SE

---

## 😤 Emotional and Psychological Reflexive Verbs

These verbs describe feelings and mental states. The reflexive pronoun shows that the emotion happens **inside** the subject.

### Key Verbs

| Infinitivo      | Meaning          | Example                         |
| --------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------- |
| preocuparse     | to worry         | Me preocupo por los exámenes.   |
| enfadarse       | to get angry     | Se enfada cuando llego tarde.   |
| quejarse        | to complain      | Nos quejamos del calor.         |
| sentirse        | to feel          | Me siento feliz en Valencia.    |
| darse cuenta de | to realize       | Me doy cuenta del error.        |
| acordarse de    | to remember      | ¿Te acuerdas de aquella fiesta? |
| alegrarse de    | to be glad about | Nos alegramos de la noticia.    |
| arrepentirse de | to regret        | Me arrepiento de no haber ido.  |

### 🔄 Conjugation: preocuparse (to worry)

| Person              | Spanish         | English                   |
| ------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------- |
| Yo                  | me preocupo     | I worry                   |
| Tú                  | te preocupas    | you worry                 |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | se preocupa     | he/she worries, you worry |
| Nosotros/as         | nos preocupamos | we worry                  |
| Vosotros/as         | os preocupáis   | you all worry             |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | se preocupan    | they/you all worry        |

### 🔄 Conjugation: sentirse (to feel) — stem change e → ie

| Person              | Spanish      | English                |
| ------------------- | ------------ | ---------------------- |
| Yo                  | me siento    | I feel                 |
| Tú                  | te sientes   | you feel               |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | se siente    | he/she feels, you feel |
| Nosotros/as         | nos sentimos | we feel                |
| Vosotros/as         | os sentís    | you all feel           |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | se sienten   | they/you all feel      |

### 📖 Example Sentences

1. **Me preocupo mucho por mis hijas cuando salen solas**

   - (I worry a lot about my daughters when they go out alone)

2. **Papá se enfada cuando dejamos los platos sucios**

   - (Dad gets angry when we leave dirty dishes)

3. **Los vecinos siempre se quejan del ruido de las Fallas**

   - (The neighbors always complain about the noise from Fallas)

4. **Nos sentimos muy cómodos viviendo en Valencia**

   - (We feel very comfortable living in Valencia)

5. **¿Te das cuenta de cuánto español hablas ya?**

   - (Do you realize how much Spanish you speak already?)

6. **¿Os acordáis de nuestro primer día en España?**

   - (Do you remember our first day in Spain?)

7. **Me alegro de que haga buen tiempo hoy**

   - (I'm glad the weather is nice today)

8. **Se arrepiente de no haber visitado el Oceanogràfic antes**
   - (She regrets not having visited the Oceanogràfic sooner)

## 🤝 Reciprocal Verbs

When two or more people do something **to each other**, Spanish uses the same reflexive pronouns — but only the plural forms: **nos**, **os**, **se**.

### How It Works

| Pronoun | Meaning                |
| ------- | ---------------------- |
| nos     | we ... each other      |
| os      | you all ... each other |
| se      | they ... each other    |

### 📖 Example Sentences

1. **Nos vemos todos los días en el colegio**

   - (We see each other every day at school)

2. **Se ayudan con los deberes**

   - (They help each other with homework)

3. **Se escriben mensajes constantemente**

   - (They write to each other constantly)

4. **Nos queremos mucho en esta familia**

   - (We love each other a lot in this family)

5. **¿Os llamáis por teléfono o por WhatsApp?**

   - (Do you call each other by phone or WhatsApp?)

6. **Se conocieron en Valencia hace tres años**
   - (They met each other in Valencia three years ago)

### 🤔 Reciprocal or Reflexive? How to Tell the Difference

Sometimes the same sentence could mean "themselves" (reflexive) or "each other" (reciprocal). To clarify, add:

- **el uno al otro / la una a la otra** — each other (two people)
- **los unos a los otros / las unas a las otras** — each other (groups)
- **mutuamente** — mutually / each other

| Ambiguous                                        | Clarified Reciprocal                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Se miran. (They look at themselves? each other?) | Se miran **el uno al otro**. (They look at each other.) |
| Nos ayudamos. (We help ourselves? each other?)   | Nos ayudamos **mutuamente**. (We help each other.)      |

## 🔀 Meaning Changes with SE

This is one of the trickiest parts of Spanish. Many common verbs **completely change meaning** when you add a reflexive pronoun. You already know some of these — now let's study them systematically.

### The Big Table

| Verb    | Meaning                     | Reflexive         | Meaning                 |
| ------- | --------------------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------- |
| ir      | to go                       | irse              | to leave / go away      |
| dormir  | to sleep                    | dormirse          | to fall asleep          |
| poner   | to put                      | ponerse           | to put on / to become   |
| quedar  | to remain / arrange to meet | quedarse          | to stay                 |
| llamar  | to call                     | llamarse          | to be called / named    |
| parecer | to seem                     | parecerse a       | to look like / resemble |
| llevar  | to carry / wear             | llevarse bien/mal | to get along well/badly |

### 📖 Side-by-Side Examples

**ir vs irse**

1. **Voy al cine esta noche** — (I'm going to the cinema tonight)
2. **Me voy, ¡hasta luego!** — (I'm leaving, see you later!)

**dormir vs dormirse**

3. **Duermo ocho horas cada noche** — (I sleep eight hours every night)
4. **Me dormí en el sofá viendo la tele** — (I fell asleep on the sofa watching TV)

**poner vs ponerse**

5. **Pongo la mesa para cenar** — (I set the table for dinner)
6. **Me pongo nervioso antes de los exámenes** — (I get nervous before exams)
7. **Me pongo la chaqueta porque hace frío** — (I put on my jacket because it's cold)

**quedar vs quedarse**

8. **Quedo con amigos en la plaza** — (I'm meeting friends at the plaza)
9. **Me quedo en casa este fin de semana** — (I'm staying home this weekend)

**llamar vs llamarse**

10. **Llamo a mi madre todos los domingos** — (I call my mother every Sunday)
11. **Me llamo Miguel, ¿y tú?** — (My name is Miguel, and yours?)

**parecer vs parecerse a**

12. **Parece fácil, pero no lo es** — (It seems easy, but it isn't)
13. **Se parece mucho a su padre** — (She looks a lot like her father)

**llevar vs llevarse bien/mal**

14. **Llevo una mochila al colegio** — (I carry a backpack to school)
15. **Nos llevamos muy bien con los vecinos** — (We get along very well with the neighbors)

## 🧩 Reflexive + Object Pronoun Stacking

When a reflexive verb also has a direct object pronoun, both pronouns go together. The order is always:

**Reflexive pronoun FIRST, then object pronoun**

> **me / te / se / nos / os / se** + **lo / la / los / las**

### Examples

1. **Me pongo la chaqueta** → **Me la pongo**

   - (I put on the jacket → I put it on)

2. **Se quita los zapatos** → **Se los quita**

   - (She takes off the shoes → She takes them off)

3. **Nos lavamos las manos** → **Nos las lavamos**
   - (We wash our hands → We wash them)

**⚠️ Common Mistake:** Don't reverse the order! "Lo me pongo" is incorrect — it must be "Me lo pongo."

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

| #   | Wrong                                        | Right                        | Why                                                                     |
| --- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Voy. (meaning "I'm leaving")                 | **Me** voy.                  | Without the pronoun, it means "I'm going (somewhere)" not "I'm leaving" |
| 2   | Duermo en el sofá. (meaning "I fell asleep") | **Me** dormí en el sofá.     | Dormir = to sleep; dormirse = to fall asleep                            |
| 3   | Quedo en casa.                               | **Me** quedo en casa.        | Quedar = to arrange to meet; quedarse = to stay                         |
| 4   | Lo me pongo.                                 | **Me lo** pongo.             | Reflexive pronoun always comes first                                    |
| 5   | Se miran. (wanting "each other")             | Se miran **el uno al otro**. | Add clarifier when reciprocal meaning isn't obvious                     |
| 6   | Me acuerdo la fecha.                         | Me acuerdo **de** la fecha.  | Acordarse requires the preposition DE                                   |

## 🌟 Cultural Note: Emotions in Spain

Spaniards are generally very expressive with emotions. Don't be surprised if your neighbors in Valencia se quejan del calor loudly in summer, se enfadan visibly in traffic, or se alegran with big hugs when they see you. The reflexive verbs for emotions get a real workout in daily Spanish life. Embrace it!
