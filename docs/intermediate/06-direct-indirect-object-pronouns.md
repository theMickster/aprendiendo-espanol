# 🔀 Direct & Indirect Object Pronouns

## Stop Repeating Yourself: Let Pronouns Do the Work!

**⏱️ Study Time: 15-20 minutes**
**🎯 Goal: Replace nouns with object pronouns and combine them naturally!**

---

## Overview

In English, we say "I see **him**" instead of "I see **Carlos**" to avoid repetition. Spanish does the same thing with object pronouns -- but they go in different places than in English. This is one of the biggest leaps toward sounding natural in Spanish.

---

## 1️⃣ Direct Object Pronouns (Pronombres de Objeto Directo)

The direct object is the thing or person directly receiving the action. "I see **the dog**" → "I see **it**."

| Pronoun | Replaces                                | Example                        |
| ------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| me      | me (a mí)                               | Me ves (You see me)            |
| te      | you (a ti)                              | Te llamo (I call you)          |
| lo      | him / it (masc.) / you (usted, masc.)   | Lo veo (I see him/it)          |
| la      | her / it (fem.) / you (usted, fem.)     | La conozco (I know her)        |
| nos     | us (a nosotros)                         | Nos invitan (They invite us)   |
| os      | you all (a vosotros)                    | Os espero (I wait for you all) |
| los     | them (masc.) / you all (ustedes, masc.) | Los compro (I buy them)        |
| las     | them (fem.) / you all (ustedes, fem.)   | Las quiero (I want them)       |

---

## 2️⃣ Indirect Object Pronouns (Pronombres de Objeto Indirecto)

The indirect object is the person who receives the benefit or result of the action. "I give the book **to María**" → "I give **her** the book."

| Pronoun | Meaning                         | Example                                               |
| ------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| me      | to/for me                       | Me dan un regalo (They give me a gift)                |
| te      | to/for you                      | Te escribo una carta (I write you a letter)           |
| le      | to/for him/her/you (usted)      | Le digo la verdad (I tell him/her the truth)          |
| nos     | to/for us                       | Nos traen comida (They bring us food)                 |
| os      | to/for you all (vosotros)       | Os mando un mensaje (I send you all a message)        |
| les     | to/for them / you all (ustedes) | Les explico la lección (I explain the lesson to them) |

---

## 3️⃣ Where Do They Go? Placement Rules

### Before a conjugated verb

This is the most common placement:

1. **Lo veo cada día** (I see him every day)
2. **Te quiero mucho** (I love you a lot)
3. **Le doy el libro** (I give him/her the book)
4. **No los conozco** (I don't know them)

### Attached to an infinitive (two options -- both correct!)

5. **Voy a verlo** = **Lo voy a ver** (I'm going to see him)
6. **Quiero comprarte algo** = **Te quiero comprar algo** (I want to buy you something)
7. **Necesito decirle algo** = **Le necesito decir algo** (I need to tell him/her something)

### Attached to a gerund (two options -- both correct!)

8. **Estoy haciéndolo** = **Lo estoy haciendo** (I'm doing it)
9. **Estoy escribiéndote** = **Te estoy escribiendo** (I'm writing to you)

**⚠️ Note:** When attaching to a gerund, add an accent mark to keep the stress: haciéndo**lo**, escribiéndo**te**.

### Attached to affirmative commands (only one option!)

10. **¡Dímelo!** (Tell it to me!)
11. **¡Cómpralo!** (Buy it!)
12. **¡Escríbele!** (Write to him/her!)

**⚠️ But** with negative commands, the pronoun goes BEFORE the verb:

- **¡No me lo digas!** (Don't tell it to me!)
- **¡No lo compres!** (Don't buy it!)

---

## 4️⃣ Double Object Pronouns

When you have BOTH an indirect and direct object pronoun, the indirect goes first:

**Indirect + Direct + Verb**

1. **Me lo das** (You give it to me)
2. **Te la mando** (I send it to you)
3. **Nos los traen** (They bring them to us)

### The LE → SE Rule

When **le** or **les** comes before **lo/la/los/las**, it changes to **se**:

| You might expect... | But it becomes... | Meaning              |
| ------------------- | ----------------- | -------------------- |
| ~~Le lo doy~~       | **Se lo doy**     | I give it to him/her |
| ~~Le la mando~~     | **Se la mando**   | I send it to him/her |
| ~~Les los doy~~     | **Se los doy**    | I give them to them  |
| ~~Les las mando~~   | **Se las mando**  | I send them to them  |

4. **¿El libro? Se lo doy a María** (The book? I give it to María)
5. **¿Las llaves? Se las dejo en la mesa** (The keys? I leave them on the table for him/her)
6. **¿Los deberes? Se los mando mañana** (The homework? I send it to them tomorrow)

---

## 5️⃣ Redundant "Le" -- It's Normal!

In Spanish, it's completely normal (and often required) to use the indirect object pronoun EVEN when the full noun is present:

7. **Le doy el libro a mi madre** (I give the book to my mother)
8. **Les digo la verdad a mis hijas** (I tell my daughters the truth)
9. **Le compro un regalo a mi esposa** (I buy my wife a gift)

This feels redundant in English ("I give the book to her to my mother") but in Spanish it sounds natural and is expected. Don't skip it!

## 🏖️ Valencia Daily Life Examples

### At a restaurant:

10. **¿Me trae la cuenta, por favor?** (Can you bring me the check, please?)
11. **¿Nos pone dos cañas?** (Can you get us two beers?)
12. **Se lo recomiendo** -- pointing at a dish (I recommend it to you)

### At a tienda:

13. **¿Me lo puede envolver?** (Can you wrap it for me?)
14. **Se las llevo** -- about some oranges (I'll take them)
15. **¿Me da una bolsa?** (Can you give me a bag?)

### With the family:

16. **¿Te lo paso?** -- about the salt (Should I pass it to you?)
17. **Se lo dije a los niños** (I told it to the kids)
18. **Nos lo explicó la profesora** (The teacher explained it to us)

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

| Wrong                        | Right                          | Why                                                     |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| ~~Yo veo lo~~                | **Lo veo** or **Voy a verlo**  | Pronouns go BEFORE conjugated verbs                     |
| ~~Le veo~~ (for "I see him") | **Lo veo**                     | "Le" is indirect; seeing someone uses the direct object |
| ~~Le lo digo~~               | **Se lo digo**                 | le + lo → se lo                                         |
| ~~Digo te lo~~               | **Te lo digo** or **¡Dímelo!** | Pronouns before conjugated verb, or attached to command |
| ~~No dímelo~~                | **¡No me lo digas!**           | Negative commands: pronoun goes BEFORE the verb         |

**Lo vs Le confusion:** In much of Spain (including Valencia), you'll hear "le" used for masculine people instead of "lo" -- this is called **leísmo** and is accepted. But the standard rule is: **lo/la** = direct object, **le** = indirect object.
