# 🎯 Adjective Placement and Forms

## Position Matters: Where You Put the Adjective Changes Everything!

**Level:** Intermediate (B1)
**Estimated Time:** 10-15 minutes
**Topics:** Adjective placement, apocopation, meaning changes by position

---

## Overview

You already know that Spanish adjectives usually go after the noun. But that's only half the story -- some adjectives go before the noun, some shorten when they do, and for a handful of adjectives, moving them before or after the noun completely changes the meaning. This lesson covers the rules you need to sound natural.

## Learning Objectives

- Know which adjectives go before and which go after the noun
- Apply apocopation rules (shortened adjective forms)
- Recognize adjectives whose meaning changes based on position
- Order multiple adjectives correctly

---

## 📏 The Default Rule: Descriptive Adjectives Go AFTER

In Spanish, most adjectives that describe qualities (color, size, shape, personality) go **after** the noun. This is the opposite of English.

| English                | Spanish                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ |
| A **big** house        | Una casa **grande**      |
| The **red** car        | El coche **rojo**        |
| A **delicious** paella | Una paella **deliciosa** |
| The **narrow** streets | Las calles **estrechas** |

**Think of it this way:** Spanish names the thing first, then describes it.

---

## 📌 Adjectives That Always Go BEFORE the Noun

These categories always precede the noun:

**Articles:** el, la, los, las, un, una, unos, unas

**Demonstratives:** este, ese, aquel (and their forms)

**Possessives:** mi, tu, su, nuestro, vuestro

**Numbers:** uno, dos, tres, primero, segundo...

**Indefinites:** algún, ningún, cada, otro, poco, mucho, varios, todo

**Examples:**

1. **Mis dos hijas van al colegio** (My two daughters go to school)
2. **Cada mañana paseo por el Turia** (Every morning I walk through the Turia)
3. **Hay muchas naranjas en Valencia** (There are many oranges in Valencia)
4. **Otro día iremos a la playa** (Another day we'll go to the beach)

---

## ✂️ Apocopation: Adjectives That Shorten

Some adjectives lose their final syllable or letter when placed before a **masculine singular** noun. This is called "apocopation."

### Before Masculine Singular Nouns

| Full Form | Shortened | Example                              |
| --------- | --------- | ------------------------------------ |
| primero   | primer    | El **primer** día en Valencia        |
| tercero   | tercer    | El **tercer** piso del edificio      |
| bueno     | buen      | Un **buen** restaurante en El Carmen |
| malo      | mal       | Un **mal** momento para salir        |
| alguno    | algún     | ¿Hay **algún** supermercado cerca?   |
| ninguno   | ningún    | No hay **ningún** problema           |

**Note:** These ONLY shorten before masculine singular nouns.

- Un **buen** amigo (masculine singular -- shortened)
- Una **buena** amiga (feminine -- NOT shortened)
- Unos **buenos** amigos (plural -- NOT shortened)

### Special Case: GRANDE

**Grande** shortens to **gran** before ANY singular noun (masculine or feminine), and the meaning shifts from "big" to "great":

| Form   | Placement | Meaning | Example                              |
| ------ | --------- | ------- | ------------------------------------ |
| gran   | before    | great   | Una **gran** ciudad (A great city)   |
| gran   | before    | great   | Un **gran** momento (A great moment) |
| grande | after     | big     | Una ciudad **grande** (A big city)   |

- Valencia es una **gran** ciudad (Valencia is a great city)
- Valencia es una ciudad **grande** (Valencia is a big city)

## 🔄 Meaning Changes by Position

This is where it gets interesting. These adjectives mean different things depending on whether they go before or after the noun:

| Adjective | BEFORE the Noun                        | AFTER the Noun                          |
| --------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| antiguo   | former -- un **antiguo** profesor      | old/ancient -- un edificio **antiguo**  |
| cierto    | certain/some -- **cierta** información | true -- información **cierta**          |
| diferente | various -- **diferentes** opciones     | distinct -- opciones **diferentes**     |
| medio     | half -- **media** hora                 | average -- un ciudadano **medio**       |
| mismo     | same -- el **mismo** libro             | himself/itself -- el profesor **mismo** |
| nuevo     | new to the owner -- un **nuevo** coche | brand new -- un coche **nuevo**         |
| pobre     | unfortunate -- el **pobre** hombre     | not wealthy -- el hombre **pobre**      |
| viejo     | long-time -- un **viejo** amigo        | elderly -- un amigo **viejo**           |
| único     | only -- la **única** opción            | unique -- una opción **única**          |

### Examples in Context

1. **Mi antiguo jefe vive en Madrid** (My former boss lives in Madrid)
2. **El centro histórico tiene edificios antiguos** (The historic center has ancient buildings)
3. **Tenemos un nuevo piso en Ruzafa** (We have a new apartment in Ruzafa -- new to us)
4. **Quiero un coche nuevo** (I want a brand new car)
5. **El pobre niño perdió su juguete** (The poor/unfortunate child lost his toy)
6. **El niño pobre no tiene juguetes** (The poor/not wealthy child doesn't have toys)
7. **Es la única playa con bandera azul** (It's the only beach with a blue flag)
8. **Es una experiencia única** (It's a unique experience)

---

## 📐 Multiple Adjective Ordering

When a noun has more than one adjective, the most distinguishing adjective goes **closest to the noun**.

- Una casa **grande** y **bonita** (a big, beautiful house)
- Un restaurante **valenciano** **pequeño** (a small Valencian restaurant)

If one adjective is more essential to identifying the noun, place it right next to it:

- Un **buen** vino tinto **español** (A good Spanish red wine)
  - "tinto" is closest because it distinguishes the type of wine
  - "español" adds further description
  - "buen" goes before as usual

## 🍊 Valencia Examples

**Describing places:**

1. **El Mercado Central es un edificio antiguo y hermoso** (The Central Market is an ancient and beautiful building)
2. **Hay diferentes barrios en Valencia** (There are various neighborhoods in Valencia)
3. **La Ciudad de las Artes es una gran atracción** (The City of Arts is a great attraction)

**Describing people:**

4. **Mi vieja amiga de Olympia me visitó** (My long-time friend from Olympia visited me)
5. **Es un buen profesor de español** (He's a good Spanish teacher)

**Describing food:**

6. **Probamos un nuevo restaurante en El Cabanyal** (We tried a new restaurant in El Cabanyal -- new to us)
7. **La paella es la única comida que pido los domingos** (Paella is the only food I order on Sundays)

## ✏️ Practice Exercises

### A. Place the Adjective Correctly

Put the adjective in the right position and use the correct form:

1. Es un \_\_\_ día \_\_\_ (bueno)
2. Tengo una \_\_\_ amiga \_\_\_ (viejo -- meaning: long-time)
3. Quiero una \_\_\_ casa \_\_\_ (nuevo -- meaning: brand new)
4. Es el \_\_\_ piso \_\_\_ (tercero)
5. Valencia es una \_\_\_ ciudad \_\_\_ (grande -- meaning: great)

### B. Translate with Meaning Changes

Translate into Spanish, paying attention to adjective placement:

1. My former teacher lives in Valencia
2. That is an ancient building
3. It's the only option
4. She is an average student
5. He himself said it

### C. Apocopation Practice

Fill in the correct shortened or full form:

1. Es un \_\_\_ momento para hablar (malo)
2. Hoy es el \_\_\_ día de clase (primero)
3. No hay \_\_\_ problema (ninguno)
4. Es una \_\_\_ noticia (bueno)
5. Fue una \_\_\_ fiesta (grande -- meaning: great)
