# 🇪🇸 The Complete Vosotros Guide

## You Live in Spain -- You Need Vosotros!

**⏱️ Study Time: 15 minutes**
**🎯 Goal: Master vosotros in every tense you've learned -- this is your one-stop reference!**

---

## Overview

If you live in Spain, vosotros is not optional. It's how Spaniards say "you all" in every informal context -- with friends, family, coworkers, neighbors, even strangers your age. Latin American textbooks skip it. You can't.

This lesson consolidates every vosotros form across every tense you've learned so far into one reference.

## 4️⃣ OS -- The Vosotros Object Pronoun

Just like "me" is for yo and "te" is for tú, **os** is for vosotros.

### Direct / Indirect Object

1. **Os quiero**

   - (I love you all)

2. **Os lo digo**

   - (I'm telling you all)

3. **Os voy a llamar mañana**

   - (I'm going to call you all tomorrow)

4. **¿Os ha gustado la película?**

   - (Did you all like the movie?)

5. **Os traigo un regalo**
   - (I'm bringing you all a gift)

### Placement Rules

- Before conjugated verb: **Os veo** (I see you all)
- Before haber in perfect tenses: **Os he dicho** (I've told you all)
- Attached to infinitive: **Quiero veros** (I want to see you all)
- Attached to gerund: **Estoy esperándoos** (I'm waiting for you all)
- Attached to affirmative command: **Sentaos** (Sit down, all of you)

---

## 5️⃣ Vosotros Reflexive: OS + Verb

When the action is reflexive (you all do something to yourselves), use **os**:

6. **Os levantáis temprano**

   - (You all get up early)

7. **Os habéis duchado ya**

   - (You all have showered already)

8. **Os vais a divertir**
   - (You all are going to have fun)

### Reflexive Commands

**Affirmative:** Drop the -d, add -os:

- sentad + os → **Sentaos** (Sit down, all of you)
- levantad + os → **Levantaos** (Get up, all of you)
- callaos → **Callaos** (Be quiet, all of you)

**Exception:** irse → **Idos** (Go away, all of you) -- though in casual speech you'll hear "iros"

**Negative:** no + os + subjunctive (normal order):

- **No os preocupéis** (Don't worry, all of you)
- **No os sentéis ahí** (Don't sit there, all of you)

## 6️⃣ When Spaniards Switch to Ustedes

Even in Spain, there are situations where people use ustedes instead of vosotros:

| Context                                | Example                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Very formal settings (legal, business) | "¿Ustedes han firmado el contrato?"                                                          |
| Addressing elderly strangers           | "¿Ustedes necesitan ayuda?" (though this is decreasing)                                      |
| Southern Spain & Canarias              | Much of Andalucía and the Canary Islands use ustedes instead of vosotros -- even informally! |

**For Valencia:** You'll use vosotros in almost every situation. Ustedes sounds overly formal or foreign here.

## 7️⃣ Common Vosotros Phrases for Valencia Daily Life

9. **¿Vosotros qué queréis?**

   - (What do you guys want?)

10. **¿Os apetece ir a la playa?**

    - (Do you feel like going to the beach?)

11. **¿Habéis probado la horchata?**

    - (Have you all tried horchata?)

12. **Venid a cenar a nuestra casa**

    - (Come have dinner at our house)

13. **¿Os quedáis un rato más?**

    - (Are you all staying a bit longer?)

14. **Pasadlo bien**

    - (Have a good time, all of you)

15. **Decidme qué pensáis**
    - (Tell me what you all think)

## 💡 Cultural Note

In Valencia, vosotros is the default for any group you'd address informally. At school pick-up, at the park, ordering for a group at a bar -- it's always vosotros. The only time you might hear ustedes is in very official contexts or from Latin American speakers. If someone uses ustedes with you casually, they're probably not from mainland Spain!
