# 🔄 Preterite vs Imperfect - Beyond the Basics

## Expand Your Past Tense: Regular Patterns, Irregular Stems, and Storytelling!

**⏱️ Study Time: 15-20 minutes**
**🎯 Goal: Conjugate regular and irregular preterites and use both past tenses to tell stories!**

---

## Overview

In [Lesson 01](01-building-block-verbs-past-tense.md), you learned the preterite and imperfect for the 6 building block verbs (SER, ESTAR, IR, TENER, HACER, HABER). Those are all irregular. Now it is time to learn the **regular** preterite patterns that work for hundreds of verbs, plus a set of common irregular preterite stems. With these tools, you can narrate anything that happened in the past.

This lesson also builds on [beginner/05 - Regular Verb Patterns](../beginner/05-regular-verb-patterns.md), where you learned -AR, -ER, and -IR present tense endings.

---

## 1️⃣ Regular -AR Preterite Endings

Drop the **-ar** and add these endings. The pattern is completely predictable.

**Model verb: HABLAR (to speak)**

| Person              | Ending      | Conjugation | English        |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ----------- | -------------- |
| Yo                  | **-é**      | hablé       | I spoke        |
| Tú                  | **-aste**   | hablaste    | you spoke      |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | **-ó**      | habló       | he/she spoke   |
| Nosotros/as         | **-amos**   | hablamos    | we spoke       |
| Vosotros/as         | **-asteis** | hablasteis  | you all spoke  |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | **-aron**   | hablaron    | they/you spoke |

**⚠️ Note:** The nosotros form (hablamos) is identical to the present tense! Context and time markers tell you which is which.

**More regular -AR verbs that follow this pattern:**

| Infinitive | Yo (preterite) | English   |
| ---------- | -------------- | --------- |
| caminar    | caminé         | I walked  |
| estudiar   | estudié        | I studied |
| cocinar    | cociné         | I cooked  |
| comprar    | compré         | I bought  |
| trabajar   | trabajé        | I worked  |
| llegar     | llegué\*       | I arrived |

\*Spelling change: **llegar → llegué** (g → gu before é to keep the hard "g" sound)

## 2️⃣ Regular -ER/-IR Preterite Endings

-ER and -IR verbs share the **same** preterite endings. One less set to memorize!

**Model verb: COMER (to eat)**

| Person              | Ending      | Conjugation | English      |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ----------- | ------------ |
| Yo                  | **-í**      | comí        | I ate        |
| Tú                  | **-iste**   | comiste     | you ate      |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | **-ió**     | comió       | he/she ate   |
| Nosotros/as         | **-imos**   | comimos     | we ate       |
| Vosotros/as         | **-isteis** | comisteis   | you all ate  |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | **-ieron**  | comieron    | they/you ate |

**Works the same for -IR verbs (vivir → viví, viviste, vivió...)**

**More regular -ER/-IR verbs that follow this pattern:**

| Infinitive | Yo (preterite) | English    |
| ---------- | -------------- | ---------- |
| beber      | bebí           | I drank    |
| correr     | corrí          | I ran      |
| aprender   | aprendí        | I learned  |
| escribir   | escribí        | I wrote    |
| abrir      | abrí           | I opened   |
| recibir    | recibí         | I received |

## 3️⃣ Common Irregular Preterites

These verbs have **irregular stems** but share a common set of **unstressed endings**: -e, -iste, -o, -imos, -isteis, -ieron (no accents on yo or él/ella!).

### PODER (to be able to) - stem: **pud-**

| Person              | Conjugation | English           |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ----------------- |
| Yo                  | pude        | I could / managed |
| Tú                  | pudiste     | you could         |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | pudo        | he/she could      |
| Nosotros/as         | pudimos     | we could          |
| Vosotros/as         | pudisteis   | you all could     |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | pudieron    | they/you could    |

### PONER (to put) - stem: **pus-**

| Person              | Conjugation | English      |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ------------ |
| Yo                  | puse        | I put        |
| Tú                  | pusiste     | you put      |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | puso        | he/she put   |
| Nosotros/as         | pusimos     | we put       |
| Vosotros/as         | pusisteis   | you all put  |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | pusieron    | they/you put |

### SABER (to know) - stem: **sup-**

| Person              | Conjugation | English            |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ------------------ |
| Yo                  | supe        | I found out        |
| Tú                  | supiste     | you found out      |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | supo        | he/she found out   |
| Nosotros/as         | supimos     | we found out       |
| Vosotros/as         | supisteis   | you all found out  |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | supieron    | they/you found out |

**💡 Meaning shift:** In the preterite, SABER means "found out / learned" rather than just "knew."

### QUERER (to want) - stem: **quis-**

| Person              | Conjugation | English          |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| Yo                  | quise       | I wanted / tried |
| Tú                  | quisiste    | you wanted       |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | quiso       | he/she wanted    |
| Nosotros/as         | quisimos    | we wanted        |
| Vosotros/as         | quisisteis  | you all wanted   |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | quisieron   | they/you wanted  |

### VENIR (to come) - stem: **vin-**

| Person              | Conjugation | English       |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ------------- |
| Yo                  | vine        | I came        |
| Tú                  | viniste     | you came      |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | vino        | he/she came   |
| Nosotros/as         | vinimos     | we came       |
| Vosotros/as         | vinisteis   | you all came  |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | vinieron    | they/you came |

### DECIR (to say/tell) - stem: **dij-**

| Person              | Conjugation | English       |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ------------- |
| Yo                  | dije        | I said        |
| Tú                  | dijiste     | you said      |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | dijo        | he/she said   |
| Nosotros/as         | dijimos     | we said       |
| Vosotros/as         | dijisteis   | you all said  |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | dijeron\*   | they/you said |

### TRAER (to bring) - stem: **traj-**

| Person              | Conjugation | English          |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| Yo                  | traje       | I brought        |
| Tú                  | trajiste    | you brought      |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | trajo       | he/she brought   |
| Nosotros/as         | trajimos    | we brought       |
| Vosotros/as         | trajisteis  | you all brought  |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | trajeron\*  | they/you brought |

**⚠️ Note:** Verbs with **j-stems** (dij-, traj-) use **-eron** (not -ieron) in the ellos form.

### DAR (to give) - irregular but short!

| Person              | Conjugation | English       |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ------------- |
| Yo                  | di          | I gave        |
| Tú                  | diste       | you gave      |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | dio         | he/she gave   |
| Nosotros/as         | dimos       | we gave       |
| Vosotros/as         | disteis     | you all gave  |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | dieron      | they/you gave |

**💡 Note:** DAR uses -ER/-IR preterite endings despite being an -AR verb. No accent marks needed.

### VER (to see) - irregular but short!

| Person              | Conjugation | English      |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ------------ |
| Yo                  | vi          | I saw        |
| Tú                  | viste       | you saw      |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | vio         | he/she saw   |
| Nosotros/as         | vimos       | we saw       |
| Vosotros/as         | visteis     | you all saw  |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | vieron      | they/you saw |

## 4️⃣ Storytelling: Imperfect vs Preterite Together

The real power of past tense comes from **combining** both tenses in one narrative.

### The Rule

| Imperfect (background)          | Preterite (events)            |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Sets the scene                  | Moves the story forward       |
| Describes what was happening    | Says what happened            |
| Weather, time, feelings, habits | Actions that started or ended |
| "It was raining..."             | "...when I arrived"           |

### 📖 A Saturday in Valencia

**Imperfect** = _italic_ | **Preterite** = **bold**

> _Era_ un sábado por la mañana. _Hacía_ sol y _no hacía_ mucho calor. Toda la familia _estaba_ en casa. Las niñas _jugaban_ en su habitación.
>
> De repente, papá **dijo**: "¡Vamos a la playa!" Las niñas **gritaron** de alegría. Mamá **preparó** una bolsa con toallas y protector solar. Todos **caminamos** hasta la playa de la Malvarrosa.
>
> _Había_ mucha gente porque _era_ un día perfecto. Las niñas **corrieron** al agua inmediatamente. Papá **compró** helados para todos. **Comimos** paella en un restaurante cerca del mar. **Fue** un día increíble.

_Translation: It was a Saturday morning. It was sunny and not too hot. The whole family was at home. The girls were playing in their room. Suddenly, Dad said: "Let's go to the beach!" The girls shouted with joy. Mom prepared a bag with towels and sunscreen. We all walked to the Malvarrosa beach. There were lots of people because it was a perfect day. The girls ran to the water immediately. Dad bought ice cream for everyone. We ate paella at a restaurant near the sea. It was an incredible day._

## 5️⃣ The Interruption Pattern

One of the most common ways to combine both tenses:

### **Estaba + gerund + cuando + preterite**

"I was doing X when Y happened"

1. **Estaba caminando por el Turia cuando empezó a llover**

   - (I was walking through the Turia when it started to rain)

2. **Estábamos comiendo paella cuando llegaron nuestros amigos**

   - (We were eating paella when our friends arrived)

3. **Las niñas estaban jugando en el parque cuando las llamé**

   - (The girls were playing in the park when I called them)

4. **Estaba estudiando español cuando sonó el teléfono**

   - (I was studying Spanish when the phone rang)

5. **Mi hija estaba leyendo un libro cuando se fue la luz**
   - (My daughter was reading a book when the power went out)

**The pattern:** The imperfect (estaba + -ando/-iendo) sets the ongoing action. The preterite interrupts it with a sudden event.

## 💡 Cultural Note: Storytelling in Spain

Spaniards love to tell stories! When chatting with friends or family, you will hear both tenses woven together constantly. Pay attention to how native speakers in Valencia switch between imperfect (setting scenes, describing feelings) and preterite (narrating what happened). Listening to conversations at a terraza or in the mercado is one of the best ways to internalize these patterns naturally.
