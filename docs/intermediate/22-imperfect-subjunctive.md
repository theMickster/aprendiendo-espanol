# 🌙 The Imperfect Subjunctive (Imperfecto de Subjuntivo)

## The Gateway to Sounding Truly Fluent

**⏱️ Study Time: 15-20 minutes**
**🎯 Goal: Use the imperfect subjunctive for hypotheticals, wishes, and polite requests -- the key to advanced Spanish!**

---

## Overview

The imperfect subjunctive is where your Spanish starts to sound truly fluent. It's the tense that lets you say "if I were...", "I wish I could...", and "as if he were..." -- the language of imagination, dreams, and polite requests. It sounds intimidating, but the formation is beautifully logical once you see the pattern.

---

## 1️⃣ Formation: From Preterite to Imperfect Subjunctive

The secret: take the **ellos** preterite form, remove **-ron**, and add the -ra endings.

### Step by Step

1. Take the **ellos** preterite: hablaron, comieron, vivieron
2. Remove **-ron**: habla-, comie-, vivie-
3. Add the endings: -ra, -ras, -ra, -ramos, -rais, -ran

### HABLAR (Regular -AR)

Ellos preterite: habla**ron** → stem: **habla-**

| Person              | Spanish    | English              |
| ------------------- | ---------- | -------------------- |
| Yo                  | hablara    | (that) I spoke       |
| Tú                  | hablaras   | (that) you spoke     |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | hablara    | (that) he/she spoke  |
| Nosotros/as         | habláramos | (that) we spoke      |
| Vosotros/as         | hablarais  | (that) you all spoke |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | hablaran   | (that) they spoke    |

**Note:** The nosotros form always gets an accent: habláramos, comiéramos, viviéramos.

### COMER (Regular -ER)

Ellos preterite: comie**ron** → stem: **comie-**

| Person              | Spanish    | English            |
| ------------------- | ---------- | ------------------ |
| Yo                  | comiera    | (that) I ate       |
| Tú                  | comieras   | (that) you ate     |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | comiera    | (that) he/she ate  |
| Nosotros/as         | comiéramos | (that) we ate      |
| Vosotros/as         | comierais  | (that) you all ate |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | comieran   | (that) they ate    |

### VIVIR (Regular -IR)

Ellos preterite: vivie**ron** → stem: **vivie-**

| Person              | Spanish    | English              |
| ------------------- | ---------- | -------------------- |
| Yo                  | viviera    | (that) I lived       |
| Tú                  | vivieras   | (that) you lived     |
| Él/Ella/Usted       | viviera    | (that) he/she lived  |
| Nosotros/as         | viviéramos | (that) we lived      |
| Vosotros/as         | vivierais  | (that) you all lived |
| Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes | vivieran   | (that) they lived    |

---

## 2️⃣ The -SE Alternative

You'll encounter a second set of endings in writing and formal speech:

| -RA form   | -SE form   |
| ---------- | ---------- |
| hablara    | hablase    |
| hablaras   | hablases   |
| hablara    | hablase    |
| habláramos | hablásemos |
| hablarais  | hablaseis  |
| hablaran   | hablasen   |

Both are correct and interchangeable. **Use -ra in speech** -- it's far more common. You'll see -se in literature, legal texts, and formal writing.

---

## 3️⃣ Irregular Forms

Here's the beautiful part: **all irregulars follow the same rule.** If the preterite is irregular, the imperfect subjunctive inherits that irregularity automatically.

This is why learning preterite stems (lesson 01) pays off now!

| Infinitive | Ellos Preterite | Imp. Subjunctive (yo) | English             |
| ---------- | --------------- | --------------------- | ------------------- |
| tener      | tuvieron        | **tuviera**           | (that) I had        |
| poder      | pudieron        | **pudiera**           | (that) I could      |
| hacer      | hicieron        | **hiciera**           | (that) I did/made   |
| ser / ir   | fueron          | **fuera**             | (that) I was/went   |
| decir      | dijeron         | **dijera**            | (that) I said       |
| saber      | supieron        | **supiera**           | (that) I knew       |
| haber      | hubieron        | **hubiera**           | (that) I had (aux.) |
| querer     | quisieron       | **quisiera**          | (that) I wanted     |
| poner      | pusieron        | **pusiera**           | (that) I put        |
| venir      | vinieron        | **viniera**           | (that) I came       |
| estar      | estuvieron      | **estuviera**         | (that) I was        |
| traer      | trajeron        | **trajera**           | (that) I brought    |

## 4️⃣ Si Clauses: If...Then

This is the most common use. **Si + imperfect subjunctive + conditional:**

1. **Si tuviera más tiempo, viajaría más**

   - (If I had more time, I would travel more)

2. **Si viviéramos en Madrid, iríamos al teatro cada semana**

   - (If we lived in Madrid, we would go to the theater every week)

3. **Si pudiera hablar español perfecto, sería increíble**

   - (If I could speak perfect Spanish, it would be incredible)

4. **Si no lloviera, iríamos a la playa**

   - (If it weren't raining, we would go to the beach)

5. **Si mis padres estuvieran aquí, les encantaría Valencia**
   - (If my parents were here, they would love Valencia)

### ⚠️ Three Types of Si Clauses

| Type       | Structure                                   | Example                              |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Likely     | Si + present + present/future               | Si llueve, no vamos                  |
| Unlikely   | Si + imp. subjunctive + conditional         | **Si lloviera, no iríamos**          |
| Impossible | Si + pluperfect subj. + conditional perfect | Si hubiera llovido, no habríamos ido |

This lesson covers the "unlikely" type. You'll encounter the "impossible" type later.

---

## 5️⃣ Ojalá + Imperfect Subjunctive

**Ojalá** (I wish / if only) changes meaning depending on which subjunctive you use:

| Structure                     | Meaning                                      | Example                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ojalá + present subjunctive   | Wish about something **possible**            | Ojalá llueva (I hope it rains)                                 |
| Ojalá + imperfect subjunctive | Wish about something **unlikely/impossible** | Ojalá lloviera (I wish it would rain -- but it probably won't) |

6. **Ojalá pudiera volar**

   - (I wish I could fly -- impossible)

7. **Ojalá tuviera más dinero**

   - (I wish I had more money -- unlikely right now)

8. **Ojalá estuviéramos en la playa ahora mismo**

   - (I wish we were at the beach right now)

9. **Ojalá hablara español como un nativo**
   - (I wish I spoke Spanish like a native)

---

## 6️⃣ Como si + Imperfect Subjunctive (As if...)

**Como si** always takes the imperfect subjunctive -- no exceptions:

10. **Habla como si fuera español**

    - (He speaks as if he were Spanish)

11. **Gasta dinero como si fuera millonario**

    - (He spends money as if he were a millionaire)

12. **Me mira como si no me conociera**

    - (She looks at me as if she didn't know me)

13. **Actúan como si no pasara nada**
    - (They act as if nothing were happening)

---

## 7️⃣ Polite Requests -- The Spain Classic

Using **quisiera** and **pudiera** is the elegant, polite way to make requests in Spain:

14. **Quisiera un café con leche, por favor**

    - (I would like a coffee with milk, please)

15. **Quisiera reservar una mesa para cuatro**

    - (I would like to reserve a table for four)

16. **¿Pudiera decirme dónde está el baño?**

    - (Could you tell me where the bathroom is?)

17. **Quisiera saber si tienen habitaciones disponibles**
    - (I would like to know if you have rooms available)

**Tip:** Quisiera is more polite than "quiero" and more natural than "querría" in everyday Spanish.

## 💡 Cultural Note

You'll hear "quisiera" constantly in Spanish service situations -- at cafés, restaurants, hotels, and shops. It's the go-to polite form. While "quiero" (I want) isn't rude, "quisiera" shows a level of courtesy that Spaniards appreciate. It's also a great way to practice the imperfect subjunctive naturally every time you order coffee!
