## 🎯 What This Topic Covers

This is the **foundation topic** for the entire DGT exam. It covers the official legal definitions of roads, road parts, road users, and vehicle types as defined in the Ley de Tráfico (Anexo I). The DGT exam loves testing whether you know the precise difference between similar terms — for example, _calzada_ vs _plataforma_, or _parada_ vs _estacionamiento_. Getting these definitions right is easy marks on the exam.

## ⚠️ Why This Topic Trips People Up

The DGT definitions are very **specific and legal**. A few traps to watch for:

- A **peatón** (pedestrian) includes someone pushing a pram or walking a bicycle — not just someone walking empty-handed
- An **arcén** (hard shoulder) is NOT for normal driving — only exceptional circumstances
- **Parada** (stopping) = less than 2 minutes, driver stays with the vehicle. **Estacionamiento** (parking) = everything else
- A **travesía** is a section of interurban road that passes _through_ a town — it follows urban rules but isn't a true urban road
- **Autopista** and **autovía** look identical but have different legal access rules

## 🛣️ PILLAR 1: The Road and Its Parts — La Vía y Sus Partes

### Types of Road by Location

The DGT classifies roads first by whether they're inside or outside a built-up area (_poblado_):

| Español             | English         | DGT Definition                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------- | --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vía urbana**      | Urban road      | Any public road situated **inside** a built-up area, except for through-roads (_travesías_)                                             |
| **Vía interurbana** | Interurban road | Any public road situated **outside** a built-up area                                                                                    |
| **Travesía**        | Through-road    | A stretch of **interurban** road that passes through a built-up area. It follows urban speed rules but is legally classified separately |

This distinction matters because **speed limits, priority rules, and parking rules** all change depending on whether you're on an urban road, an interurban road, or a through-road.

### Types of Interurban Road

| Español                    | English                          | Key Features                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Autopista**              | Motorway (may be tolled)         | Separated carriageways, no level crossings, no access from adjacent properties, no pedestrians/cyclists. May require toll (_peaje_) |
| **Autovía**                | Dual carriageway (free)          | Same features as autopista BUT may have some access from adjacent properties in limited cases. Always free                          |
| **Vía para automóviles**   | Road reserved for motor vehicles | Single carriageway, no access from adjacent properties. Signposted with S-3                                                         |
| **Carretera convencional** | Conventional road                | Any road that doesn't meet autopista, autovía, or vía para automóviles criteria. May be single or dual carriageway                  |

**Key DGT exam trap:** Autopista and autovía look almost identical on the road. The main practical difference is that autopistas may charge tolls and have stricter access control. Both have separated carriageways and no level crossings.

### Parts of the Road

These are the **official DGT definitions** from the Ley de Tráfico (Anexo I). The exam tests these exact definitions.

| Español           | English                            | Official DGT Definition                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Plataforma**    | Platform                           | The zone of the road intended for vehicle use, formed by the carriageway (_calzada_), the central reservation (_mediana_), the hard shoulders (_arcenes_), and paved verges (_bermas_) |
| **Calzada**       | Carriageway                        | The part of the road intended for vehicle traffic. It is made up of a number of lanes (_carriles_)                                                                                     |
| **Carril**        | Lane                               | A longitudinal strip into which the carriageway may be divided, marked or unmarked, wide enough for a single file of cars (not motorbikes)                                             |
| **Arcén**         | Hard shoulder                      | A paved longitudinal strip alongside the carriageway, NOT intended for motor vehicles except in exceptional circumstances                                                              |
| **Mediana**       | Central reservation / Median       | A longitudinal strip between two separated platforms, not intended for traffic                                                                                                         |
| **Acera**         | Pavement / Sidewalk                | A longitudinal zone of the road, raised or not, intended for pedestrian traffic                                                                                                        |
| **Zona peatonal** | Pedestrian zone                    | Part of the road, raised or otherwise delimited, reserved for pedestrians. Includes the pavement (_acera_), promenade (_paseo_), and footpath (_andén_)                                |
| **Refugio**       | Pedestrian refuge / Traffic island | A pedestrian zone situated on the carriageway, protected from vehicle traffic                                                                                                          |
| **Isleta**        | Traffic island                     | A raised or delimited zone on the carriageway, usually at junctions, designed to channel traffic                                                                                       |
| **Berma**         | Verge                              | The strip between the outer edge of the hard shoulder and the ditch                                                                                                                    |

### Junctions and Crossings

| Español          | English                 | Official DGT Definition                                                                                               |
| ---------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Intersección** | Intersection / Junction | A point in the road network where all possible vehicle crossings occur **at the same level**                          |
| **Glorieta**     | Roundabout              | A circular intersection where connecting roads communicate through a ring with rotary traffic around a central island |
| **Paso a nivel** | Level crossing          | A crossing at the same level between a public road and a railway line                                                 |
| **Apartadero**   | Lay-by                  | A widening of the platform intended for temporary stopping or parking of vehicles                                     |

## 🚶 PILLAR 2: Road Users — Los Usuarios de la Vía

These are the **legal definitions** the DGT uses. The exam specifically tests the boundaries of each definition.

### Conductor — Driver

The person who steers or is in command of a vehicle, or who is in charge of animals on the road. **Important:** In a learner vehicle (_vehículo de aprendizaje_), the **instructor** is legally considered the driver because they have access to the dual controls.

### Conductor novel — New driver

A driver whose licence is **less than one year old**. New drivers must display the green L-plate and start with **8 points** (rather than the 12 that experienced drivers can accumulate).

### Conductor profesional — Professional driver

A driver whose **occupation** is driving vehicles for the transport of goods or passengers.

### Peatón — Pedestrian

A person who, without being a driver, travels on foot on public roads. The DGT definition **also** includes:

- People pushing a pram (_cochecito de niño_) or a wheelchair
- People pushing or walking alongside any small non-motorised vehicle
- People walking a bicycle or two-wheeled moped on foot
- People with disabilities travelling at walking pace in a wheelchair (with or without a motor)

**DGT exam trap:** Someone walking their bicycle is a **pedestrian**, not a cyclist. The moment they get on and ride, they become a driver.

### Titular del vehículo — Vehicle owner (registered keeper)

The person in whose name the vehicle is registered in the official register. This person is responsible for keeping the vehicle and its documentation in good order.

## 🚗 PILLAR 3: Vehicles — Los Vehículos

### Vehicle Classifications for Permiso B

The DGT classifies vehicles in great detail. For the Permiso B exam, you need to know the main categories:

| Español                            | English                           | Key Defining Features                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ciclo**                          | Cycle                             | Vehicle with at least 2 wheels, powered by the rider's muscular effort (pedals/cranks)               |
| **Bicicleta**                      | Bicycle                           | A cycle with two wheels                                                                              |
| **Bicicleta con pedaleo asistido** | Electric-assist bicycle (pedelec) | A bicycle with a motor that cuts out above 25 km/h                                                   |
| **Ciclomotor**                     | Moped                             | Engine ≤50 cm³ (if combustion), max speed ≤45 km/h by design                                         |
| **Motocicleta**                    | Motorcycle                        | Engine >50 cm³ OR max speed >45 km/h. Two wheels (or three with a sidecar)                           |
| **Turismo**                        | Car                               | Motor vehicle for transporting people, at least 4 wheels, max **9 seats** including the driver       |
| **Furgoneta**                      | Van                               | Derived from a car (_derivado de turismo_), cabin integrated into the body, for goods, max 9 seats   |
| **Camión**                         | Lorry / Truck                     | Motor vehicle with 4+ wheels for transporting goods, cabin NOT integrated into the body, max 9 seats |
| **Autobús / Autocar**              | Bus / Coach                       | Motor vehicle with **more than 9 seats** (including the driver), for transporting people and luggage |
| **Remolque**                       | Trailer                           | Non-self-propelled vehicle designed to be towed by a motor vehicle                                   |
| **Remolque ligero**                | Light trailer                     | A trailer with a maximum authorised mass (MMA) not exceeding 750 kg                                  |
| **Vehículo especial**              | Special vehicle                   | Self-propelled or towed vehicle designed for works or services (includes agricultural machinery)     |

### What Can You Drive with Permiso B?

With a standard Class B licence, you can drive:

- **Turismos** (cars) with a maximum authorised mass (MMA) of **3,500 kg** and maximum **9 seats** including the driver
- **Light trailers** (≤750 kg MMA) behind your car
- Heavier trailers if the combined MMA of car + trailer does not exceed **3,500 kg** (with some exceptions up to 4,250 kg with additional training)

### Key Legal Distinction: Parada vs Estacionamiento

| Español             | English                     | Official DGT Definition                                                                     |
| ------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Detención**       | Stopping (involuntary)      | Immobilising a vehicle due to emergency, traffic needs, or to comply with a regulation      |
| **Parada**          | Stopping (voluntary, brief) | Immobilising a vehicle for **less than 2 minutes**, without the driver leaving the vehicle  |
| **Estacionamiento** | Parking                     | Immobilising a vehicle for longer than 2 minutes, or any time the driver leaves the vehicle |

**DGT exam trap:** If you stop for 1 minute but get out of the car — that's **estacionamiento**, not _parada_, because the driver left the vehicle.

## 🗣️ Conjugation Spotlight

### CIRCULAR — to drive / to travel (on a road)

A regular -ar verb, but essential for the DGT exam — it appears in countless questions.

|             | Presente   | Pretérito    | Imperfecto   |
| ----------- | ---------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| yo          | circulo    | circulé      | circulaba    |
| tú          | circulas   | circulaste   | circulabas   |
| él/ella     | circula    | circuló      | circulaba    |
| nosotros    | circulamos | circulamos   | circulábamos |
| vosotros    | circuláis  | circulasteis | circulabais  |
| ellos/ellas | circulan   | circularon   | circulaban   |

**Key sentences:**

- _Los vehículos circulan por la calzada._ — Vehicles travel on the carriageway.
- _Ayer circulé por la autopista._ — Yesterday I drove on the motorway.
- _Está prohibido circular por el arcén._ — It is forbidden to drive on the hard shoulder.

### CRUZAR — to cross

|             | Presente | Pretérito  | Imperfecto |
| ----------- | -------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| yo          | cruzo    | crucé      | cruzaba    |
| tú          | cruzas   | cruzaste   | cruzabas   |
| él/ella     | cruza    | cruzó      | cruzaba    |
| nosotros    | cruzamos | cruzamos   | cruzábamos |
| vosotros    | cruzáis  | cruzasteis | cruzabais  |
| ellos/ellas | cruzan   | cruzaron   | cruzaban   |

**Note:** _Cruzar_ has a **z→c** spelling change in the preterite yo form: _crucé_ (not ~~cruzé~~).

**Key sentences:**

- _Los peatones cruzan por el paso de peatones._ — Pedestrians cross at the pedestrian crossing.
- _Crucé la intersección con cuidado._ — I crossed the junction carefully.

## 🏙️ Valencia Connection

Valencia gives you excellent real-world examples of these definitions:

- **Autopista:** The AP-7 (tolled sections) running along the coast
- **Autovía:** The A-7 / V-30 ring road around Valencia (free)
- **Vía urbana:** Streets within the city of Valencia, like Calle Colón or Gran Vía
- **Travesía:** When the N-332 passes through small coastal towns south of Valencia
- **Glorieta:** The large roundabouts where the Turia riverbed park meets major roads
- **Carril-bici:** Valencia has an extensive network — including the one running through the Turia park
- **Zona peatonal:** The historic old town around Plaza de la Reina
- **Arcén:** Visible on conventional roads driving out to the Albufera

## ⚠️ UK → Spain Differences for This Topic

| UK Concept                | Spain Equivalent                  | Key Difference                                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Motorway (M roads)        | Autopista / Autovía               | Spain has two types: autopista (may be tolled) and autovía (always free). UK motorways are always free       |
| A-road / dual carriageway | Autovía or carretera convencional | Spain's autovía is more like a motorway than a UK A-road                                                     |
| Hard shoulder             | Arcén                             | Same concept, but in Spain the arcén is also where cyclists and slow vehicles may travel on interurban roads |
| Pavement / sidewalk       | Acera                             | Same concept                                                                                                 |
| Lay-by                    | Apartadero                        | Same concept                                                                                                 |
| Roundabout                | Glorieta / Rotonda                | Traffic flows **anticlockwise** in Spain (opposite to UK). Give way to traffic already on the roundabout     |
| Level crossing            | Paso a nivel                      | Same concept, but Spain uses different warning signs                                                         |
| Pedestrian                | Peatón                            | Spanish definition is **broader** — includes people walking bikes or pushing prams                           |
| Car                       | Turismo                           | Legal term in Spain. Max 9 seats (not 8 as sometimes misremembered — it's 8 passengers + 1 driver)           |

## 📌 Summary — What You Need to Remember

1. **Vía urbana** = inside a town. **Vía interurbana** = outside a town. **Travesía** = interurban road passing through a town.
2. **Autopista** may be tolled; **autovía** is always free. Both have separated carriageways.
3. **Calzada** = the part vehicles drive on. **Plataforma** = calzada + hard shoulders + median + verges.
4. **Arcén** = hard shoulder, NOT for normal traffic (except emergencies and certain slow vehicles on interurban roads).
5. **Peatón** includes anyone walking a bike, pushing a pram, or using a wheelchair at walking pace.
6. A **turismo** = max 9 seats including the driver. More than 9 = bus/coach.
7. **Parada** = less than 2 min AND driver stays with vehicle. **Estacionamiento** = anything else.
8. In a learner car, the **instructor** is legally the driver.
9. Key verbs: _circular_ (to drive/travel), _cruzar_ (to cross), _transitar_ (to travel on foot).
