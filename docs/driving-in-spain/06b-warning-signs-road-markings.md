## ⭐ Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐ (Core Exam Knowledge)

## 🎯 What This Section Covers

Warning signs alert you to **dangers ahead**. Road markings are the **painted lines and symbols on the road surface** that guide, separate, and regulate traffic. Together, they're the second most heavily tested category on the DGT exam.

> **🆕 2025 Sign Catalogue Update (RD 465/2025)** — In force since 1 July 2025, on DGT theory exams since 1 October 2025. Among the warning changes: new wildlife-crossing pictograms, updated 2+1 road warnings, and a new aerial-enforcement warning ("radar aéreo"). ## ⚠️ WARNING SIGNS — Señales de Advertencia de Peligro

### The Universal Rule

**All warning signs are TRIANGULAR — white background, red border, black symbol pointing upward.**

They warn you about a danger ahead. They do NOT prohibit or oblige — they simply say "be careful, something is coming."

**💡 Distance Rule:** On interurban roads, warning signs are placed **150–250 metres before the danger**. In urban areas, they're much closer.

### 🚨 DGT Exam Traps — Warning Signs

1. **P-5 vs P-6:** With barriers (P-5) is safer but you still must obey the lights. Without barriers (P-6) is MORE dangerous — look both ways!
2. **P-8/P-9 distance markers:** The bars count DOWN. Three bars = 300m. One bar = 100m. The exam tests whether you know the distances.
3. **P-50 (Other dangers):** A triangle with a "!" means a danger NOT covered by any specific sign. An additional panel below explains the exact danger. The DGT exam loves asking "What does P-50 mean?" — the answer is ALWAYS "other danger not specifically warned about."

## 🟡 TEMPORARY/ROADWORKS SIGNS — Señales Circunstanciales

Temporary signs have the same shapes as permanent signs BUT use **orange/yellow backgrounds instead of white**. They indicate roadworks, diversions, or temporary conditions.

**Key rule:** Temporary signs **OUTRANK** permanent vertical signs in the hierarchy! (Only police signals outrank them.)

| Type                  | Background              | Example                                 |
| --------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Temporary warning     | Yellow/orange triangle  | Roadworks ahead, temporary lane closure |
| Temporary prohibition | Yellow/orange circle    | Temporary speed limit at roadworks      |
| Temporary direction   | Yellow/orange rectangle | Diversion route                         |

**💡 Mick's Memory Trick:** If the sign looks "wrong colour" (orange instead of white) — it's temporary, and it outranks the permanent signs around it!

## ═══ ROAD MARKINGS — Marcas Viales

Road markings are at the **bottom of the sign hierarchy** — they're overruled by everything else. But on a road with no other signs, they're the ONLY guidance you have.

### Colours of Road Markings

| Colour     | Spanish  | Meaning                                                 |
| ---------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **White**  | Blanco   | Standard markings — lanes, edges, crossings (PERMANENT) |
| **Yellow** | Amarillo | Parking/stopping restrictions, bus lanes, zigzag lines  |
| **Orange** | Naranja  | Temporary markings (roadworks) — override white lines   |
| **Blue**   | Azul     | Limited parking zones (ORA/zona azul)                   |

### Longitudinal Markings (Lines Along the Road)

| Marking                              | Spanish                 | English            | What It Means                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ━━━━━━━ Solid white                  | Línea continua          | Continuous line    | **CANNOT cross or straddle.** No overtaking, no lane changing.                              |
| ╌╌╌╌╌╌╌ Dashed white                 | Línea discontinua       | Broken/dashed line | CAN cross — overtaking and lane changes permitted                                           |
| ━━╌━━╌━━ Solid + dashed side by side | Línea mixta             | Mixed line         | Follow YOUR side: if your side is solid, you CANNOT cross. If your side is dashed, you CAN. |
| ━━━━━━━ Thick solid                  | Línea continua de borde | Edge line          | Marks the edge of the carriageway — do not cross                                            |

### 🚨 DGT Exam Trap — Mixed Lines (Línea Mixta)

The DGT's favourite road marking question! When a solid and dashed line run side by side:

- If the **dashed line is on YOUR side** → you CAN cross to overtake
- If the **solid line is on YOUR side** → you CANNOT cross
- After overtaking, you MUST return before the dashed line becomes solid

### Transversal Markings (Lines Across the Road)

| Marking                       | Spanish               | English                              | What It Means                                       |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| ━━━━━ Thick solid across road | Línea de detención    | Stop line                            | STOP here when sign/light requires it               |
| ╌╌╌╌╌ Dashed across road      | Línea de ceda el paso | Yield line                           | Yield here — stop only if needed                    |
| ▓▓▓▓▓ White stripes           | Paso de peatones      | Pedestrian crossing / Zebra crossing | Pedestrians have priority when on or about to enter |
| ◆◆◆ Diamond pattern           | Paso de ciclistas     | Cyclist crossing                     | Cyclists have priority at this crossing             |

### 🚨 DGT Exam Trap — Stop Line vs Yield Line

- **Solid line across the road** = STOP (matches R-2 sign). You MUST stop AT the line.
- **Dashed line across the road** = YIELD (matches R-1 sign). You only stop IF needed.

### Special Road Markings

| Marking             | Spanish              | English               | What It Means                                            |
| ------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🔺 Triangle on road | Ceda el paso pintado | Painted yield         | Yield symbol reinforced on the road surface              |
| STOP on road        | STOP pintado         | Painted STOP          | STOP reinforced on road surface — you must still stop    |
| ⬆️ Arrows on road   | Flechas de selección | Lane selection arrows | Choose your lane — straight, left, right                 |
| ▨▨▨ Hatched area    | Cebreado             | Hatched area          | DO NOT drive on or over this area                        |
| ═══ Yellow zigzag   | Zigzag amarillo      | Yellow zigzag         | Bus stop zone — NO stopping or parking by other vehicles |
| 🟦 Blue parking bay | Zona azul / ORA      | Blue zone parking     | Time-limited paid parking — get a ticket!                |

### 🚨 DGT Exam Trap — Hatched Areas (Cebreado)

Hatched areas (diagonal lines within borders) are **NOT for driving on**. They separate traffic flows or protect turning areas. The DGT exam will show a hatched area and ask if you can drive across it — the answer is NO.

## 🗣️ Conjugation Spotlight

### ADVERTIR — to warn / to notice

A stem-changing verb: **e → ie** in the present, **e → i** in the preterite (third person).

|             | Presente   | Pretérito    | Imperfecto  |
| ----------- | ---------- | ------------ | ----------- |
| yo          | advierto   | advertí      | advertía    |
| tú          | adviertes  | advertiste   | advertías   |
| él/ella     | advierte   | advirtió     | advertía    |
| nosotros    | advertimos | advertimos   | advertíamos |
| vosotros    | advertís   | advertisteis | advertíais  |
| ellos/ellas | advierten  | advirtieron  | advertían   |

**Key sentences:**

- _La señal advierte de una curva peligrosa._ — The sign warns of a dangerous bend.
- _No advertí la señal de peligro._ — I didn't notice the warning sign.
- _Las marcas viales advierten del peligro._ — The road markings warn of danger.

### CRUZAR — to cross (revisited from 04)

**Reminder:** _Cruzar_ has a **z→c** change in preterite yo: _crucé_.

**Key sentences for this section:**

- _No se puede cruzar la línea continua._ — You cannot cross the solid line.
- _Crucé la línea discontinua para adelantar._ — I crossed the dashed line to overtake.
- _Los peatones cruzan por el paso de peatones._ — Pedestrians cross at the pedestrian crossing.

## ⚠️ US → Spain Differences for Warning/Marking Signs

| US                                        | Spain                                                    | Key Difference                              |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Warning signs are yellow diamonds         | Warning signs are white triangles with red border        | Completely different shape AND colour!      |
| Yellow diamond = warning                  | Yellow diamond = priority road (P-1)                     | This WILL confuse you                       |
| Solid yellow centre line = no crossing    | Solid WHITE centre line = no crossing                    | Spain uses WHITE for all permanent markings |
| Dashed yellow centre line = overtaking OK | Dashed WHITE centre line = overtaking OK                 | Again — white, not yellow                   |
| Yellow = centre lines, white = edge       | White = everything permanent, yellow = restrictions only | Reversed colour logic!                      |
| Hatched areas sometimes drivable          | Hatched areas NEVER drivable                             | Cebreado = absolutely forbidden             |
| Orange = construction/temporary           | Orange/yellow = construction/temporary                   | Same concept!                               |
| School zone yellow pentagon               | P-21 white triangle with children symbol                 | Different shape, same meaning               |
| Level crossing = X-shaped sign            | Level crossing = triangle + distance bars (P-8/P-9)      | Different warning system                    |

## 📌 Summary — What You Need to Remember

1. **Warning signs = white triangle, red border, black symbol.** They warn, they don't prohibit.
2. **Warning signs are placed 150–250m before the danger** on interurban roads.
3. **P-50 (exclamation mark) = other dangers** not covered by specific signs.
4. **Solid line (línea continua) = CANNOT cross.** Dashed line (línea discontinua) = CAN cross.
5. **Mixed line: follow YOUR side.** Dashed on your side = you may cross. Solid on your side = you may not.
6. **White = permanent markings. Yellow = restrictions. Orange = temporary.**
7. **Temporary signs (orange) OUTRANK permanent signs.**
8. **Hatched areas (cebreado) = NEVER drive on them.**
9. **Stop line = solid across road. Yield line = dashed across road.**
10. **Key verbs:** _advertir_ (to warn), _cruzar_ (to cross), _reducir_ (to reduce).
