# 22 — Accidents, First Aid & Emergencies

## ⭐ Difficulty: ⭐⭐ (Core Exam Knowledge)

## 🎯 What This Topic Covers

What to do if you witness or are involved in an accident. The DGT teaches the **PAS protocol** (Proteger–Avisar–Socorrer / Protect–Alert–Help). You don't need to be a paramedic — the exam tests whether you know the correct sequence, when to call 112, and when NOT to move a casualty. With the 2026 V-16 beacon, the protection step has changed significantly.

## 🛡️ THE PAS PROTOCOL — Proteger, Avisar, Socorrer

This is the CORE of the DGT's emergency response teaching. Every question about accidents revolves around this sequence.

### P — PROTEGER (Protect)

**Protect yourself, the casualties, and other road users FIRST.**

| Step | Action (2026)                             | Detail                                                                                                                           |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | **Turn on hazard lights**                 | Immediately upon stopping                                                                                                        |
| 2    | **Activate V-16 beacon**                  | Activate it, then place on your vehicle's roof — no need to walk into traffic like with triangles. Sends GPS location to DGT 3.0 |
| 3    | **Put on reflective vest**                | BEFORE leaving the vehicle (vest is in the cabin, not the boot)                                                                  |
| 4    | **Exit vehicle safely**                   | Only if safe to do so — exit on the side away from traffic                                                                       |
| 5    | **Assess the scene**                      | Check for fire, fuel leaks, unstable vehicles, live wires                                                                        |
| 6    | **Turn off ignition** of crashed vehicles | If safe to approach — reduces fire risk                                                                                          |

**💡 2026 key change:** The V-16 beacon replaces walking 50–100m to place triangles. You stay with/near the vehicle — much safer.

### A — AVISAR (Alert)

**Call 112 — Spain's universal emergency number.** Available 24/7, free from any phone, works without SIM.

| Information to Provide | Spanish Phrase                                                            |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Where**              | _El accidente está en la autovía V-30, kilómetro 12, dirección Valencia._ |
| **What happened**      | _Hay un accidente entre dos coches._                                      |
| **How many injured**   | _Hay dos heridos. Uno está inconsciente._                                 |
| **Hazards present**    | _Hay un derrame de combustible._ (There's a fuel spill.)                  |
| **Your phone number**  | _Mi número es..._                                                         |

**Key rule:** Do NOT hang up until the operator tells you to. They may give you instructions.

### S — SOCORRER (Help)

**Help the casualties — but only within your abilities.**

| DO ✅                                               | DON'T ❌                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Stay calm and reassure the injured                  | Move the injured person (unless fire/explosion risk) |
| Check if they're conscious and breathing            | Remove a motorcyclist's helmet                       |
| If unconscious but breathing: recovery position     | Give food or water                                   |
| If bleeding heavily: apply firm pressure with cloth | Try to straighten broken limbs                       |
| Cover them with a blanket to prevent shock          | Leave them alone (stay until help arrives)           |
| Talk to them — keep them awake and calm             | Administer medication                                |

### 🚨 THE BIG DGT EXAM TRAP — When to Move a Casualty

**NEVER move an injured person UNLESS:**

1. There is an **immediate risk of fire or explosion**
2. They are **not breathing** and you need to perform CPR on a flat surface
3. They are in **immediate danger** from another hazard (e.g., vehicle about to fall)

If you move someone with a spinal injury, you could cause paralysis. The DGT exam ALWAYS asks this.

## 🔥 SPECIAL EMERGENCY SITUATIONS

### Vehicle Fire

1. Stop the vehicle and turn off the engine
2. Get all passengers out and away (at least 50 metres)
3. Call 112
4. If small fire and you have an extinguisher: aim at the base of the flames
5. NEVER open the bonnet fully if engine bay is on fire — oxygen fuels flames. Open slightly and spray extinguisher through the gap
6. If fire is large: GET AWAY and wait for bomberos (firefighters)

### Fuel Spill

1. Do NOT start the engine — sparks could ignite fuel
2. Do NOT smoke
3. Move passengers away
4. Call 112
5. If possible, contain the spill with earth or sand (never water)

### Electrical Wires Down

1. Stay IN the vehicle if wires are touching it
2. If you must exit: JUMP clear (both feet together, don't touch the car and ground simultaneously)
3. Shuffle away with small steps (don't stride — ground current can electrocute)
4. Call 112

## 📋 LEGAL OBLIGATIONS AFTER AN ACCIDENT

| Obligation               | Detail                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Stop**                 | You MUST stop if involved in an accident — hit-and-run is a criminal offence                                 |
| **Protect the scene**    | V-16, hazard lights, vest                                                                                    |
| **Help the injured**     | PAS protocol                                                                                                 |
| **Call 112**             | If there are injuries                                                                                        |
| **Exchange information** | Names, ID, insurance details, registration numbers                                                           |
| **Parte amistoso**       | If no injuries, fill out the European Accident Statement (parte amistoso de accidente) with the other driver |
| **Do NOT admit fault**   | Describe what happened factually — let the insurance companies decide                                        |

## 🏙️ Valencia Connection

- **112 works everywhere in Valencia** — operators speak English, Spanish, Valenciano
- **V-30 accidents:** Common during rush hour, especially at merging points — know the PAS protocol
- **Fallas (March):** Increased fire risk from firecrackers and mascletás — stay alert
- **Beach roads:** Accident risk increases in summer on the N-332 and roads to the Albufera
- **Parte amistoso:** Keep a blank European Accident Statement in your glove compartment — your autoescuela or insurance company can provide one

## ⚠️ US → Spain Differences

| US                                                | Spain                                                                                             | Key Difference                        |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 911 emergency number                              | **112** emergency number                                                                          | Different number!                     |
| Good Samaritan laws vary by state                 | Obligation to help — failure to help can be an offence                                            | Spain requires you to stop and assist |
| Emergency triangles behind car                    | **V-16 beacon on roof (2026)**                                                                    | No walking on the road                |
| Move vehicles out of traffic after minor accident | **Do NOT move vehicles** until police arrive (if injuries). Move for minor fender-benders if safe | Different default                     |

## 📌 Summary — What You Need to Remember

1. **PAS = Proteger, Avisar, Socorrer.** Always in this order.
2. **Protect first:** V-16 beacon + hazard lights + reflective vest BEFORE exiting.
3. **Call 112.** Give location, what happened, how many injured, hazards present. Don't hang up.
4. **NEVER move an injured person** unless fire, explosion, or they need CPR.
5. **NEVER remove a motorcyclist's helmet.**
6. **If unconscious but breathing:** recovery position.
7. **If bleeding heavily:** press firmly with a cloth.
8. **You are legally obligated to stop and help** — failure to do so is an offence.
9. **Parte amistoso** for non-injury accidents — fill it out with the other driver.
10. **Key verbs:** _proteger_ (to protect), _avisar_ (to alert), _socorrer_ (to help), _mover_ (to move).
