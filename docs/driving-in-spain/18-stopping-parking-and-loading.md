## ⭐ Difficulty: ⭐⭐ (Core Exam Knowledge)

## 🎯 What This Topic Covers

The DGT makes sharp legal distinctions between **detención** (involuntary stop), **parada** (brief voluntary stop), and **estacionamiento** (parking). Getting these definitions right is worth easy exam marks. This topic also covers where you can and cannot park, loading zones, and disabled parking.

## 🔑 THE BIG THREE — Detención vs Parada vs Estacionamiento

| Term                | Spanish         | Definition                                                                                              | Key Test                       |
| ------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Detención**       | Detención       | Vehicle stops involuntarily — traffic, emergency, red light, police                                     | Not the driver's choice        |
| **Parada**          | Parada          | Vehicle stops voluntarily for **< 2 minutes** AND the driver **stays with the vehicle**                 | BOTH conditions must be met    |
| **Estacionamiento** | Estacionamiento | Vehicle stops voluntarily for **≥ 2 minutes** OR the driver **leaves the vehicle** (regardless of time) | EITHER condition triggers this |

### 🚨 DGT Exam Trap — The Classic Question

**"You stop for 1 minute but get out of the car. Is this a parada or estacionamiento?"**

**Answer: ESTACIONAMIENTO** — because the driver left the vehicle. Time doesn't matter if the driver leaves.

**"You stay in the car for 3 minutes. Parada or estacionamiento?"**

**Answer: ESTACIONAMIENTO** — because it's more than 2 minutes. Staying in the car doesn't matter if it's over 2 minutes.

## 🚫 WHERE IS PARKING (ESTACIONAMIENTO) PROHIBITED?

| Location                                         | Why                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| 🚫 On bends and near hilltops without visibility | Obstruction + danger                          |
| 🚫 On pedestrian crossings and within 5m         | Blocks visibility for pedestrians and drivers |
| 🚫 On cycle lanes or paths                       | Blocks cyclists                               |
| 🚫 At bus stops (yellow zigzag markings)         | Blocks public transport                       |
| 🚫 At intersections and within 5m                | Blocks visibility                             |
| 🚫 Blocking driveways (vados)                    | Blocks access — signed with "vado permanente" |
| 🚫 On the carriageway when parking spaces exist  | Use proper spaces                             |
| 🚫 Double parking (doble fila)                   | Blocks traffic and other parked vehicles      |
| 🚫 On pavements/sidewalks (aceras)               | Pedestrian space                              |
| 🚫 Where R-308 sign is displayed                 | No parking sign active                        |

## 🚫 WHERE IS STOPPING (PARADA) ALSO PROHIBITED?

Everything above PLUS:

| Location                         | Why                                         |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 🚫 Where R-307 sign is displayed | No stopping AND no parking                  |
| 🚫 On motorway carriageways      | Stop only on the hard shoulder in emergency |
| 🚫 Blocking traffic flow         | Even briefly                                |

**💡 Reminder:** R-307 (single red bar) = no stopping at all. R-308 (X) = no parking but brief parada is OK.

## 🅿️ TYPES OF PARKING ZONES

### Urban Parking in Valencia (and most Spanish cities)

| Zone               | Colour                         | Who Can Park                                         | Cost                     | Time Limit                            |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| **Zona azul**      | 🔵 Blue lines                  | Anyone                                               | €0.50–2.00/hour          | Usually 1–2 hours max                 |
| **Zona verde**     | 🟢 Green lines                 | Residents (cheaper) + non-residents (more expensive) | Varies                   | Residents: all day. Others: 1–2 hours |
| **Zona naranja**   | 🟠 Orange lines                | Special — varies by city                             | Varies                   | Varies                                |
| **Free on-street** | ⬜ No coloured lines           | Anyone                                               | Free                     | No limit (unless signed)              |
| **Loading zone**   | 🟡 Yellow marking + sign       | Commercial vehicles for loading/unloading only       | Free during posted hours | Usually 30 min max                    |
| **Disabled space** | ♿ Blue with wheelchair symbol | Disabled badge holders ONLY                          | Usually free             | Usually no limit                      |

### Parking on Hills

| Slope           | How to Park                                                                             |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Uphill** ⬆️   | Turn front wheels AWAY from the kerb (left). If car rolls, wheels hit kerb and stop it. |
| **Downhill** ⬇️ | Turn front wheels TOWARD the kerb (right). Same logic — wheels catch the kerb.          |
| **Both**        | Engage handbrake. Leave in first gear (uphill) or reverse (downhill) for manual cars.   |

### 🚨 DGT Exam Trap — Hill Parking

The DGT loves asking which way to turn the wheels on a hill. The trick: **always angle the wheels so the car would roll INTO the kerb, not away from it.** Think: "If my brakes failed, would the kerb stop me?"

## 🚛 LOADING & UNLOADING — Carga y Descarga

Loading zones have yellow markings and specific time restrictions (usually posted on a sign). Rules:

| Rule              | Detail                                                                 |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Who can use**   | Commercial vehicles actively loading/unloading                         |
| **Time limit**    | Usually 30 minutes maximum                                             |
| **Hours**         | Typically 8:00–20:00 (check local sign)                                |
| **Outside hours** | Anyone may park (if not otherwise restricted)                          |
| **Private cars**  | May use ONLY for picking up/dropping off heavy items, and only briefly |

---

## ♿ DISABLED PARKING — Aparcamiento para Personas con Discapacidad

| Rule                | Detail                                                                       |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Who can use**     | Only vehicles displaying a valid disabled badge (tarjeta de estacionamiento) |
| **Badge display**   | Must be clearly visible on the dashboard                                     |
| **Fine for misuse** | €200 (serious infraction)                                                    |
| **Validity**        | The badge belongs to the PERSON, not the car — it can be used in any vehicle |

## 🗣️ Conjugation Spotlight

### APARCAR — to park

A regular -ar verb — but with **c→qu** in the preterite yo form.

|             | Presente  | Pretérito   | Imperfecto  |
| ----------- | --------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| yo          | aparco    | aparqué     | aparcaba    |
| tú          | aparcas   | aparcaste   | aparcabas   |
| él/ella     | aparca    | aparcó      | aparcaba    |
| nosotros    | aparcamos | aparcamos   | aparcábamos |
| vosotros    | aparcáis  | aparcasteis | aparcabais  |
| ellos/ellas | aparcan   | aparcaron   | aparcaban   |

**Key sentences:**

- _Aparqué en la zona azul y pagué dos euros._ — I parked in the blue zone and paid two euros.
- _No se puede aparcar en doble fila._ — You cannot double-park.
- _¿Dónde puedo aparcar cerca del Aqua?_ — Where can I park near the Aqua?

### ESTACIONAR — to park (formal/legal term)

Regular -ar verb. Used in legal/sign language. In everyday speech, _aparcar_ is more common.

**Key sentences:**

- _Estacionamiento prohibido en este lado de la calle._ — Parking prohibited on this side of the street.
- _Está prohibido estacionar en doble fila._ — It is prohibited to double-park.

## 🏙️ Valencia Connection

- **Zona azul:** Throughout old town, Colón, Ruzafa, and near the Ciudad de las Artes — bring coins or use the app (apparkB or similar)
- **Zona verde:** Residential streets in Camins al Grau and Penya-Roja — get your resident parking permit at the Ayuntamiento
- **Loading zones:** Along Av. del Puerto and commercial streets — watch the posted hours carefully
- **Disabled spaces:** Throughout the city — fines actively enforced
- **Free parking:** Harder to find in the centre, but some streets in outer Penya-Roja and near the beach have unlined spaces
- **Aqua Mall:** Underground parking garage — 3 hours free with purchase
- **The grúa is REAL:** Valencia's tow trucks are active. If you park illegally near bus stops or on vados, your car WILL be towed. Recovery costs €150+
- **Vados:** Common in Penya-Roja — marked with "vado permanente" signs on garage entrances

## ⚠️ US → Spain Differences

| US                                                      | Spain                                                                                             | Key Difference                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| "No parking" = no parking (stopping may be OK)          | R-308 = no parking (parada OK). R-307 = no stopping at all                                        | Spain has TWO signs — learn the difference |
| Parking meters common                                   | Zona azul / ORA systems + apps                                                                    | Similar concept, different system          |
| No timed resident zones in most US cities               | Zona verde = resident priority parking                                                            | Get your resident permit!                  |
| Double parking: varies by enforcement                   | Double parking: actively fined + towed                                                            | Valencia enforces this                     |
| Fire hydrant = no parking (15 feet)                     | No equivalent US-style hydrant rule — but don't block fire access                                 | Check for signs instead                    |
| Hill parking: varies by city                            | Hill parking: DGT specifies wheel direction                                                       | Tested on the exam                         |
| Disabled space: blue with wheelchair                    | Disabled space: same concept, badge on dashboard                                                  | Similar system                             |
| "Parada" vs "estacionamiento" distinction doesn't exist | Two legally distinct concepts: < 2 min + driver stays = parada. Everything else = estacionamiento | Critical for the exam                      |

## 📌 Summary — What You Need to Remember

1. **Parada = < 2 minutes AND driver stays.** Estacionamiento = ≥ 2 minutes OR driver leaves. DGT tests this constantly!
2. **R-307 = no stopping at all.** R-308 = no parking (but brief parada OK).
3. **Never park** on pedestrian crossings, within 5m of intersections, at bus stops, on cycle lanes, or blocking driveways (vados).
4. **Zona azul = pay parking.** Zona verde = resident priority. Loading zone = commercial vehicles during posted hours.
5. **Hill parking:** Turn wheels so the car would roll INTO the kerb if brakes fail.
6. **Disabled spaces:** Only with a valid badge visible on the dashboard. €200 fine for misuse.
7. **Double parking is actively enforced** in Valencia — the grúa will tow you.
8. **Get a resident parking permit** for Penya-Roja/Camins al Grau — zona verde is much cheaper with one.
9. **Key verbs:** _aparcar_ (to park, everyday), _estacionar_ (to park, legal/formal), _cargar/descargar_ (to load/unload).
10. **If in doubt: don't park there.** The fine + tow cost far outweighs the convenience.
