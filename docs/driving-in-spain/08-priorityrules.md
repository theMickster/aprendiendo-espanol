## ⭐ Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐ (Core Exam Knowledge)

## 🎯 What This Topic Covers

Priority rules determine **who goes first** at intersections, roundabouts, narrow roads, and when encountering emergency vehicles or pedestrians. This is one of the trickiest DGT exam topics because Spain's rules differ significantly from both US and UK systems. A failure to respect priority (_no respetar la prioridad_) is also the **#2 reason people fail the practical exam**.

## 🔑 THE MASTER RULE: Right Has Priority — Prioridad a la Derecha

### The Default Rule

At any **unsignposted intersection** (without signs, lights, or officer), the vehicle approaching from the **RIGHT** has priority over you.

**This is the single most important priority rule in Spain.**

It applies ONLY when:

- There are no traffic signs
- There are no traffic lights
- There is no police officer directing traffic
- You are not on a priority road

**💡 Mick's Memory Trick:** "When in doubt, look RIGHT and yield."

## 📋 THE SEVEN PRIORITY SCENARIOS

### Scenario 1: Signposted Intersections

**When signs or lights exist → THEY decide priority, not the right-hand rule.**

| You See…               | You Must…                                         |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| 🛑 STOP sign (R-2)     | Stop completely, then yield to ALL traffic        |
| 🔺 Yield sign (R-1)    | Slow down, yield to traffic (only stop if needed) |
| 🔶 Priority road (P-1) | You go first — others yield to you                |
| 🚦 Traffic light       | Follow the light (green = go, red = stop)         |
| 👮 Police officer      | Follow the officer's signals above all else       |

**Key principle:** The **sign hierarchy** (from page 06a) applies here. Officer > temporary signs > lights > vertical signs > markings.

---

### Scenario 2: Unsignposted Intersections — Prioridad a la Derecha

At an intersection with **no signs, no lights, no officer**:

**→ Yield to the vehicle coming from your RIGHT.**

**Exceptions to the right-hand rule:**

| Exception                                       | Who Has Priority                                   | Why                              |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| **Paved road vs unpaved road**                  | Vehicle on the **paved** road                      | Paved roads carry more traffic   |
| **Rail vehicles (trams)**                       | The **tram/train** ALWAYS                          | Rail vehicles cannot stop easily |
| **Vehicles already in a roundabout**            | Vehicles **inside** the roundabout                 | Special roundabout rule          |
| **Motorway/autovía traffic vs merging traffic** | Vehicles **already on** the motorway               | Merging vehicles must yield      |
| **Cyclists in a group**                         | The **entire group** once the first cyclist enters | They count as ONE vehicle        |

### 🚨 DGT Exam Trap — The Paved Road Exception

If you're on a paved road and a car approaches from your right on an **unpaved/dirt road**, YOU have priority — the right-hand rule does NOT apply. The DGT loves testing this!

---

### Scenario 3: Roundabouts — Glorietas

**THE ROUNDABOUT RULE: Vehicles INSIDE the roundabout have priority over vehicles trying to enter.**

This overrides the normal right-hand rule. Even though entering traffic comes from the right of those inside, the vehicles circulating within the roundabout go first.

**How to use a roundabout in Spain:**

| Step | Action                                                          | Indicator                                      |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | **Approach** — reduce speed, check for traffic inside           | None yet                                       |
| 2    | **Yield** — give way to all vehicles already in the roundabout  | None                                           |
| 3    | **Enter** from the right lane (when safe)                       | Left indicator (entering left into the circle) |
| 4    | **Circulate** — stay in your lane, anticlockwise                | Left indicator while inside                    |
| 5    | **Prepare to exit** — move to the outer (right) lane in advance | Right indicator                                |
| 6    | **Exit** from the outer lane only                               | Right indicator                                |

**Critical rules:**

- **ALWAYS enter from the right lane** (unless road markings direct otherwise)
- **ALWAYS exit from the outer (right) lane** — never cut across from an inner lane
- **If you miss your exit** — go around again! Never stop or reverse
- **Use indicators:** Left indicator while circulating, right indicator to exit

### 🚨 DGT Exam Traps — Roundabouts

1. **Can you exit from an inner lane?** NO. As a general rule, you must be in the outer lane to exit.
2. **Who signals what?** Left indicator entering/circulating, right indicator exiting.
3. **Large roundabouts may have traffic lights INSIDE** — in that case, obey the lights, even though you're already inside.
4. **Cyclists in a group:** If the first cyclist has entered the roundabout, the entire group has priority to follow.

---

### Scenario 4: Narrow Roads & Mountain Passes — Estrechamientos y Pendientes

When the road narrows and two vehicles cannot pass simultaneously:

**General rule:** The vehicle that **arrived first** has priority.

**On a slope (pendiente):**

| Who Has Priority        | Who Must Yield        | Why                                               |
| ----------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vehicle going UP** ⬆️ | Vehicle going DOWN ⬇️ | Starting from a stop on an incline is much harder |

**Exception:** If the vehicle going DOWN has an **apartadero** (lay-by) closer, they should pull over to let the other pass.

**Between vehicle types:**

| Priority Order (highest → lowest)      |
| -------------------------------------- |
| 1. Buses and coaches (autobuses)       |
| 2. Lorries / heavy vehicles (camiones) |
| 3. Vans (furgonetas)                   |
| 4. Cars (turismos)                     |
| 5. Motorcycles (motocicletas)          |

**💡 Memory trick:** The BIGGER and HARDER-to-manoeuvre vehicle has priority on narrow roads.

---

### Scenario 5: Emergency Vehicles — Vehículos Prioritarios

**Emergency vehicles with sirens AND lights activated ALWAYS have priority.** You must:

1. **Move to the right** as far as safely possible
2. **Stop if necessary** to let them pass
3. **Do NOT follow** behind an emergency vehicle taking advantage of the space it clears
4. On motorways with traffic jams: **create an emergency corridor** — left lane moves left, right lane moves right (2026 rule being formalised)

**Types of emergency vehicles (vehículos prioritarios):**

- 🚑 Ambulances (ambulancias)
- 🚒 Fire engines (bomberos)
- 🚓 Police vehicles (policía)
- 🚨 Civil Guard (Guardia Civil)

**Key point:** The emergency vehicle must have its **lights AND siren** active. A police car with only its lights on (no siren) does NOT automatically have priority — but you should still be cautious.

---

### Scenario 6: Pedestrians — Peatones

**Pedestrians have priority in these situations:**

| Situation                                                         | Pedestrian Priority?                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| At a signposted pedestrian crossing (paso de peatones señalizado) | ✅ YES — ALWAYS                         |
| Pedestrian is crossing/about to enter a crossing                  | ✅ YES                                  |
| Vehicle is turning into a street where pedestrians are crossing   | ✅ YES — even without a marked crossing |
| Vehicle crosses an arcén (hard shoulder) where pedestrians walk   | ✅ YES                                  |
| In a zona peatonal (pedestrian zone)                              | ✅ YES — absolute priority              |
| In a zona residencial (S-220)                                     | ✅ YES — absolute priority              |
| Pedestrian crossing a road mid-block with no crossing             | ❌ NO — but still drive with caution    |

### 🚨 DGT Exam Trap — Turning Vehicles & Pedestrians

When YOU are turning into another street, **pedestrians crossing that street ALWAYS have priority** — even if there is no marked crossing. This catches many people on the exam.

---

### Scenario 7: Merging & Lane Changes — Incorporación y Cambio de Carril

**When merging onto a road (e.g., joining a motorway via an acceleration lane):**

- You MUST yield to vehicles already on the road
- The vehicles already on the road have NO obligation to let you in (but courtesy helps!)
- Use the acceleration lane to match speed before merging

**When changing lanes:**

- The vehicle in the lane you're moving INTO has priority
- You must signal, check mirrors and blind spot, and only move when safe

**When two vehicles want to change into the same lane simultaneously:**

- The vehicle that is CLOSER to that lane (has to move less) has priority

## 🗣️ Conjugation Spotlight

### CEDER — to yield / to give way

A regular -er verb — but essential for priority topics.

|             | Presente | Pretérito | Imperfecto |
| ----------- | -------- | --------- | ---------- |
| yo          | cedo     | cedí      | cedía      |
| tú          | cedes    | cediste   | cedías     |
| él/ella     | cede     | cedió     | cedía      |
| nosotros    | cedemos  | cedimos   | cedíamos   |
| vosotros    | cedéis   | cedisteis | cedíais    |
| ellos/ellas | ceden    | cedieron  | cedían     |

**Key sentences:**

- _Debes ceder el paso a la derecha._ — You must yield to the right.
- _No cedí el paso y me multaron._ — I didn't yield and I got fined.
- _En la glorieta, ceda el paso a los que ya circulan dentro._ — At the roundabout, yield to those already inside.

### INCORPORARSE — to merge / to join (reflexive)

|             | Presente         | Pretérito         | Imperfecto         |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ----------------- | ------------------ |
| yo          | me incorporo     | me incorporé      | me incorporaba     |
| tú          | te incorporas    | te incorporaste   | te incorporabas    |
| él/ella     | se incorpora     | se incorporó      | se incorporaba     |
| nosotros    | nos incorporamos | nos incorporamos  | nos incorporábamos |
| vosotros    | os incorporáis   | os incorporasteis | os incorporabais   |
| ellos/ellas | se incorporan    | se incorporaron   | se incorporaban    |

**Key sentences:**

- _Me incorporo a la autovía por el carril de aceleración._ — I merge onto the dual carriageway via the acceleration lane.
- _Al incorporarse, ceda el paso._ — When merging, yield.

## 🏙️ Valencia Connection

- **Unsignposted intersections:** Rare in Valencia city but common in smaller towns around the Albufera and Huerta
- **Roundabouts everywhere:** The large glorietas where Turia park meets Av. de las Balears, Av. de Francia, and the V-30 exits
- **Pedestrian priority:** All crossings around Colón, old town (Ciutat Vella), and near Ciudad de las Artes
- **Zona residencial (S-220):** Parts of the old town near Plaza de la Reina — 20 km/h, pedestrians rule
- **Emergency corridor:** On the V-30 during rush hour — traffic jams are common
- **Merging:** The V-30/A-7 interchange near Quatre Carreres has multiple acceleration lanes
- **Cyclists in groups:** Weekend cycling groups on roads heading to the Albufera — they have priority once the first enters

## ⚠️ US → Spain Differences for Priority Rules

| US                                                | Spain                                                                            | Key Difference                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 4-way stop is common                              | 4-way stop almost NEVER exists                                                   | In Spain, one road always has priority or the right-hand rule applies   |
| "First to arrive" at a stop sign goes first       | "First to arrive" only applies at narrow passages, NOT at intersections          | At intersections: signs or right-hand rule decide                       |
| Right on red allowed (usually)                    | Right on red **NEVER** allowed                                                   | Even if no traffic — never                                              |
| Pedestrians have priority at pedestrian crossings | Pedestrians have priority at crossings AND when you're turning into their street | Spain's rule is BROADER                                                 |
| Roundabouts: yield to traffic inside              | Roundabouts: yield to traffic inside                                             | Same rule!                                                              |
| Emergency vehicles: pull to the right             | Emergency vehicles: pull to the right                                            | Same rule!                                                              |
| Merging: "zipper merge" common                    | Merging: you must yield — no obligation for road traffic to let you in           | Less courtesy, more rule-based                                          |
| School buses: all traffic MUST stop               | No school bus stop rule                                                          | Spain does NOT have this — use the P-21 (children) warning sign instead |
| Funeral processions have informal priority        | No funeral procession rules                                                      | Not a concept in Spain                                                  |

## 📌 Summary — What You Need to Remember

1. **Default rule: YIELD TO THE RIGHT** at unsignposted intersections.
2. **Exceptions to the right rule:** paved vs unpaved, trams, roundabouts, motorway traffic, cyclists in groups.
3. **Roundabouts: vehicles INSIDE have priority** over those entering. Enter from the right lane, exit from the outer lane.
4. **On slopes: vehicle going UP has priority.** The one going down yields.
5. **Emergency vehicles with lights + siren: ALWAYS yield.** Move right, stop if necessary.
6. **Pedestrians have priority at crossings** AND when you're turning into a street they're crossing.
7. **When merging: YOU yield** to traffic already on the road. They don't have to let you in.
8. **Right on red: NEVER allowed.** No exceptions.
9. **Cyclists in a group:** Once the first enters an intersection or roundabout, the whole group has priority.
10. **Key verbs:** _ceder el paso_ (to yield), _incorporarse_ (to merge), _respetar la prioridad_ (to respect priority).
