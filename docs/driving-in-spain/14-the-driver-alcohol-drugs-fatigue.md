# 14 — The Driver: Alcohol, Drugs & Fatigue

## ⭐ Difficulty: ⭐⭐ (Core Exam Knowledge)

## 🎯 What This Topic Covers

This topic covers the **human factor** — how alcohol, drugs, medication, fatigue, and distractions affect your ability to drive. The DGT considers these the **"4 Fatales"** (4 Deadly Factors): alcohol, speed, distractions, and not wearing a seatbelt. This is one of the easier DGT topics to study because the rules are straightforward numbers — but the exam still catches people on the details.

## 🍺 ALCOHOL — The Rules

### Current Legal Blood Alcohol Limits

| Driver Type                    | Blood (g/l) | Breath (mg/l) | Who                                         |
| ------------------------------ | ----------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **General drivers**            | **0.5 g/l** | **0.25 mg/l** | All standard licence holders                |
| **Professional drivers**       | **0.3 g/l** | **0.15 mg/l** | Bus, taxi, lorry, VTC drivers               |
| **Novice drivers** (< 2 years) | **0.3 g/l** | **0.15 mg/l** | Anyone with a licence less than 2 years old |
| **Under 18** (moped/bike)      | **0.0 g/l** | **0.0 mg/l**  | Zero tolerance                              |

**⚠️ 2026 UPDATE — Proposed Lower Limit:**
A new law has been proposed (and passed initial parliamentary stages) to reduce the general limit to **0.2 g/l** (0.10 mg/l breath) for ALL drivers. This would make Spain one of the strictest in Europe, alongside Sweden and Norway. Final approval is pending — check current status before your exam. The DGT's recommendation: **the safest limit is 0.0 g/l — don't drink and drive.**

### What Does This Mean in Practice?

The DGT provides these APPROXIMATE guidelines (varies by body weight, food, health):

**For men (70–90 kg):**

| Drink                      | Safe?                 |
| -------------------------- | --------------------- |
| 🍺 1 small beer (33cl, 5%) | Probably OK           |
| 🍺 2 small beers           | Likely OVER the limit |
| 🍷 1 glass wine (100ml)    | Probably OK           |
| 🍷 2 glasses wine          | At or OVER the limit  |
| 🥃 1 small spirit (45ml)   | Probably OK           |
| 🥃 2 spirits               | OVER the limit        |

**For women (50–70 kg):**

| Drink             | Safe?                |
| ----------------- | -------------------- |
| 🍺 1 small beer   | Probably OK          |
| 🍺 2 small beers  | OVER the limit       |
| 🍷 1 glass wine   | At the limit         |
| 🥃 1 small spirit | At or near the limit |

**💡 Key DGT Message:** These are estimates. The ONLY truly safe limit is **0.0 g/l**. The DGT's campaign is clear: _"Si bebes, no conduzcas"_ — If you drink, don't drive.

### How Alcohol Affects Driving

| BAC Level | Risk Increase             | Effects                                      |
| --------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 0.3 g/l   | 1.5x more likely to crash | Reduced attention, slightly slower reactions |
| 0.5 g/l   | 2x more likely to crash   | Impaired judgment, reduced field of vision   |
| 0.8 g/l   | 5x more likely to crash   | Serious impairment, tunnel vision            |
| 1.2 g/l   | 10x+ more likely to crash | Criminal offence level — possible prison     |

### Alcohol Elimination

- The body eliminates alcohol at approximately **0.10–0.15 g/l per hour**
- You CANNOT speed this up — coffee, water, exercise, fresh air do NOT work
- A big night out could mean you're still over the limit the **next morning**
- The DGT reports many "morning after" positive tests, especially on weekends

### Penalties

| Offence                     | Fine                                   | Points Lost | Criminal?                  |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------- |
| BAC 0.25–0.50 mg/l (breath) | €500                                   | 4 points    | No (administrative)        |
| BAC 0.50–0.60 mg/l (breath) | €1,000                                 | 6 points    | No (administrative)        |
| BAC > 0.60 mg/l (breath)    | Prison 3–6 months or community service | 6 points    | **YES — criminal offence** |
| **Refusing breathalyser**   | Prison 6–12 months                     | 6 points    | **YES — criminal offence** |

### 🚨 DGT Exam Trap — Refusing the Test

**Refusing to take a breathalyser test is a CRIMINAL OFFENCE** — punishable by 6–12 months in prison. This is harsher than actually being slightly over the limit! The DGT tests this frequently.

## 💊 DRUGS & MEDICATION

### Illegal Drugs

Driving under the influence of ANY illegal drug is prohibited. There is **no minimum threshold** — any positive result for drugs means automatic penalties.

**Most commonly detected drugs in fatal accidents in Spain:**

1. Cannabis (most common)
2. Cocaine
3. Amphetamines

**Penalties:** Same framework as alcohol — administrative fines, point deductions, and criminal charges for serious impairment.

### Medication

Many common medications can affect driving ability. Look for this warning symbol on medication packaging:

**🚗 ⚠️ Triangle with a car** = This medication may affect your ability to drive

**Types of medication that commonly affect driving:**

- Antihistamines (allergy medication) → drowsiness
- Antidepressants → delayed reactions
- Painkillers (especially opioid-based) → drowsiness, impaired judgment
- Sleeping pills → "morning after" effects
- Some cold/flu medications → drowsiness

**Key DGT rule:** If your medication carries the driving warning symbol, **consult your doctor** before driving. You're not automatically banned from driving, but you must ensure you're safe.

## 😴 FATIGUE & DROWSINESS

### The DGT's Recommended Driving/Rest Pattern

| Rule                           | Detail                                                       |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Maximum continuous driving** | 2 hours                                                      |
| **Break after 2 hours**        | At least 15–20 minutes                                       |
| **Best rest activities**       | Walk, stretch, fresh air, light snack                        |
| **Avoid driving**              | Between 2:00–5:00 AM (peak drowsiness) and after heavy meals |

### Warning Signs of Fatigue

| Sign                        | Spanish                            | What It Means                |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 😑 Heavy eyelids            | Párpados pesados                   | You're getting drowsy        |
| 🥱 Frequent yawning         | Bostezos frecuentes                | Fatigue is setting in        |
| 😵 Difficulty concentrating | Dificultad para concentrarse       | Attention is dropping        |
| 🚗 Drifting between lanes   | Desviarse entre carriles           | You may be microsleeping     |
| 🤔 Can't remember last km   | No recordar los últimos kilómetros | Dangerous — stop immediately |

**What to do:** Stop at the nearest safe location, rest for 15–20 minutes, and do NOT rely on coffee, open windows, or loud music — they only work temporarily.

### 🚨 DGT Exam Trap — "Quick Fixes" Don't Work

The DGT exam may ask: "What should you do if you feel drowsy while driving?" The WRONG answers include drinking coffee, turning up the radio, or opening the window. The CORRECT answer is always: **STOP and REST.**

## 📱 DISTRACTIONS

### Mobile Phone Rules

| Rule               | Detail                                                   |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Handheld phone** | 🚫 PROHIBITED — even at red lights or in traffic jams    |
| **Hands-free**     | ✅ Permitted, but not recommended                        |
| **Fine**           | €200 + 3 points                                          |
| **Navigation**     | Must be mounted — you cannot hold the phone to check GPS |

### Other Distractions

- Eating while driving — not illegal but strongly discouraged
- Headphones — prohibited (both ears). One earbud may be tolerated for calls but is not recommended
- Smoking — not illegal but dropping a cigarette counts as a distraction

## 🗣️ Conjugation Spotlight

### AFECTAR — to affect

A regular -ar verb — key for this topic.

|             | Presente  | Pretérito   | Imperfecto  |
| ----------- | --------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| yo          | afecto    | afecté      | afectaba    |
| tú          | afectas   | afectaste   | afectabas   |
| él/ella     | afecta    | afectó      | afectaba    |
| nosotros    | afectamos | afectamos   | afectábamos |
| vosotros    | afectáis  | afectasteis | afectabais  |
| ellos/ellas | afectan   | afectaron   | afectaban   |

**Key sentences:**

- _El alcohol afecta al tiempo de reacción._ — Alcohol affects reaction time.
- _Este medicamento puede afectar a la conducción._ — This medication may affect driving.

### NEGARSE — to refuse (reflexive, stem-changing e → ie)

|             | Presente    | Pretérito    | Imperfecto    |
| ----------- | ----------- | ------------ | ------------- |
| yo          | me niego    | me negué     | me negaba     |
| tú          | te niegas   | te negaste   | te negabas    |
| él/ella     | se niega    | se negó      | se negaba     |
| nosotros    | nos negamos | nos negamos  | nos negábamos |
| vosotros    | os negáis   | os negasteis | os negabais   |
| ellos/ellas | se niegan   | se negaron   | se negaban    |

**Note:** _Negarse_ has **e→ie** stem change in present AND **g→gu** in preterite yo: _me negué_.

**Key sentences:**

- _Negarse a soplar en el alcoholímetro es un delito._ — Refusing to blow into the breathalyser is a criminal offence.
- _No me niego a hacer la prueba._ — I don't refuse to take the test.

## 🏙️ Valencia Connection

- **Police checkpoints:** Common on Friday/Saturday nights near the old town, Ruzafa, and beach areas
- **Morning-after testing:** The Guardia Civil conducts early morning checks on the V-30 and roads heading out of the city on Sundays
- **Fallas (March):** Heavy police presence and alcohol testing during the festival — drive extra carefully
- **Medication:** Spanish pharmacies clearly label medications with the driving warning triangle — ask your _farmacéutico_
- **Long drives:** Valencia → Madrid is about 3.5 hours — plan at least one rest stop
- **Mobile phones:** Police actively enforce the no-phone rule in Valencia, especially at traffic lights

## ⚠️ US → Spain Differences

| US                                         | Spain                                                            | Key Difference                                |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| BAC limit typically 0.08% (0.8 g/l)        | BAC limit **0.5 g/l** (general) or **0.3 g/l** (novice)          | Spain is MUCH stricter                        |
| Refusing breathalyser varies by state      | Refusing = **criminal offence** (6–12 months prison)             | Extremely harsh in Spain                      |
| DUI = mainly criminal                      | Low BAC = administrative fine. High BAC (>0.6 breath) = criminal | Two-tier system                               |
| No driving warning on medications          | Clear 🚗⚠️ triangle on medication packaging                      | Very helpful visual system                    |
| No mandatory rest breaks (general drivers) | DGT recommends stop every **2 hours**                            | Not legally enforced but strongly recommended |
| Handheld phone: varies by state            | Handheld phone: **always prohibited**, even at red lights        | Spain is absolute about this                  |
| Open container laws vary                   | No specific "open container" law, but BAC limits apply           | The result is the same — don't drink          |
| "Morning after" testing less common        | "Morning after" testing is **very common**                       | A big surprise for many expats                |

## 📌 Summary — What You Need to Remember

1. **General BAC limit: 0.5 g/l blood (0.25 mg/l breath).** Novice/professional: 0.3 g/l (0.15 mg/l).
2. **Proposed 2026 change:** 0.2 g/l for ALL drivers — check current status.
3. **DGT's message: the only safe limit is 0.0 g/l.** "Si bebes, no conduzcas."
4. **Refusing a breathalyser = CRIMINAL OFFENCE** — worse penalties than being slightly over!
5. **The body eliminates ~0.10–0.15 g/l per hour.** Coffee and cold water do NOT help.
6. **Any illegal drug = prohibited while driving.** Zero tolerance.
7. **Check medication for the 🚗⚠️ driving warning triangle.** Consult your doctor.
8. **Stop every 2 hours.** If drowsy, the ONLY solution is to STOP and REST.
9. **Handheld phone: ALWAYS prohibited** — even at red lights.
10. **Key verbs:** _afectar_ (to affect), _negarse_ (to refuse), _soplar_ (to blow), _descansar_ (to rest).
